1:1-12|Faith Growing Under Pressure|Endurance while justice remains with God|Paul, Silvanus and Timothy give thanks for the growth of the church's faith and love during persecution. They speak of God's righteous judgement and of relief when Jesus is revealed. Paul prays that God will fulfil the believers' good purposes and work of faith so that Jesus is glorified in them.|Paul notices growth that suffering has not erased. The promise of just judgement supports his prayer that the believers' present work may continue.|What qualities does Paul see growing in the church while it faces persecution?
2:1-12|A Report That Shook the Church|Discernment when a claim sounds urgent|Paul asks the believers not to be shaken by claims, even a letter presented as coming from him, that the day of the Lord has arrived. He recalls what he taught about rebellion and the disclosure of the lawless one. He describes deceptive signs and the eventual defeat of the lawless one by the Lord Jesus.|An urgent claim can borrow the authority of a trusted messenger. Paul asks the church to remember what it received and to love the truth while weighing the claim.|What kinds of messages did Paul say might have unsettled the believers?
2:13-17|Stand Firm in What You Received|Encouragement for settled obedience|Paul gives thanks for the believers' calling through the gospel and speaks of sanctification by the Spirit and belief of the truth. He urges the church to hold to what it received by word or letter. He asks Jesus and the Father to comfort their hearts and establish their words and works.|Paul's response to confusion includes encouragement as well as correction. A settled heart is meant to show itself in dependable speech and work.|What two areas does Paul ask God to establish after encouraging them to stand firm?
3:1-18|Praying for the Word and Working Quietly|Perseverance with responsible care|Paul asks for prayer that the message will spread. He reminds the church of the example he set by working and addresses those who refuse to work and interfere in others' affairs. He urges them to work quietly. When correction is necessary, Paul tells the church to admonish the person as a brother. He closes with peace and grace.|Paul's instruction concerns refusal to work. Correction addresses the conduct while maintaining a relationship in which a person can hear and respond.|What distinction does Paul make between warning someone and treating that person as an enemy?
