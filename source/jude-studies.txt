1:1-16|The Faith Entrusted to the Saints|Jude calls believers to recognise the danger of grace used as permission for sin|Jude introduces himself as a servant of Jesus Christ and a brother of James. He writes to people who are called and kept, and asks for mercy, peace and love to be multiplied to them. He had been eager to write about their common salvation. An urgent situation now requires him to appeal to them to contend for the faith once delivered to the saints. Certain people have slipped into their company. They turn the grace of God into permission for immorality and deny their Master and Lord, Jesus Christ. Their presence makes the matter immediate. People who shared in the life of the congregation were using familiar words about grace while teaching and living in ways that undermined its allegiance to Christ.~Jude asks his readers to remember events they already know. The Lord saved people out of Egypt and later destroyed those who refused to believe. Angels who abandoned the position given to them are held in darkness for judgment. Sodom and Gomorrah and the towns around them are set forth as a warning concerning sexual immorality and the judgment of fire. Jude then describes the people troubling the church. They defile the flesh, reject authority and speak abusively about glorious beings. He contrasts their speech with Michael's conduct when the archangel disputed with the devil over Moses' body. Michael left rebuke to the Lord. The intruders readily abuse what they fail to understand, and they are ruined by the things they know only by instinct.~Jude brings together three more names from Scripture. The intruders have followed Cain's way, rushed into Balaam's error for reward and perished in Korah's rebellion. He then turns to the meals believers shared in love. These men take their place there without fear and feed themselves. Jude pictures them as clouds that promise water and pass empty, and as late autumn trees whose fruit never comes and whose roots have been pulled out. They are waves throwing up their own shame and wandering stars for whom darkness has been reserved. A congregation might be impressed by confident speech or suppose that attendance at its meals proved a person's faithfulness. Jude directs attention to the people being fed, the fruit being borne and the authority being honoured.~He recalls the prophecy of Enoch, the seventh from Adam, who spoke of the Lord coming with his holy ones to judge and to expose ungodly deeds and harsh words spoken against him. Jude then draws the warning back into the company of people his readers actually know. The intruders grumble and complain, follow their own desires, make boastful claims and flatter others for advantage. The details give the church ways to understand what is happening in its midst. They also explain why Jude interrupted his intended subject. The message of their common salvation was worth guarding because the conduct of the people who claimed to speak for it shaped the life of the whole congregation.|Grace calls us to serve Jesus Christ as Lord. The fruit of a teacher's conduct and the way that teacher treats others deserve attention alongside what is said.|Where might you be tempted to excuse a wrong course of action by appealing to God's grace?
1:17-25|Mercy for Those in Danger|A church under pressure is called to grow, pray and help people in danger|Jude now turns directly to his beloved readers and asks them to remember what the apostles of the Lord Jesus Christ had said. In the last time there would be mockers who followed their own ungodly desires. These are people who cause divisions, are governed by worldly desires and lack the Spirit. The apostles had prepared the church for such behaviour. Jude wants the congregation to recognise it without letting the mockers set the terms of its common life. He has already named how disorder can enter shared meals and relationships. His next instructions direct believers towards the habits by which they can stay faithful to Christ and care for people whose footing is less secure.~They are to build themselves up in their most holy faith and pray in the Holy Spirit. They are to keep themselves in the love of God as they look for the mercy of the Lord Jesus Christ that leads to eternal life. Prayer and patience belong together here. The congregation is to keep growing in the faith it received while looking ahead to the mercy on which its own future depends. Jude addresses people together. What they believe can shape how they pray for one another, how they respond when divisions arise and how they treat someone who has been pulled towards error. Remembering their own dependence on the Lord's mercy prepares them for what Jude asks of them next.~His counsel recognises that people in danger need help suited to their situation. Some need compassion. Others need urgent rescue, pictured as pulling someone out of a fire. Mercy must also be exercised with fear where sin has left its stain. Jude uses the image of a garment polluted by the flesh to convey the seriousness of that danger. A person can reach out to someone who has been misled while taking care over the conduct that brought them into trouble. In a congregation that has heard hard words about false teachers, Jude still expects its members to go after people who might otherwise be left in danger. That effort asks for patience in one case and quick action in another.~The letter ends with praise to the one who is able to keep believers from falling and present them faultless before the presence of his glory with great joy. Glory, majesty, dominion and power belong to God the Saviour. The ending takes the readers back to the opening, where Jude addressed them as people who are kept. Between those assurances he has asked them to remember, pray, stay in God's love and take real responsibility for one another. Their hope as they carry out those duties is that God himself can bring them into his presence with joy. The people they seek to rescue need that same keeping power, which gives the congregation a reason to continue giving help even when the work is difficult.|Stay close to God through faith and prayer, and give people in danger the kind of help their situation requires. Trust God to uphold you while you do it.|Who needs your patient compassion or prompt help, and how can you give it while guarding your own conduct?
