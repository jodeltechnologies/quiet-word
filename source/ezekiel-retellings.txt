47:1-48:35|The River and the City With a New Name|Life flows from the place where the Lord dwells|Ezekiel saw water flowing eastward from beneath the temple's threshold, passing south of the altar. The guide led him outside and measured successive distances along the stream. At first the water reached his ankles, then his knees, then his waist. Farther on it had become a river too deep to cross on foot. Its growth drew attention to the life proceeding from the sanctuary. The vision was not presenting a technique by which people could obtain successive spiritual ranks. The guide was showing what flowed from God's dwelling and how abundant it became beyond the prophet's ability to walk through it.~Returning to the bank, Ezekiel saw many trees on both sides. The water went towards the eastern desert and the sea, bringing healing and an abundance of fish. Fishermen would spread nets along its shores. Marshes left for salt remained within the description, while trees beside the river bore fresh fruit continually. Their fruit provided food and their leaves healing because their water came from the sanctuary. The imagery portrays a restored world receiving life from God's presence. It invites hope for his renewing work without becoming a guarantee that every present illness will disappear immediately. The source matters throughout the scene. The river's power is received from the place where the Lord dwells.~The land's boundaries and distribution followed. Israel's tribes would receive their inheritance, and resident strangers who had established families among them were also to receive a share in the tribe where they lived. Restoration therefore included belonging for people who might otherwise have remained at the margins. The northern and southern tribal portions were arranged around the holy district, with provision for priests, Levites, city workers and the prince. The Lord's sanctuary remained central. The vision's ordered geography differs from the earlier historical distribution and should be allowed to speak in its own prophetic form. It shows an inheritance arranged beneath God's rule, where worship and the common life are related without making vulnerable residents invisible.~The city's gates bore the names of Israel's tribes, three on each side. The final declaration gave the city its defining name, The Lord is there. This closing word answers the book's long movement from the glory beside the Chebar, through the defiled temple and the departure of glory, to return and renewed life. Buildings, land and abundance reach their meaning in God's abiding presence. For Christian readers, the river and city also prepare for the final Biblical vision of life with God. Ezekiel's ending calls the exiles beyond despair and calls every reader beyond a merely outward idea of restoration. The greatest hope is to belong among a people whose life is sustained, ordered and made fruitful because the Lord is with them.|God's presence is the source and goal of lasting renewal. His promised restoration brings life, belonging and a faithful common life.|What would it mean for your home or community to be known above all as a place where God's presence shapes its life?
