1:1-2:9|The Offerings They Would Not Give a Governor|The Lord questions worship that gives him what people would be ashamed to offer elsewhere|The Lord opens his word to Israel through Malachi by declaring his love. The people ask how he has loved them. He points to Jacob and Esau, brothers whose descendants have known different histories. The Lord loved Jacob and laid Esau's hill country waste. Edom may declare that it will rebuild its ruins, but the Lord says he will tear down what it builds. Israel will see and acknowledge that the Lord is great beyond its own borders. The people who questioned his love are now asked to see their history in light of his choice and his authority outside the land. The Lord then puts a question of his own to the priests, whose dealings at his altar show how little honour they give him.~A son honours his father and a servant his master. The Lord asks where his honour and reverence are if he is their Father and Master. The priests ask how they have despised his name and polluted his altar. They have treated his table with contempt by bringing blind, lame and sick animals for sacrifice. Would their governor receive such a gift and be pleased with them? Would he show them favour? The Lord would have someone shut the doors so that they would cease lighting useless fires on his altar. His name will be great among the nations from sunrise to sunset, and a pure offering will be made to him. The priests who have access to his own altar complain of weariness and bring animals that are stolen or defective.~The Lord pronounces a curse on the person who has a sound male in his flock, vows it and then sacrifices a blemished animal. He is a great King and his name is feared among the nations. He warns the priests that if they refuse to listen and give glory to his name, he will curse their blessings. Their offspring are threatened, and the refuse from their festivals will be spread on their faces and they will be taken away with it. The warning is given so that they may know whose command this is and that the Lord's covenant with Levi may stand. The ceremonies that had become a weariness to the priests will expose what their worship has become.~The Lord describes that covenant as one of life and peace. Levi feared him and stood in awe of his name. True instruction was in his mouth. He walked with God in peace and uprightness and turned many away from wrongdoing. A priest ought to guard knowledge, and people should be able to seek instruction from his mouth, for he is the messenger of the Lord of hosts. The present priests have turned from the way and caused many to stumble by their instruction. They have corrupted the covenant and shown partiality in applying the law. The Lord has made them despised before the people. Their place should have helped others approach God with reverence, and their conduct has made the path harder for those who came to learn.|Honour for God shows in what we give him and in the care we take when others rely on our instruction. The Lord sees the difference between a promise and what is actually brought to the altar.|What part of your worship receives the effort you would be embarrassed to offer someone whose approval you value?
2:10-3:5|The Tears at the Altar and the Coming Refiner|Broken covenants bring grief, and the Lord promises to come and put things right|Malachi asks whether the people have one Father and whether one God created them. Why then do they deal treacherously with one another and profane the covenant of their fathers? Judah has been faithless and a detestable thing has been done in Israel and Jerusalem. Judah has profaned what the Lord loves by marrying the daughter of a foreign god. The Lord is asked to cut off the offender from Jacob's tents even if that person brings an offering. The step Malachi rebukes concerns allegiance and worship, and it is taken by people who may still come to the altar. Their presence there does not remove the wrong he has named.~There is another cause of grief. The altar is covered with tears and weeping because the Lord no longer regards the offering or receives it with favour. The people ask why. The Lord has been witness between a man and the wife of his youth, his companion and wife by covenant, towards whom he has dealt treacherously. The prophet tells his hearers to guard their spirit and be faithful, and connects that faithfulness with the godly offspring he seeks. The Lord speaks against putting away a wife and against covering one's garment with violence. The tears at the altar are brought into the same account as the promises people have broken at home. God has been present as a witness to what the person making the offering might prefer to leave unmentioned.~The people have wearied the Lord with their words. They ask how. They have claimed that everyone who does evil is good in the Lord's sight and that he delights in such people, or they have asked where the God of justice is. The Lord says he will send his messenger to prepare the way before him. The Lord whom they seek will suddenly come to his temple, and the messenger of the covenant whom they desire will come. Malachi asks who can endure the day of his coming and who can stand when he appears. The arrival they have demanded will also bring their own practices into judgment.~He will be like a refiner's fire and like the soap used by people who wash cloth. He will sit as a refiner of silver and purify the sons of Levi like gold and silver, so that they may bring an offering in righteousness. Then the offering of Judah and Jerusalem will please the Lord as in former days. He will draw near for judgment and be a swift witness against sorcerers, adulterers, false swearers and people who cheat labourers of their wages, oppress widows and orphans or turn aside the stranger. Their conduct shows that they do not fear him. The answer to the question about justice names homes, wages, testimony and worship, and it begins with the Lord at his own temple.|God witnesses the promises we make and the harm done when we break them. His coming brings both the cleansing needed for sincere worship and justice for those who have been wronged.|Which promise or responsibility needs your attention before you go on asking God to put everyone else's conduct right?
3:6-4:6|The Book of Remembrance and the Coming Day|The Lord calls his people to return, remembers those who fear him and promises a messenger|The Lord says that he does not change, and for this reason the children of Jacob have not been consumed. They have turned aside from his ordinances since the days of their fathers. He calls them to return to him and promises to return to them. They ask how they are to return. The Lord asks whether a person will rob God, and when they ask how they have robbed him he names tithes and offerings. The whole nation is under a curse. They are to bring the full tithe to the storehouse so that there is food in his house. He invites them to put him to the test in this matter. He will open heaven's windows, pour out blessing and rebuke the devourer so their crops and vines are spared. The nations will call their land blessed.~The Lord says their words against him have been harsh. They ask what they have said. They have declared it useless to serve God, asking what profit they gained from keeping his charge and walking mournfully before him. They call the proud happy and say that evildoers prosper and people who test God get away with it. Then those who fear the Lord speak with one another. He listens and hears, and a book of remembrance is written before him for those who fear him and think upon his name. He calls them his treasured possession on the day he acts and says he will spare them as a father spares a son who serves him. People will again see the difference between the righteous and the wicked, between the one who serves God and the one who does not.~The day is coming, burning like an oven. The arrogant and all who do wickedly will be stubble and the day will leave them neither root nor branch. For those who fear the Lord's name the sun of righteousness will rise with healing in its wings. They will go out leaping like calves released from the stall. The wicked will be like ashes under their feet on the day the Lord acts. Malachi has allowed the complaint about the prosperity of evildoers to be heard clearly. The people who fear God are asked to live by the future he has promised while the circumstances that prompted the complaint are still visible.~The Lord tells Israel to remember the law of Moses, his servant, given at Horeb for all Israel, with its statutes and judgments. Before the great and dreadful day of the Lord, he will send Elijah the prophet. He will turn the hearts of fathers to their children and the hearts of children to their fathers, lest the Lord strike the land with a curse. The final promise reaches into the household. A people waiting for God's day are told to remember the word they already have and to look for a work of turning that reaches across generations. The book ends with relationships that can be restored under the same call to return with which this reading began.|God hears those who speak faithfully of him when service appears unrewarded. Returning to him reaches what we withhold, what we say to one another and the relationships we have allowed to grow cold.|What part of returning to God could you begin this week through a concrete act of giving, faithful speech or reconciliation?
