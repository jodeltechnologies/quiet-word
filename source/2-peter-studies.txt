1:1-11|Make Every Effort to Grow|God's generous provision calls for a diligent response|The letter opened with Simon Peter identifying himself as a servant and apostle of Jesus Christ. He addressed those who had received a faith of equal standing through the righteousness of the God and Saviour Jesus Christ, and asked that grace and peace increase through the knowledge of God and of Jesus the Lord. The opening placed the readers beside the apostle in the worth of the faith received. Their confidence did not require the same office or personal history. It rested upon the righteousness and generosity of the one in whom they believed. Spiritual growth would therefore begin with receiving what God had given and coming to know him more fully.~God's divine power had supplied what was needed for life and godliness through the knowledge of the one who called them by his glory and goodness. His precious and very great promises enabled them to share in the divine nature, escaping the corruption in the world through sinful desire. This sharing concerns participation in the life and moral likeness God gives, rather than a claim that believers become independent gods. The passage joined promise, knowledge and transformed living. God's generosity was sufficient for a real departure from corruption. It did not leave his people merely admiring holiness from a distance while assuming that no change in their settled desires could be expected.~For this reason they were to make every effort to add virtue to faith, knowledge to virtue, self-control to knowledge, endurance to self-control, godliness to endurance, brotherly affection to godliness and love to brotherly affection. The list described growth that touched understanding, desire, persistence and relationships. These qualities increasing in them would keep their knowledge of Jesus Christ from becoming idle or unfruitful. Anyone lacking them was short-sighted and had forgotten cleansing from former sins. Peter did not present the qualities as a price paid to obtain cleansing. Remembered cleansing was the reason for growth, and failure to grow revealed a forgetfulness of the mercy already received.~The readers were therefore urged to be diligent in confirming their calling and election through this faithful response. Practising these things would keep them from falling and accompany an abundant entrance into the eternal kingdom of the Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ. Assurance was to become active rather than careless. The believer could look towards God's kingdom while cultivating the habits appropriate to it now. A practical response begins by examining a particular link in Peter's list. Knowledge may need the discipline of self-control, endurance may need the warmth of affection, or an existing concern for fellow believers may need to widen into love. Received grace makes such effort meaningful, and diligence lets its fruit become visible.|God provides what is needed for a godly life. Respond to his promises with diligent growth in character, endurance and love.|Which quality in Peter's list most needs deliberate practice in your present circumstances?
1:12-21|The Reminder That Would Outlive the Witness|Apostolic testimony and the sure word of prophecy|Peter explained why he intended to keep reminding the readers of truths they already knew and in which they were established. Remembering remained necessary even for informed believers. While he lived in the tent of his body, he considered it right to stir them up through reminders. He knew that his departure was approaching, as the Lord Jesus Christ had made clear to him. His concern was that after his death they should still be able to recall these things. The passage gives teaching a purpose larger than the continuing presence of the teacher. Faithful ministry seeks people grounded in truth well enough to remember and practise it when the familiar human voice is no longer beside them.~The announcement of Jesus Christ's power and coming did not arise from cleverly devised myths. Peter had been an eyewitness of his majesty. He recalled the honour and glory Jesus received from God the Father when the heavenly voice identified him as the beloved Son in whom the Father was pleased. Peter and the other witnesses had heard that voice while with Jesus on the holy mountain. The memory supplied testimony concerning the person whose future appearing the letter announced. The apostle was not asking readers to rest their lives upon an attractive invention. He spoke as someone whose own experience had included a disclosure of Christ's glory before the suffering and waiting that followed.~The prophetic word was also presented as a firm ground to which believers should pay attention, like a lamp shining in a dark place until the day dawned and the morning star rose in their hearts. The image recognised their present condition. They lived before the full revelation of the glory they awaited, and needed guidance within that interval. A lamp did not remove every surrounding darkness at once. It supplied light by which to attend and continue. Peter directed them towards the word God had given, rather than a restless dependence upon new claims of secret knowledge or impressive speech. Their hope needed a trustworthy source that would endure through delay and opposition.~He then explained that prophecy of Scripture did not arise from a prophet's own interpretation or from human will. People spoke from God as they were carried along by the Holy Spirit. The emphasis concerns the divine origin of the prophetic word, and should not be used to place one present teacher's opinion beyond examination. The God who gave the word remained the authority over its faithful handling. The passage calls the reader to remember, attend and test claims responsibly. Apostolic witness and Spirit-given Scripture provide a durable foundation. Reading carefully, retaining what has been understood and passing it on truthfully are ways to honour that foundation while awaiting the day when Christ's glory is fully revealed.|Faith rests upon trustworthy testimony and God's Spirit-given word. Give Scripture sustained attention and keep its truth available through faithful remembrance.|What truth do you need to remember more deliberately so that pressure or persuasive speech does not displace it?
2:1-22|The Teachers Who Promised Freedom|Greed and corruption expose a destructive message|Peter warned that false teachers would arise among the believers as false prophets had arisen in Israel. They would introduce destructive teachings, even denying the Master who bought them, and bring swift destruction upon themselves. Many would follow their corrupt conduct, causing the way of truth to be dishonoured. Greed would lead them to exploit people through fabricated words. The warning joined teaching, character and motive. Error was not measured only by whether a speaker used unfamiliar expressions. It could also be seen in the way people were made into a source of gain and encouraged towards conduct that contradicted the Lord the teacher claimed to represent.~God's judgment was not asleep. Peter recalled angels who sinned and were confined for judgment, the ancient world in Noah's day, and Sodom and Gomorrah reduced to ashes. He also recalled Noah's preservation and Lot's deliverance from a setting whose lawlessness distressed him. These examples showed that the Lord knew how to rescue the godly from trial and reserve the unrighteous for judgment. The passage did not depend upon adding names, ranks or elaborate histories to the angels mentioned. The stated events were sufficient for its argument. Neither a creature's former position nor the number of people sharing a corrupt way could make rebellion safe before God.~The teachers were described as bold and self-willed, speaking contemptuously about things beyond their understanding. Even greater angelic beings did not bring the kind of abusive accusations in which these speakers delighted. Their public confidence concealed appetite and ignorance. They indulged themselves while sharing meals with the community, pursued adultery, enticed unstable people and trained their hearts in greed. Balaam supplied a searching comparison. He had loved the wages of wrongdoing, yet was rebuked through a donkey speaking with a human voice. The story exposed the folly of a religious figure whose desire for reward overruled the instruction he should have obeyed. Gifted speech and spiritual reputation could not make a greedy direction right.~The false teachers were springs without water and clouds driven by a storm. Their impressive words promised freedom while they themselves were slaves of corruption. People became enslaved to what mastered them. The closing warning described the grave danger of escaping the world's pollution through knowing Christ and then returning to its control. The proverbs of the returning dog and the washed sow made relapse deliberately repulsive. The chapter calls believers to test a promised freedom by the life it actually produces. Does it deepen obedience to Christ, or excuse the desires that once held them? A faithful response remains close to God's word, refuses exploitation and seeks the holiness that an empty promise of liberty has tried to replace.|A message that excuses corruption and exploits hearers contradicts Christ. Test teaching through Scripture, its fruit and the motives it encourages.|What promised freedom needs to be examined because it may be giving an old desire permission to rule again?
3:1-18|What Kind of People Ought You to Be?|The Lord's patience calls for repentance and holy expectation|Peter described the letter as another attempt to awaken sincere thinking through remembrance. The readers should recall the words of the holy prophets and the commandment of the Lord and Saviour delivered through the apostles. Scoffers would come following their own desires and asking what had become of the promise of Christ's coming. They argued that everything continued as it had from the beginning. Peter answered that this account deliberately overlooked God's word in creation and the judgment of the ancient world through the flood. The present heavens and earth were also kept by his word for the day of judgment. Continuance was therefore not proof that God had never acted or would never act again.~The beloved readers were not to forget that one day was with the Lord as a thousand years and a thousand years as one day. The statement humbled human attempts to measure God's faithfulness through ordinary impatience. It did not provide a formula for calculating a hidden date. The Lord was not slow concerning his promise. He was patient, not willing that any should perish but that all should come to repentance. The time that scoffers interpreted as evidence against him was an expression of mercy. A faithful response used that patience to repent and help others hear the gospel, rather than making delay an excuse for preserving the very desires the coming judgment would expose.~The day of the Lord would come unexpectedly, like a thief. The heavens would pass away, the elements be dissolved with intense heat, and the earth and its works face God's decisive reckoning. Peter's question then became practical. In view of this future, what kind of people ought the readers to be in holy conduct and godliness? They were to await and earnestly desire God's day, looking according to his promise for new heavens and a new earth where righteousness dwelt. The passage did not direct them to abandon present responsibilities. It asked that their present life become appropriate to the promised future, where righteousness would be at home rather than treated as an interruption.~They should therefore be diligent to be found in peace, without spot or blemish, and count the Lord's patience as salvation. Peter referred to Paul's letters, acknowledging wisdom given to him and recognising that some things were difficult to understand. Unstable people distorted them, as they did other Scriptures, to their own destruction. The readers, already warned, should guard against being carried away by error and losing their firm footing. The final instruction was to grow in the grace and knowledge of the Lord and Saviour Jesus Christ. Glory belonged to him now and forever. The letter ended with patient expectation expressed through growth, holiness and careful attention to the word, while God's mercy still gave opportunity to turn and live.|God's patience gives opportunity for repentance. Await Christ through holiness, careful reading of Scripture and continued growth in grace.|How should the opportunity God gives today change the way you prepare for Christ's coming?
