1:1-2:9|Consider Your Ways and Build|The returned people resume the Lord's work and receive courage for what they can see|In the second year of Darius, the prophet Haggai brought the Lord's word to Zerubbabel, governor of Judah, and Joshua, the high priest. The people said the time had yet to come to build the Lord's house. Haggai asked how the time had come for them to live in panelled houses while that house lay waste. He told them to consider their ways. They sowed much and brought in little. They ate and drank without satisfaction, put on clothes without warming themselves and earned wages that seemed to go into a bag full of holes. They had worked and spent their strength. Haggai invited them to look at the direction of that work before they simply did more of it.~The Lord told them to go up into the hills, bring wood and build the house, where he would take pleasure and be glorified. They had looked for much and received little. What they brought home the Lord blew away because his house lay in ruins while each hurried about his own house. The heavens had withheld dew and drought had reached grain, wine, oil, people, livestock and the work of their hands. Zerubbabel, Joshua and the remnant obeyed and feared before the Lord. Haggai then delivered a short message from him: he was with them. The Lord stirred the spirits of their leaders and of the remnant, and on the twenty-fourth day of the sixth month they came and worked on his house.~In the seventh month another word came. Some could remember the former house and its glory. Haggai asked what the house looked like to them now. In their eyes it could seem like nothing. Their obedience had put them in a place where they were daily reminded of how much had been lost. The Lord named each of their leaders and the people of the land in his encouragement. They were to be strong and work because he was with them. He reminded them of the covenant he made when they came out of Egypt and said that his Spirit remained among them. They had reason to go on with what was in front of them, even with the memory of something greater behind them.~The Lord then promised to shake heaven, earth, sea and dry land, and to shake the nations. The treasures of the nations would come, and he would fill this house with glory. Silver and gold belonged to him. What the people could afford or produce did not set the limits of what he could do. The latter glory of the house would be greater than the former, and in that place he would give peace. The people had been asked first to fetch timber and work. The promise put that ordinary work in the hands of the God who claimed the riches of the nations and could give the peace their effort alone could never secure.|Give God's work the place obedience requires, and keep at it when outward results look small. His presence gives strength for work whose future you cannot yet see.|What responsibility before God keeps being postponed while you give your best time and resources to other things?
2:10-23|From This Day I Will Bless You|Haggai speaks of defilement, a coming harvest and the Lord's servant Zerubbabel|On the twenty-fourth day of the ninth month Haggai received another word. The Lord told him to ask the priests about the law. If a person carried holy meat in the fold of his garment and that fold touched bread, stew, wine, oil or other food, would the food become holy? They answered that it would not. Then he asked whether a person made unclean by contact with a dead body would make such food unclean by touching it. The priests said that he would. So it was, the Lord said, with this people and this nation before him. The work of their hands and what they offered there were unclean. The questions led the hearers from a ruling the priests knew well to a judgment about the standing of their own work.~Haggai tells them to consider the days before stone was set upon stone in the Lord's temple. When someone came expecting a heap of twenty measures, there were only ten. When someone came to draw fifty measures from the wine vat, there were only twenty. The Lord had struck the work of their hands with scorching, mildew and hail, yet they had not turned to him. Those numbers belonged to the experience of people counting what they needed for their households. The prophet put the familiar shortfall next to the response God had looked for. The people's activity at a holy place could not by itself make their life and offerings acceptable to him.~He tells them to mark the date, the twenty-fourth of the ninth month, from the day the foundation of the Lord's temple was laid. Is the seed still in the barn? The vine, fig tree, pomegranate and olive have yet to bear. The people hear the promise before they can point to the fruit: from this day the Lord will bless them. They can remember precisely when he spoke even though the crops have not yet proved what the season will bring. The same people who had previously come to the heap and vat expecting more than they found now have the Lord's word before there is anything in the storehouse to reassure them.~A second word came that same day, this time for Zerubbabel. The Lord would shake heaven and earth, overthrow royal thrones and destroy the strength of the kingdoms of the nations. Chariots and their riders would fall, and men would come down by the sword of their own brothers. In that day the Lord would take Zerubbabel son of Shealtiel, his servant, and make him like a signet ring because he had chosen him. The governor who had worked with a small remnant received a promise from the God who could overturn thrones. Haggai ends with the Lord's choice giving Zerubbabel a place in a future beyond what the governor could see from the rebuilding site.|Work offered to God calls for a heart that turns to him. His promises give ground for faithful labour even before we can see its fruit.|What would you do faithfully this week if you stopped using immediate results as your measure of whether to obey?
