1:1-2:13|The Horsemen and the City Without Walls|The Lord remembers Jerusalem and promises to dwell among his people|In the eighth month of the second year of Darius, Zechariah brought the Lord's word to the people who had returned from exile. Their fathers had refused to listen when earlier prophets told them to turn from their evil ways. Those fathers had died, and the prophets had died too, but the words and judgments of the Lord had overtaken the people. They had to acknowledge that he had dealt with them according to their ways. The returning generation heard the appeal while it still had a choice before it. The Lord called the people to return to him and promised to return to them. Jerusalem's rebuilding began with the question of whether those who now lived there would listen any better than those who had gone before them.~Three months later, in the night, Zechariah saw a man riding a red horse among myrtle trees in a low place. Behind him stood red, speckled and white horses. The riders had been sent to walk through the earth and reported that it was at rest. The angel of the Lord asked how long mercy would be withheld from Jerusalem and the cities of Judah, against which the Lord had been angry these seventy years. The quiet condition of the nations did not settle the question of Jerusalem's suffering. The Lord answered with comforting words. He was jealous for Jerusalem and angry with the nations at ease, which had made the calamity worse. His house would be built and a measuring line stretched over the city. Its towns would again overflow with good things.~Zechariah lifted his eyes and saw four horns. The angel said they were the powers that had scattered Judah, Israel and Jerusalem. Then the Lord showed him four craftsmen who had come to terrify and cast down the horns that had lifted themselves against Judah. In the next vision a man went out with a measuring line to find Jerusalem's width and length. An angel told another messenger to run to meet him with news of a city too full of people and livestock to be contained by walls. The Lord himself would be a wall of fire around her and glory in her midst. The promised protection came from the same presence that would make the city a place worth returning to.~The scattered people are called to flee from the land of the north and escape from Babylon. The Lord speaks of the nations that plundered them and describes an injury against his people as touching the apple of his eye. Daughter Zion is called to sing because he is coming to dwell in her midst. Many nations will join themselves to the Lord in that day and become his people. Judah will be his portion in the holy land, and Jerusalem will again be his choice. All flesh is told to be silent before him because he has roused himself from his holy dwelling. The city that the horsemen's report might have made easy to overlook receives a promise that reaches further than its future streets.|God remembers people whose suffering others have learned to live with. His call to return joins the promise of his presence to a call for renewed obedience.|What part of your life needs a return to God before you can think clearly about rebuilding what was lost?
3:1-4:14|Clean Garments and a Lamp That Burns|Joshua is cleansed and Zerubbabel learns where strength for rebuilding comes from|Zechariah sees Joshua the high priest standing before the angel of the Lord. Satan stands at his right hand to accuse him. The Lord rebukes the accuser and speaks of Jerusalem as his chosen city and of Joshua as a brand pulled out of the fire. Joshua is wearing filthy garments. Those standing before the angel are told to remove them, and Joshua hears that his iniquity has been taken away. He is to be clothed in clean garments. Zechariah asks that a clean turban be placed on his head, and it is done. The high priest's place in the vision begins with a condition he could not conceal and a change he could not make to his own standing.~Joshua is then charged to walk in the Lord's ways and keep his charge. He will govern the house and have care of its courts. Joshua and his companions are a sign of things to come, for the Lord will bring his servant called the Branch. A stone with seven eyes is set before Joshua, and the Lord will engrave it and remove the land's iniquity in one day. In that day each person will invite a neighbour to sit beneath vine and fig tree. The cleansing of the priest opens onto a promise for the land, and the picture of neighbours sitting together gives a simple shape to the peace for which the people have waited.~The angel wakes Zechariah as one wakened from sleep and asks what he sees. There is a golden lampstand with a bowl on top and seven lamps with their channels. On either side stands an olive tree. Zechariah asks what these mean. The answer comes first as a word for Zerubbabel. The task will be accomplished by the Lord's Spirit. Might and power are insufficient for it. The great mountain before Zerubbabel will become a plain, and he will bring out the top stone amid cries of grace to it. The hands that laid the temple's foundation will finish it. The day of small things deserves no contempt. The plumb line in Zerubbabel's hand will become a cause for rejoicing.~The seven are described as the eyes of the Lord ranging through the whole earth. Zechariah returns to his question about the olive trees and then asks about the two branches that pour golden oil through golden pipes. The angel tells him they are the two anointed ones who stand by the Lord of all the earth. Zechariah's hearers have now seen the high priest receive clean garments and the governor receive a word that his hands will finish the work. Each has duties that call for faithful action. The Lord supplies cleansing where guilt was exposed and his Spirit where the work looked too great for the people charged with it.|God supplies cleansing for service and strength for work his people cannot complete by human resources alone. A small beginning can be received with gratitude and tended faithfully.|What small act of service have you been tempted to despise because you cannot yet see what God may bring from it?
5:1-6:15|The Scroll, the Basket and the Crown|God deals with wickedness and gives a sign of the priestly ruler to come|Zechariah looks up and sees a flying scroll, twenty cubits long and ten cubits wide. The angel tells him it is a curse going out over the whole land against the thief and the person who swears falsely. The Lord will send it into the house of the one who steals and into the house of the one who swears falsely by his name. It will remain there and consume even its timber and stones. After the promise that the temple will be finished, Zechariah sees God's word enter houses in which people's daily dealings have dishonoured him. A building can be sound to the eye and still contain wrongs for which its household must answer.~The angel next shows him a measuring basket with a lead cover. A woman sits inside it, and the angel calls her Wickedness. He pushes her down into the basket and throws the heavy cover over its opening. Two women with wings like a stork's lift the basket between earth and sky. When Zechariah asks where they are taking it, the angel says they are going to build it a house in the land of Shinar. When the house is ready the basket will be set there on its base. Zechariah is given a place and a destination in the vision. Wickedness, which the scroll had exposed in the houses of the land, is now carried away in a vessel whose lid has been firmly shut.~Four chariots come out from between two mountains of bronze. Their horses are red, black, white and strong dappled horses. The angel describes them as four winds or spirits of heaven going out after standing before the Lord of all the earth. The black horses go to the north country, the white ones follow, and the dappled ones go to the south. The strong horses are eager to patrol the earth and are sent out to do so. Those going north have given the Lord's spirit rest there. The prophet who first saw horsemen returning from their patrol now sees chariots sent out from the presence of the Lord who governs the places to which they go.~The Lord then gives Zechariah a task among people who have returned from Babylon. He is to receive silver and gold from the returnees, go to Josiah's house, make a crown and set it on the head of Joshua the high priest. He is to speak of the man called the Branch, who will grow up from his place and build the Lord's temple. He will bear royal honour, sit and rule on his throne, and priesthood and rule will be joined in a counsel of peace. The crown will remain in the temple as a memorial. People from far away will come and help to build. Zechariah closes the scene with a call to diligent obedience to the Lord's voice.|The Lord sees wrong done in private and has authority to remove it. His promise of the Branch gives his people reason to rebuild with a conscience attentive to his word.|What dishonesty needs to be put right before you can give yourself freely to the work God has put before you?
7:1-8:23|The Fast That Became a Feast|The Lord answers a question about mourning by calling Jerusalem to truth and mercy|In the fourth year of Darius, men came to seek the Lord's favour and ask priests and prophets whether they should continue to weep and abstain in the fifth month as they had for years. The Lord asked whether their fasts in the fifth and seventh months during the seventy years had really been for him. When they ate and drank, they did it for themselves. He pointed them back to the words spoken by the earlier prophets when Jerusalem and the surrounding country were inhabited and at ease. The question the delegation had brought concerned a date on the calendar. The Lord led them to examine how they had treated people on all the days that calendar contained.~The earlier word was to give true judgments and show mercy and compassion to one another. They were forbidden to oppress the widow, orphan, stranger or poor, or to plan evil against one another in their hearts. Their fathers turned a stubborn shoulder, stopped their ears and made their hearts hard against the law and the words sent by God's Spirit through the prophets. When the Lord called, they did not listen. When they called, he would not listen. He scattered them with a whirlwind and the pleasant land became desolate. Zechariah makes the delegates remember what brought about the sorrow they had been commemorating. Their answer about fasting had to take that history seriously.~The Lord says he is jealous for Zion and will return to dwell in Jerusalem. It will be called the city of truth, and his mountain the holy mountain. Old men and women will sit in its streets with staffs in their hands, and boys and girls will play there. What seems marvellous to the remnant is possible for the Lord. He will save people from east and west to be his people in truth and righteousness. Those hearing the words spoken since the temple's foundation was laid are to strengthen their hands. The former time brought scant wages and little safety. Now he promises a seed of peace, fruitful vines, crops and dew, and says the people that had been a curse among the nations will become a blessing.~He tells them to speak truth, give judgments that make for peace, refuse to devise evil and hate false oaths. Then he answers the question directly. Fasts of the fourth, fifth, seventh and tenth months will become joyful feasts for Judah, so they are to love truth and peace. People from many cities will urge one another to go seek the Lord in Jerusalem. Ten people from nations of different languages will take hold of a Jew's garment and ask to go with him because they have heard that God is with his people. The city whose streets had emptied now hears of visitors asking to join its worship.|Worship that pleases God reaches the way we judge disputes and deal with vulnerable people. Truth and peace give substance to the joy of gathered worship.|Which person would experience God's mercy more plainly this week if you changed the way you deal with them?
9:1-10:12|The King Who Comes Riding on a Donkey|Judgment reaches the surrounding cities and Zion is promised a king who brings peace|The word of the Lord reaches the lands and cities around Israel. It names Hadrach, Damascus and Hamath, then Tyre and Sidon with their wisdom. Tyre built herself a fortress and heaped up silver like dust and gold like the mud of the streets. The Lord will dispossess her, strike her power at sea and leave her to be consumed by fire. The Philistine cities hear and tremble. Gaza's king will perish, Ashkelon will lose its inhabitants and the pride of the Philistines will be cut off. The Lord will remove blood and forbidden food from their mouths, and a remnant will belong to him. He will encamp around his house to guard it against those who pass back and forth.~Zion is told to rejoice because her king is coming. He is righteous and brings salvation, humble and riding on a donkey, on the colt of a donkey. Chariot, war horse and battle bow are cut off. The king will speak peace to the nations and his rule will extend from sea to sea. The Lord speaks of the blood of his covenant with Zion and of freeing prisoners from a pit without water. Prisoners of hope are called back to the stronghold, and he promises to restore double to them. The passage pictures Judah as the Lord's bow and Ephraim its arrow. He will appear over his people, sound the trumpet and defend them. They will shine like the stones of a crown in his land, and grain and new wine will sustain the young.~The people are told to ask the Lord for rain in the season of spring rain. He makes the storm clouds and gives showers and crops. Household idols speak nonsense, diviners see lies and dreamers give empty comfort. The people wander like sheep afflicted for lack of a shepherd. The Lord is angry with the shepherds and will attend to his flock, the house of Judah. From Judah will come the cornerstone, the tent peg, the battle bow and every ruler. The people will have strength for battle because the Lord is with them. The call to ask him for rain puts their daily needs alongside the promise of deliverance. Both give them reason to cease trusting the voices that have led them astray.~The Lord promises to strengthen Judah and save the house of Joseph. He will bring them back because he has compassion for them. Ephraim's heart will rejoice, and their children will see it. The Lord will whistle for the people he has redeemed and gather them. Though scattered among the nations, they will remember him in distant places and return with their children. He will bring them from Egypt and Assyria to Gilead and Lebanon until there is scarcely room for them. The sea and the river cannot prevent their passage. He will strengthen them in himself and they will walk in his name. The chapter ends with people who had wandered without a shepherd walking under the name of the one who brought them home.|The humble king of Zion brings peace under his righteous rule. The Lord calls his people to seek him for their daily needs and trust him to gather and strengthen those who have been scattered.|Where are you seeking reassurance from a source that cannot give what you need to ask of God?
11:1-17|The Shepherd Sold for Thirty Pieces|Zechariah acts out the rejection of faithful care and the rise of a worthless shepherd|Lebanon is told to open its doors to fire. The cedars fall, cypresses wail, and the oaks of Bashan are brought down. The sound of shepherds mourning is heard because their glory is ruined. Young lions roar because the thickets of the Jordan are laid waste. Then the Lord tells Zechariah to feed a flock marked for slaughter. Its buyers kill the sheep and escape punishment. Its sellers bless the Lord because they have grown rich. Its own shepherds have no pity for it. The Lord says he will no longer spare the inhabitants of the land. They will fall into the hands of their neighbours and their king, and he will leave them to experience the harm those hands bring.~Zechariah takes up the work of feeding the flock, with particular attention to its afflicted sheep. He takes two staffs. One he calls Favour and the other Union. In one month he removes three shepherds. He grows weary of them and they detest him. Then he says he will no longer feed the flock. What is dying will die, what is being destroyed will be destroyed, and those remaining will devour one another. He takes the staff called Favour and breaks it, ending the covenant it represented with the peoples. The afflicted members of the flock who were watching recognise that what they have seen is the word of the Lord. They have paid attention to the shepherd in circumstances where the people profiting from the flock had little reason to care what he said.~The shepherd asks for his wages if his hearers think it right to pay him. They weigh out thirty pieces of silver. The Lord tells him to throw that price, the sum at which they valued him, to the potter. Zechariah takes the silver and throws it to the potter in the house of the Lord. He then breaks the other staff, Union, to break the brotherhood between Judah and Israel. The prophet gives us objects to remember when considering what has been lost. One staff is broken, the shepherd is paid off at a stated price, and then the staff bearing the name of the people's bond is broken as well.~The Lord tells Zechariah to take up the equipment of a foolish shepherd. A shepherd will arise who does not attend to the perishing, seek the young or scattered, heal the injured or feed the healthy. He devours the flesh of the fat sheep and tears their hoofs. The Lord pronounces woe over this worthless shepherd who deserts the flock. A sword will strike his arm and his right eye, so the arm withers and the eye is blinded. The shepherd who used his office to feed himself is held to account for the harm his neglect allowed and for the injury he did with his own hands.|The Lord holds shepherds accountable for the people entrusted to them. A community also has reason to examine how it receives faithful care before rejecting it for easier arrangements.|Who has responsibility for someone easily overlooked in your fellowship, and what part of that care can you take up this week?
12:1-13:9|The Pierced One and the Open Fountain|Jerusalem receives a spirit of prayer, mourns and is offered cleansing|The Lord who stretched out the heavens, founded the earth and formed the human spirit speaks concerning Israel. Jerusalem will become a cup that makes the surrounding peoples reel and a heavy stone that injures those who try to lift it. Nations gather against it. The Lord will strike horses with panic and their riders with madness while keeping his eyes open over Judah. Judah's leaders will recognise that the inhabitants of Jerusalem find strength in the Lord of hosts. The leaders will be like a fire among wood and among sheaves, and Jerusalem will remain in its place. God will save Judah's tents first so that the house of David and the inhabitants of Jerusalem cannot exalt themselves over Judah. Even the feeble will be like David, and the Lord will seek to destroy the nations that come against the city.~Then the Lord promises to pour on David's house and Jerusalem's inhabitants a spirit of grace and supplication. They will look upon the one they have pierced and mourn as for an only child, bitterly as for a firstborn. The mourning will be as great as that at Hadad Rimmon in the plain of Megiddo. The land will mourn family by family, with the houses of David, Nathan, Levi and Shimei named and their wives apart. All the remaining families will mourn in the same way. Zechariah follows the defence of Jerusalem with a scene in which the rescued city is brought to grief over what has happened in its own midst. The households that share the sorrow still have to face it individually.~A fountain will be opened for David's house and Jerusalem for sin and uncleanness. The Lord will remove the names of idols, the false prophets and the unclean spirit from the land. A person who prophesies falsely in the Lord's name will be opposed even by his own parents. Those prophets will be ashamed of their visions and give up the hairy garment once used to deceive. One says he is a farmer and, when asked about his wounds, answers that he received them in the house of his friends. The promise of cleansing has consequences for what the people will call worship and whose claims they will accept as words from God.~The Lord calls for the sword to awake against his shepherd, the man who is his companion. The shepherd will be struck and the sheep scattered, and the Lord will turn his hand upon the little ones. Two parts in the land will perish and a third remain. The Lord will bring that third through fire, refining it as silver and testing it as gold. They will call upon his name and he will answer. He will say they are his people and they will say that the Lord is their God. The reading ends with that exchange. The prayer first poured out on the mourning city becomes the speech of people who have passed through refining and found that the Lord hears them.|God gives grace to mourn sin and offers cleansing to the people who need it. Through refining he draws people to call upon his name and acknowledge him as their God.|What would honest grief over sin lead you to confess, repair or leave behind?
14:1-21|The Lord Will Be King Over All the Earth|Jerusalem's final siege gives way to worship and holiness in ordinary life|Zechariah describes a day of the Lord when the spoil of Jerusalem will be divided in the city. The nations gather against it. Houses are plundered, women are violated and half the city goes into exile, though a remnant remains. The Lord goes out to fight those nations. His feet stand on the Mount of Olives east of Jerusalem and the mountain splits, opening a valley to which the people can flee as they did in the days of the earthquake under Uzziah. The Lord comes with his holy ones. Zechariah begins with the city exposed to terrible harm and then places deliverance in the coming of the Lord himself.~The day is known to the Lord and has light at evening. Living waters flow from Jerusalem, half towards the eastern sea and half towards the western sea, in summer as well as in winter. The Lord will be king over all the earth. In that day the Lord will be one and his name one. The surrounding land becomes a plain while Jerusalem is raised and inhabited, from its gates to the king's winepresses. People will dwell safely and the curse of destruction will no longer threaten the city. A place once plundered in its own streets is described by the prophet through dependable water, recognisable bounds and the safety of those who live within them.~The Lord strikes the peoples who fought against Jerusalem with a plague. Their flesh, eyes and tongues waste away, and panic from the Lord causes them to turn on one another. Judah will fight at Jerusalem and gather the wealth of the surrounding nations, including gold, silver and clothing. A plague also falls on the animals in the enemy camps. After this, survivors from those nations go up year by year to worship the King, the Lord of hosts, and keep the Feast of Booths. Families that refuse to go receive no rain. Egypt is named in the warning as well. The nations first seen gathering to war now appear in the same prophecy as people required to come to Jerusalem for worship.~The inscription of holiness to the Lord will be found even on the horses' bells. The cooking pots in the Lord's house will be like the bowls before the altar, and every pot in Jerusalem and Judah will be holy to the Lord. Those who offer sacrifices will use them. The chapter closes with the removal from the Lord's house of the people the final verse calls Canaanites. Zechariah has moved from a besieged city to a city where vessels from ordinary households can be used in worship. The Lord's kingship reaches the nations and the places where his people prepare a meal. The book ends with holiness visible in such common things.|The Lord's reign calls the nations to worship and reaches the ordinary things his people use. Holiness belongs in the home and the street as surely as in the place of sacrifice.|What ordinary tool, room or routine could you use more consciously in service to the Lord?
