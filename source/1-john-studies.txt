1:1-2:29|Walking in the Light Together|Confession, obedience and fellowship with Christ|John begins by speaking of what was from the beginning, which he and others had heard, seen with their eyes and touched with their hands concerning the Word of life. The life was manifested, and the witnesses proclaim it so their readers may share fellowship with them, with the Father and with his Son Jesus Christ. John writes for fullness of joy. The message he received is that God is light and in him there is no darkness. A claim to fellowship while walking in darkness is false. Walking in the light brings fellowship with one another, and the blood of Jesus cleanses from all sin.~John anticipates the ways people might avoid this truth. A denial of sin deceives the speaker, while confession meets the faithfulness and justice of God, who forgives and cleanses. John writes to keep his readers from sin, and he gives hope to the person who has sinned. Jesus Christ the righteous is their advocate with the Father and the atoning sacrifice for sins, with a reach that extends to the whole world. Knowing him takes visible form in keeping his commandments and walking as he walked. A person may claim to be in the light and still hate a brother. John says such hatred leaves the person walking blind in the dark.~John addresses children, fathers and young people, reminding them of forgiveness, knowledge of the Father and of him who is from the beginning, and victory over the evil one through the abiding word of God. He urges them to refuse the world's desires and pride in possessions. The world and its desires pass away, while the person who does God's will abides. John then speaks of the last hour and of antichrists who had gone out from the community. The controversy touches the identity of Jesus. Denial that Jesus is the Christ involves denial of the Father and the Son. Whoever confesses the Son has the Father also.~The readers had an anointing from the Holy One. John urges them to let what they heard from the beginning abide in them. In that way they would abide in the Son and in the Father, with eternal life as the promise given them. His words about the anointing come as a safeguard against those who would lead them astray. He calls the children to abide in Christ so they may have confidence at his coming. The passage closes by connecting God's righteousness with righteous practice in those born of him. What began with eyewitness testimony now reaches the habits of people who share in that fellowship.|Fellowship with Christ shows itself in honest confession, love for fellow believers and fidelity to what the apostles taught about him.|What part of your conduct would change this week if you brought it openly into God's light?
3:1-4:21|Love With More Than Words|The children of God, the testing of spirits and the love that casts out fear|John asks his readers to consider the love the Father has given them in calling them children of God. What they will be has yet to appear, but when Christ appears they will be like him because they will see him as he is. This hope has a present consequence. A person who has it purifies himself. John describes sin as lawlessness and says Christ appeared to take away sins and destroy the works of the devil. He puts the difference between the children of God and the children of the devil in terms his readers can examine, including whether a person practises righteousness and loves a brother.~Cain provides a warning. His own deeds were evil and his brother's were righteous, and he murdered him. John tells his readers that love for the brothers is evidence of passing from death to life. The shape of that love comes from Christ laying down his life for them. John then imagines a believer with enough of this world's goods who sees a brother in need and shuts his heart. The question is whether God's love can abide in such a person. Love is to take form in deed and truth. John brings the matter into prayer, assuring readers that God is greater than a condemning heart and calling them to believe in Jesus Christ and love one another.~John then tells the beloved to test the spirits because many false prophets have gone out into the world. The test concerns whether Jesus Christ is confessed as having come in the flesh. He contrasts the voices the world hears with the testimony that comes from God. The readers can take heart because the one who is in them is greater than the one who is in the world. The warning about deception sits alongside a direct command to love. John can urge both because the same God who sent his Son supplies the truth by which teaching is measured and the love by which believers are to live.~God showed his love by sending his only Son so that people might live through him. The Father sent the Son as the Saviour of the world. Whoever confesses Jesus as the Son of God abides in God, and the gift of the Spirit assures believers of that relationship. Perfect love casts out fear connected with punishment, giving confidence for the day of judgment. John traces the ability to love back to God's first love. He finishes by asking how a person can love the unseen God while hating a brother he has seen. The command to love God comes with the command to love the brother standing before us.|God's love gives us confidence before him and calls us to meet the needs of people we can see.|Who has a need you already know about, and what practical act of love could you offer this week?
5:1-21|The Witness God Has Given About His Son|Faith, eternal life and prayer with confidence|John says that everyone who believes Jesus is the Christ has been born of God. Love for the Father extends to those born of him, and love for God's children goes together with loving God and keeping his commands. His commands are described as being within the reach of those who have been born of him. Faith is the victory that overcomes the world, and John identifies the person who overcomes as the one who believes Jesus is the Son of God. The passage gathers together matters the letter has already brought into view. Belief about Jesus, family love and obedience belong within the same life.~John speaks of Jesus Christ coming by water and blood and joins these to the testimony of the Spirit, who is truth. He insists on the place of the blood as well as the water. These witnesses agree. If human testimony is received, God's testimony about his Son has greater weight. The believer has this testimony within, while rejecting it treats God's witness as false. The substance of the testimony is that God has given eternal life and that this life is in his Son. John's purpose in writing to believers is that they may know they have eternal life. Assurance rests on God's testimony about the Son in whom that life is found.~John turns from assurance to prayer. The confidence believers have before God includes knowing that he hears requests made according to his will. This confidence extends to intercession when a brother is seen committing a sin that does not lead to death. John speaks of God giving life in answer to such prayer. He also refers to sin leading to death, without telling readers to pray concerning that case, and reminds them that all wrongdoing is sin. The distinction is stated briefly. The practical direction is clear enough to act on. Seeing another believer fall can be an occasion to turn towards God on that person's behalf.~The letter ends with a series of statements about what believers know. The one born of God is kept from the evil one, even while the whole world lies in the evil one's power. The Son of God has come and given understanding so believers may know the true one. John speaks of being in the one who is true, in his Son Jesus Christ, and brings together the true God and eternal life. Then comes the plain instruction to keep away from idols. It is a fitting last word for a letter that has traced life to the Son and asked readers to give their allegiance a visible form in everyday conduct.|God's testimony about his Son grounds assurance and gives believers confidence to pray according to his will.|How would assurance of life in Christ change the way you pray for someone who has fallen into sin?
