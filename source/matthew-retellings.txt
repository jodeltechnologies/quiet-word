1:1-17|The Family Into Which Jesus Came|God's promise through generations|Matthew opens with a family record. Beginning with Abraham, he follows the line through David and the kings of Judah, through the exile in Babylon, and on to Joseph, the husband of Mary. He names Tamar, Rahab, Ruth and the wife of Uriah along the way. The record ends with Jesus, who is called Christ.|The exile could seem to mark the end of David's house. Matthew keeps tracing the line beyond it. God's faithfulness can outlast the losses that make his promises seem remote.|What does Matthew gain by keeping the exile in the family record?
1:18-25|Joseph Takes Mary Home|Obedience when plans change|Mary was pledged to marry Joseph. Before they came together, she was found to be expecting a child. Matthew tells the reader from the beginning that the child was conceived by the Holy Spirit. Joseph did not yet have that explanation. He had to decide what to do with the news as he understood it.~Joseph was a righteous man, and he did not want to make Mary a public example. He intended to put her away privately. While he was considering what to do, an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream and addressed him as a son of David. The angel told him not to be afraid to take Mary as his wife, because the child she carried was from the Holy Spirit.~The instruction also concerned what would happen after the birth. Mary would have a son, and Joseph was to name him Jesus, because he would save his people from their sins. Matthew connects the birth with the prophet's words about a child called Emmanuel, which means God with us. The promise gives the child's coming a significance beyond the immediate trouble in Joseph's household.~When Joseph woke, he followed the angel's instruction and took Mary home as his wife. He did not have relations with her before she gave birth. When the son was born, Joseph gave him the name Jesus. The man who had been preparing to end the engagement now took responsibility for carrying out what the Lord had entrusted to him.|Joseph was prepared to act without making Mary a public example. When God gave him a different course, he acted on what he had heard.|Which instructions did Joseph carry out, and what do they show about his trust?
2:1-12|The Visitors From the East|Two responses to a king|After Jesus was born at Bethlehem, wise men from the East arrived in Jerusalem asking where the King of the Jews had been born. They had seen his star and had come to worship him. The question troubled Herod and, Matthew says, all Jerusalem with him. Herod gathered the chief priests and scribes and asked where the Christ was to be born. They named Bethlehem and gave him the words of the prophet about a ruler who would shepherd Israel.~Herod then met the wise men privately and asked when the star had appeared. He sent them to Bethlehem to search carefully. When they had found the child, he said, they should report back so that he too might go and worship. The visitors departed, and the star went before them until it stood over the place where the child was. Seeing it, they rejoiced greatly.~Inside the house they found the child with Mary his mother. They fell down, worshipped him and opened their treasures, offering gold, frankincense and myrrh. The journey that had begun with a question in the capital ended with them bowing before a child in a house.~Herod had asked them to return, but God warned them in a dream against doing so. They went back to their own country along another road. Herod had obtained an answer about where the ruler would come from. The visitors had gone there, given their gifts and left by the way God directed them.|Herod learned where the Christ would be born and used that knowledge to protect his power. The visitors used what they learned to seek and worship Jesus. Consider where your knowledge of Scripture is taking you.|What differences do you see between Herod's questions and the visitors' actions?

4:1-11|The Test in the Wilderness|Trusting God under pressure|After his baptism, Jesus was led by the Spirit into the wilderness to be tempted by the devil.
