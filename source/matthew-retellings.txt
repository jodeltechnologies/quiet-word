1:1-17|The Family Into Which Jesus Came|God's promise through generations|Matthew begins by naming Jesus Christ as the son of David and the son of Abraham. Then he traces the family line forward from Abraham. Isaac is followed by Jacob, then Judah and his brothers. At several points Matthew mentions women as well as fathers and sons. Perez and Zerah are named with Tamar. Rahab is named with Boaz, and Ruth with Obed. Ruth's son Obed is followed by Jesse, the father of David.~From David the record goes through the house of Judah's kings. Solomon is named as the son David had by Uriah's wife. The names that follow include Hezekiah, Manasseh and Josiah. Matthew brings this part of the record to the carrying away to Babylon. A family whose members had held a throne is now marked by a national loss.~The record continues after the exile. Jeconiah is followed by Shealtiel and Zerubbabel, and more names carry the family line towards Joseph. When Matthew comes to Joseph, the form of the record changes. He calls Joseph the husband of Mary and says that Jesus, who is called Christ, was born of her.~Matthew groups the names into three stretches of fourteen generations: Abraham to David, David to the exile, and the exile to Christ. The grouping helps the reader remember the turns in the family's history. The Gospel will now move from the names of generations to what took place in the house of Joseph and Mary.|The exile could seem to mark the end of David's house. Matthew keeps tracing the line beyond it. God's faithfulness can outlast the losses that make his promises seem remote.|What does Matthew gain by keeping the exile in the family record?
1:18-25|Joseph Takes Mary Home|Obedience when plans change|Mary was pledged to marry Joseph. Before they came together, she was found to be expecting a child. Matthew tells the reader from the beginning that the child was conceived by the Holy Spirit. Joseph did not yet have that explanation. He had to decide what to do with the news as he understood it.~Joseph was a righteous man, and he did not want to make Mary a public example. He intended to put her away privately. While he was considering what to do, an angel of the Lord appeared to him in a dream and addressed him as a son of David. The angel told him not to be afraid to take Mary as his wife, because the child she carried was from the Holy Spirit.~The instruction also concerned what would happen after the birth. Mary would have a son, and Joseph was to name him Jesus, because he would save his people from their sins. Matthew connects the birth with the prophet's words about a child called Emmanuel, which means God with us. The promise gives the child's coming a significance beyond the immediate trouble in Joseph's household.~When Joseph woke, he followed the angel's instruction and took Mary home as his wife. He did not have relations with her before she gave birth. When the son was born, Joseph gave him the name Jesus. The man who had been preparing to end the engagement now took responsibility for carrying out what the Lord had entrusted to him.|Joseph was prepared to act without making Mary a public example. When God gave him a different course, he acted on what he had heard.|Which instructions did Joseph carry out, and what do they show about his trust?
2:1-12|The Visitors From the East|Two responses to a king|After Jesus was born at Bethlehem, wise men from the East arrived in Jerusalem asking where the King of the Jews had been born. They had seen his star and had come to worship him. The question troubled Herod and, Matthew says, all Jerusalem with him. Herod gathered the chief priests and scribes and asked where the Christ was to be born. They named Bethlehem and gave him the words of the prophet about a ruler who would shepherd Israel.~Herod then met the wise men privately and asked when the star had appeared. He sent them to Bethlehem to search carefully. When they had found the child, he said, they should report back so that he too might go and worship. The visitors departed, and the star went before them until it stood over the place where the child was. Seeing it, they rejoiced greatly.~Inside the house they found the child with Mary his mother. They fell down, worshipped him and opened their treasures, offering gold, frankincense and myrrh. The journey that had begun with a question in the capital ended with them bowing before a child in a house.~Herod had asked them to return, but God warned them in a dream against doing so. They went back to their own country along another road. Herod had obtained an answer about where the ruler would come from. The visitors had gone there, given their gifts and left by the way God directed them.|Herod learned where the Christ would be born and used that knowledge to protect his power. The visitors used what they learned to seek and worship Jesus. Consider where your knowledge of Scripture is taking you.|What differences do you see between Herod's questions and the visitors' actions?
2:13-23|The Flight to Egypt and the Return|God's care amid danger and grief|After the visitors had gone, an angel appeared to Joseph in a dream. He was to take the child and his mother to Egypt and stay there until he received further word. Herod would search for the child to kill him. Joseph got up, took them that night and left. They remained in Egypt until Herod died. Matthew connects their time there with the prophet's words about God calling his son out of Egypt.~Herod realised the visitors had departed without reporting back. Enraged, he ordered the killing of the boys in Bethlehem and the surrounding district who were two years old or younger. He used the time he had learned from the visitors to set the age. Matthew recalls Jeremiah's picture of Rachel weeping for her children and refusing comfort because they were gone. The escape of one family sits alongside the grief of others.~After Herod's death, an angel told Joseph in Egypt that those seeking the child's life were dead. Joseph took the child and his mother back towards Israel. On hearing that Archelaus ruled Judea in his father's place, he was afraid to go there. Another warning in a dream directed him away, and he withdrew into Galilee.~The family settled in Nazareth. Matthew associates that place with the words of the prophets about Jesus being called a Nazarene. The chapter ends with the family finding a place to live after leaving at night and changing course again on the way home.|Matthew records both the child's deliverance and the grief of the bereaved families. God's work in history gives room for lament as well as gratitude.|Why does Matthew include the cries of the grieving families in the account of Jesus' escape?
3:1-12|A Voice in the Wilderness|Repentance that bears fruit|John the Baptist came preaching in the wilderness of Judea. He called people to repent because the kingdom of heaven was near. Matthew presents him as the voice Isaiah spoke of, calling for the Lord's way to be prepared. John's clothing was made of camel's hair, and he wore a leather belt. His food was locusts and wild honey.~People left Jerusalem, Judea and the country around the Jordan to hear him. In the river they were baptised by him while confessing their sins. The wilderness became a place to speak openly about conduct people might otherwise have kept to themselves.~When many Pharisees and Sadducees came, John asked who had warned them to flee from coming wrath. He called for fruit consistent with repentance. They should not rely on saying Abraham was their father. God could raise children to Abraham from stones. John spoke of an axe already laid at the root of trees that failed to bear good fruit.~John then directed their attention to someone who would come after him. John used water and called for repentance. The coming one was mightier, and John said he was unworthy even to carry his sandals. He would baptise with the Holy Spirit and with fire. John closed with the image of someone clearing a threshing floor, gathering the wheat and burning the chaff. His preaching prepared the crowd for a person with authority beyond his own.|John's warning reaches people who rely on their religious ancestry. Repentance becomes visible in the life that follows confession.|What would count as fruit of repentance in a situation you are facing?
3:13-17|Jesus Comes to the Jordan|The Father's beloved Son|The crowds had come to John at the Jordan confessing their sins. John had told them that someone greater was coming after him. He had spoken of being unworthy to carry that person's sandals. Matthew now records who came to the river. Jesus travelled from Galilee to John and asked to be baptised.~John resisted. He told Jesus that he himself needed baptism from him and asked why Jesus was coming to him. That question brings the earlier preaching into the encounter. The man who had baptised the crowds now spoke of his own need before the one standing in front of him.~Jesus told him to allow it for the present because it was fitting for them to fulfil all righteousness. John consented. Matthew leaves the exchange there and turns to what happened as Jesus came up from the water. The heavens opened, and Jesus saw the Spirit of God coming down like a dove and alighting on him.~A voice from heaven declared that this was God's beloved Son, in whom he was well pleased. John had questioned whether he should baptise Jesus, and Jesus had answered by speaking of righteousness. The voice from heaven says who Jesus is as he begins the work Matthew will go on to recount. The next account takes him from the water into the wilderness, carrying that declaration into the time of testing.|Jesus willingly took the course that fulfilled righteousness. The Father's declaration identifies the one whose life and teaching Matthew now follows.|What does the Father's declaration tell you about the person John baptised?
4:1-11|The Test in the Wilderness|Trusting God under pressure|After his baptism, Jesus was led by the Spirit into the wilderness to be tempted by the devil. He fasted for forty days and forty nights and was hungry. The tempter approached him at the point of that hunger. If he was the Son of God, the tempter suggested, he could command the stones to become bread. Jesus answered from Scripture that a person lives by every word that comes from God, as well as needing bread.~The devil then took him to the holy city and set him on a high point of the temple. Again he challenged Jesus to act if he was God's Son. This time he used words from Scripture, appealing to a promise that God's angels would bear him up. Jesus brought another command to bear on the proposal. God must not be put to the test.~On a very high mountain the devil showed him the kingdoms of the world and their glory. He offered them in exchange for Jesus falling down and worshipping him. Jesus told Satan to go away and replied from Scripture that worship and service belong to the Lord God alone.~The devil left, and angels came and ministered to Jesus. Matthew records their arrival after Jesus had refused to force a display of their protection. Through hunger, an appeal to a biblical promise and an offer of power, Jesus continued to answer from what God had said.|Each offer invited Jesus to take something by abandoning obedience. His answers show that a verse must be read in keeping with what God has commanded.|How does Jesus' second reply help you recognise a misuse of Scripture?
4:12-25|The First Followers by the Lake|The call to follow Jesus|Jesus heard that John had been put in prison and withdrew into Galilee. He left Nazareth and settled in Capernaum beside the sea, in the region associated with Zebulun and Naphtali. Matthew remembers Isaiah's words about light coming to people who sat in darkness. Jesus began to preach, calling for repentance because the kingdom of heaven was near.~Walking beside the Sea of Galilee, Jesus saw two brothers, Simon Peter and Andrew. They were casting a net into the water because that was their work. He called them to follow him and said he would make them fishers of people. They left their nets at once and followed.~A little farther on he saw James and John, sons of Zebedee. They were in a boat with their father, mending their nets. Jesus called them as well. They left the boat and their father and followed him. Those first calls came in the middle of work the four men already knew how to do. The promise Jesus made would take them into work they still had to learn.~Jesus travelled throughout Galilee, teaching in synagogues, preaching the good news of the kingdom and healing diseases. People brought sufferers to him from as far away as Syria. Crowds followed from Galilee, the Decapolis, Jerusalem, Judea and beyond the Jordan. The men who had left their nets now accompanied Jesus as his teaching and healing drew people from many places.|The fishermen's response had an immediate cost. Jesus gave them a new purpose whose full meaning they would learn by accompanying him.|What did the four men leave, and what did Jesus say he would make of them?
5:1-16|The People Jesus Calls Blessed|A visible life of faith|When Jesus saw the crowds, he went up the mountain and sat down. His disciples came to him, and he began to teach. He called the poor in spirit blessed because the kingdom of heaven belonged to them. He spoke of people who mourn and will be comforted, of the meek who will inherit the earth, and of those who hunger for righteousness and will be filled.~Jesus continued with the merciful, the pure in heart and the peacemakers. He attached promises to those ways of life: mercy, sight of God and the name of God's children. He included people persecuted for righteousness and spoke directly to his hearers about what to remember when people reviled them because of him. The prophets before them had also faced persecution.~Then Jesus compared his hearers to salt. Salt that has lost its savour can no longer do its work. He gave a second picture, a city on a hill which cannot be hidden. People light a lamp to put it on a stand where it gives light to the house. Covering it with a measure would defeat the purpose of lighting it.~Jesus applied the lamp to the lives of his disciples. Their good works were to be visible enough that others would glorify their Father in heaven. The people he had called blessed were now asked to let their conduct be seen for what it showed about God.|The blessings correct the way we measure a good life. The light Jesus describes draws attention to the Father through the conduct of his people.|Which of the blessings would change the way you judge a difficult situation?
5:17-26|Before You Bring Your Gift|Reconciliation and the heart|After speaking of visible good works, Jesus addressed how his teaching related to the law and the prophets. He had come to fulfil them. He gave weight even to the smallest details and spoke of the responsibility of people who both do and teach God's commands. The righteousness of his hearers must go beyond that of the scribes and Pharisees.~Jesus began with the command against murder. He brought anger and words of contempt into the discussion. A person might never have struck a fatal blow and still need to answer for how he regarded and addressed another person. Jesus' examples take the hearer from an outward offence towards conduct that can help a quarrel grow.~He pictured someone bringing a gift to the altar who remembers a brother has something against him. The worshipper should leave the gift there and go first to be reconciled. Then he can return and make the offering. What he remembers at the altar gives him something to attend to before completing his act of worship.~Jesus gave a second picture of two people on their way to court. He urged agreement while they were still on the road, before the matter passed through the judge and the officer and ended in prison. Both examples identify a moment when a hearer still has room to act. One is the moment of remembering a grievance. The other is the remaining distance before reaching the judge.|Jesus connects worship with how we treat people. A serious attempt to make peace can reveal whether we have taken his words about anger to heart.|What unfinished matter would Jesus' picture of the worshipper bring to your attention?
5:27-37|Faithfulness in Desire and Speech|Integrity without loopholes|Jesus next took up the command against adultery. He spoke of the look that is directed towards lust and brought the heart into view. What another person could see of someone's conduct was only part of the matter. Jesus asked his hearers to reckon with what they were allowing themselves to desire.~He used severe images of an eye or a hand that causes a person to stumble. He pictured giving up something precious rather than letting it lead the whole person to ruin. The force of the illustration is in the seriousness of what is at stake. A person who sees a source of temptation has a decision to make about what place to give it.~Jesus then spoke of putting away a wife. His hearers knew the provision for a written notice of divorce. He addressed the consequences for a wife dismissed by her husband and the person who marries her, giving an exception involving sexual immorality. The act of writing a notice does not exhaust the question of responsibility.~He turned from marriage to the reliability of words. His hearers knew the duty to keep oaths made to the Lord. Jesus told them to avoid oaths that appealed to heaven, earth, Jerusalem or even one's own head. A person's yes or no should be dependable. The passage ends by putting truthfulness into the simplest words people use with one another.|Faithfulness reaches into the desires we entertain and the words others must rely on. Jesus calls for decisive action against habits that make unfaithfulness easier.|Where would a truthful yes or no be better than a promise dressed up to sound convincing?
5:38-48|When an Enemy Needs Your Prayer|Love patterned after the Father|Jesus recalled the rule about an eye for an eye and a tooth for a tooth. He then gave his hearers a series of situations in which they might feel entitled to answer in kind. He spoke of a person struck on the cheek, of a lawsuit over a garment and of someone compelled to go a mile. In each picture, he asked the hearer to consider a response beyond returning the injury or doing the minimum demanded.~He went on to speak of giving to someone who asks and of receiving a person who would borrow. These are ordinary occasions when another person's claim can feel like an interruption. The situations are different, but each places a choice in the hands of the person hearing Jesus' words.~Jesus then spoke of loving enemies and praying for persecutors. He pointed to the Father, who makes his sun rise on evil and good people and sends rain on just and unjust alike. The illustration locates the example for love in what the Father gives every day to people whose conduct differs.~He asked what was distinctive about loving only those who love us or greeting only those who belong to our own circle. Even people his hearers looked down on could do as much. The passage closes with the Father's perfection as the measure. Jesus has moved from a single blow or demand to the kind of love his followers are to practise towards people who oppose them.|Jesus calls disciples to act beyond the circle of people who repay kindness. Prayer for an enemy places that person's welfare before God and tests our desire for revenge.|Whom could you pray for by asking God for a specific good in that person's life?
6:1-18|What the Father Sees in Secret|Giving, prayer and fasting|Jesus asked his disciples to consider who they were trying to please when they gave, prayed or fasted. A person could make a show of giving and receive public praise for it. Jesus described an act of giving carried out so quietly that one hand did not know what the other was doing. The Father who sees in secret would see the gift.~He raised the same question about prayer. Some chose visible places where others could see them. Jesus directed his hearers to go into a room and shut the door. He also warned against piling up words under the assumption that numbers would make God listen. Their Father knew what they needed before they asked.~Then he gave them a prayer. It begins with the Father's name, his kingdom and his will. It asks for daily bread, forgiveness as the people praying forgive others, and deliverance from temptation and evil. After the prayer Jesus returned to forgiveness, giving it direct weight in how his hearers dealt with people who had wronged them.~When he turned to fasting, he described people making their faces look miserable so everyone would notice. His followers were to wash and care for their appearance as they usually would. Their fast could then remain a matter between them and the Father who sees in secret. The three instructions leave plenty for a disciple to do where there may be no audience at all.|The prayer Jesus gave orders our desires around the Father before naming everyday needs. A private practice of mercy or prayer can expose how much we depend on being noticed.|How might the order of the Lord's Prayer change what you ask for today?
6:19-34|Treasure and Tomorrow|Trusting the Father for daily needs|Jesus spoke about two places in which treasure could be stored. On earth it could be damaged by moth or rust, or taken by thieves. Treasure in heaven was beyond those losses. He joined the choice of treasure to the direction of the heart. He then spoke of the eye as a lamp for the body and warned about what happens when the light within is darkness.~He gave his hearers another picture: one servant trying to serve two masters. A divided attachment would show itself in how the servant loved and held to one while despising the other. Jesus applied it plainly to the service of God and the service of money.~The subject of money led into the needs that make people anxious. Jesus spoke of food, drink and clothing. The birds did not sow, reap or store food in barns, yet their heavenly Father fed them. The lilies did not labour or spin, yet Solomon in his glory was not clothed like one of them. The Father who clothed grass that lasted so briefly knew his people's needs.~Jesus told his hearers to seek first God's kingdom and righteousness. He brought the teaching back to the day they had in front of them. Tomorrow would have its own concerns, and today had enough trouble of its own. The passage moves from where people store their treasure to what they give first place when real needs come to mind.|Jesus connects anxiety about provision with what has first claim on the heart. Trust allows us to attend to today's duties while placing tomorrow before the Father.|What would seeking God's kingdom first change about one decision concerning money or time?
7:1-12|The Speck and the Beam|Honest self-examination and generous prayer|Jesus began with a warning about judging. The judgement and measure his hearers applied to others would be applied to them. Then he pictured someone noticing a speck in a brother's eye while failing to notice a beam in his own. The person with the beam even offered to remove the speck. Jesus told him to deal with the beam first so that he could see clearly enough to help his brother.~Jesus gave another warning about discernment. He pictured giving what was holy to dogs or throwing pearls before pigs. What was given could be trampled and the giver attacked. The image adds a different kind of care to the warning about judgement. The person offering help still has to pay attention to the situation.~Jesus then told his hearers to ask, seek and knock. He pictured a child asking a parent for bread or a fish. A parent would not answer by handing over a stone or a serpent. If parents who fell short in other ways knew how to give good gifts, their Father in heaven would give good things to those who asked him.~He drew the instruction towards daily conduct by telling them to do for others what they wanted others to do for them. He connected this with the law and the prophets. The listener has been asked to examine his own approach to correction, trust the Father's generosity and put himself in the place of another person.|Correction should begin with honest attention to our own conduct. The Father's generosity also shapes how we approach the needs of others.|What would removing the beam first change about a conversation you need to have?
7:13-29|Two Builders in the Storm|Hearing that becomes obedience|As Jesus brought the sermon towards its close, he spoke of two gates. The broad road was easy to enter and many went down it, but it ended in destruction. The narrow way led to life. He warned that false prophets could arrive looking like sheep while being dangerous within. Fruit would show what kind of tree stood before the hearer.~Jesus then spoke of people who would call him Lord and point to impressive deeds done in his name. He said that entering the kingdom belonged to those who did his Father's will. A name spoken publicly and deeds cited in one's own defence could leave the question of obedience unanswered.~He set two builders before the crowd. A wise man heard his sayings and acted on them, like a man building on rock. Rain came, streams rose and winds beat against the house. It stood because it had been founded on rock. A foolish man heard the same words and failed to act, like a builder who set his house on sand. The same forces beat on it, and its fall was great.~When Jesus finished speaking, the crowds were astonished at the way he taught. Matthew says they heard authority different from that of their scribes. The people had heard the same sermon together. The picture Jesus left them with concerned what each person would go on to do with what he had heard.|The storm exposes a difference that was already present in how the builders chose their foundations. Jesus links a sound foundation to hearing his words and doing them.|Which instruction from the Sermon on the Mount have you heard often enough to put into practice now?
8:1-17|A Man Reaches Out, a Soldier Asks|The authority and compassion of Jesus|When Jesus came down from the mountain, large crowds followed him. A man with leprosy approached and knelt before him. The man did not question whether Jesus had the power to cleanse him. He put his request in terms of whether Jesus was willing. Jesus stretched out his hand, touched him and said he was willing. The man was cleansed at once. Jesus told him to show himself to the priest and offer what Moses had commanded.~In Capernaum a centurion approached Jesus about a servant who lay at home paralysed and in great distress. Jesus said he would come and heal him. The officer said he was unworthy to have Jesus under his roof. He understood what an order could do: one soldier went when told to go and another came when told to come. He trusted Jesus to speak the word from where he stood.~Jesus marvelled and told those following him that he had not found such faith in Israel. He spoke of people coming from east and west to sit with Abraham, Isaac and Jacob, and warned about presuming on a place at the table. Then he sent the centurion on his way. The servant was healed that hour.~In Peter's house Jesus saw Peter's mother-in-law sick with fever. He touched her hand, the fever left and she rose and served him. In the evening people brought more sufferers. Matthew connects his care for them with Isaiah's words about bearing infirmities and sicknesses.|The man with leprosy appealed to Jesus' willingness. The centurion relied on the authority of his word. Both brought a real need before him.|What did the centurion understand about authority that shaped his request?
8:18-27|The Cost of Following and the Storm|Discipleship when safety is uncertain|When Jesus saw the crowds around him, he gave orders to cross to the other side of the lake. Before he set off, a scribe offered to follow him wherever he went. Jesus answered by speaking of foxes with holes and birds with nests, while the Son of Man had nowhere to lay his head. His reply gave the man a picture of what travelling with him could involve.~Another disciple asked for leave to go first and bury his father. Jesus told him to follow and leave the dead to bury their own dead. Matthew does not say how either man answered. The next people he describes are the disciples who followed Jesus into the boat.~A violent storm arose on the lake. Waves covered the boat, and Jesus was asleep. The disciples came to wake him and cried for the Lord to save them because they were perishing. He asked why they were so afraid and spoke of their little faith. Then he stood and rebuked the winds and the sea.~A great calm followed. The people in the boat marvelled and asked what sort of man this was, since even the winds and sea obeyed him. Their question carries the account beyond relief at reaching calm water. They had set out with the man who spoke of having nowhere to lay his head, and they were discovering an authority they had not expected.|The call to follow Jesus is given before the storm arrives. In the boat the disciples discover how much more they have to learn about the one they follow.|How do Jesus' words before the crossing prepare you to read what happened on the lake?
8:28-34|Two Men Among the Tombs|When deliverance unsettles a town|When Jesus reached the other side of the lake, two men came out of the tombs to meet him. They were so fierce that people could not pass along that road. Matthew brings the crossing straight into this encounter. The men cried out to Jesus as the Son of God and asked whether he had come to torment them before the appointed time.~Some distance away a herd of pigs was feeding. The demons begged Jesus to send them into the pigs if he drove them out. He told them to go. They left the men and entered the herd. The whole herd rushed down the steep bank into the sea and died in the water. The men who tended the pigs fled to the town.~In the town, the herdsmen reported what had happened, including what had happened to the men who had been possessed. Matthew keeps both parts of their report together. The herd's loss was plain, and the power that had held the two men had been driven out. People in the town received news of both before coming out to meet Jesus.~The whole town came out. When the people saw Jesus, they begged him to leave their district. Matthew ends there without telling us what the two men said afterwards or giving a reason for the townspeople's request. Their appeal to Jesus to go is the last word in the scene.|A community can witness a powerful act and still want distance from Jesus. 9:1-13|Forgiveness, Healing and a Place at the Table|Mercy for those who need it|Jesus crossed back by boat and came to his own town. People brought him a paralysed man lying on a bed. The man on the bed and the people at the table each give the reader a place to consider the mercy Jesus came to exercise.|Forgiveness addresses a need the people bringing the man may have overlooked. 9:14-17|A Question About Fasting|Recognising the time and the occasion|John's disciples came to Jesus with a question about fasting. They fasted, and so did the Pharisees. Why did Jesus' disciples go about their days without keeping the same practice? Matthew has just described Jesus eating with tax collectors and sinners. The question now concerns another difference that people could see in the conduct of those around him.~Jesus asked them to think of a wedding. As long as the bridegroom was with the wedding party, it would be out of place for his companions to mourn. The days would come when he was taken away from them. Then they would fast. Jesus gave fasting a place in the days to come and explained why the present moment called for a different response.~He gave another example from a familiar task. A patch of new cloth sewn onto an old garment could pull away and make the tear worse. Then he spoke of new wine in old wineskins. The skins would burst, the wine would spill and the skins would be ruined. New wine belonged in new skins so that both could be preserved.~John's disciples had asked why one company followed a practice that another did not. Jesus answered by placing himself in the picture. They needed to consider who was present and what that presence meant before deciding what his disciples should be doing.|Jesus explains a religious practice in light of his presence and the occasion. The question encourages us to consider whether our habits express a living response to him.|How does the bridegroom image explain why the disciples' conduct differed?
9:18-26|Interrupted on the Way to a Child|Faith when hope seems gone|While Jesus was speaking, a ruler came and knelt before him. His daughter had just died. 27:1-26|Silver Returned and a Prisoner Released|Responsibility under pressure|In the morning the chief priests and elders agreed to put Jesus to death. They bound him and delivered him to Pilate the governor. When Judas saw that Jesus had been condemned, he brought back the thirty pieces of silver and confessed that he had betrayed innocent blood. The chief priests and elders told him that was his concern. Judas threw the money down in the temple and left. He died by suicide.~The priests decided the silver could not go into the treasury because it was the price of blood. They used it to buy the potter's field as a burial place for strangers. Matthew relates this to words spoken through the prophet Jeremiah.~Before Pilate, Jesus was asked whether he was the King of the Jews. He answered that question, then made no reply to the accusations made against him. Pilate was amazed. At the feast the governor customarily released a prisoner of the crowd's choice. He offered them Barabbas or Jesus. While he sat on the judgement seat, his wife sent him a warning based on a troubling dream.~The chief priests and elders persuaded the crowds to ask for Barabbas and demand Jesus' death. Pilate asked what wrong Jesus had done, but the cries grew louder. Seeing a disturbance forming, he washed his hands in front of the crowd and declared himself innocent of the blood. Then he released Barabbas, had Jesus scourged and handed him over to be crucified.|The priests' reply to Judas and Pilate's gesture both attempt to put responsibility elsewhere. The sequence invites us to face what our decisions actually set in motion.|What does Pilate do after washing his hands, and why does the order of those actions matter?
27:27-44|The Mocked King on the Cross|Faithfulness through humiliation|The governor's soldiers took Jesus inside and gathered their company around him. They put a scarlet robe on him, made a crown of thorns and placed a reed in his right hand. Kneeling before him, they mocked him as King of the Jews. They spat on him and struck him. When they had finished, they put his own clothes back on him and led him away.~On the way they compelled Simon, a man from Cyrene, to carry the cross. At Golgotha, a place whose name Matthew explains as the place of a skull, they offered Jesus a drink that he tasted and refused. They crucified him and cast lots for his clothes. Above his head they set the charge calling him Jesus the King of the Jews. Two others were crucified with him, one on either side.~People passing by shook their heads and challenged him to save himself. They recalled words about the temple and told him to come down if he was God's Son. Chief priests, scribes and elders also mocked him. They said he had saved others but could not save himself, and demanded that he come down if they were to believe him. They spoke of his trust in God as another reason to taunt him.~Even the men crucified beside him joined in. The title the soldiers had used in mockery now stood above his head in writing, and different people below were using it to say what they thought he must do.|The mockers treated escape as the proof of who Jesus was. Matthew has already shown Jesus choosing the path that would bring him to this place.|How do the mockers' challenges sound in light of what Jesus prayed in Gethsemane?

28:16-20|The Last Meeting on the Mountain|Making disciples with Jesus' presence|The eleven disciples went into Galilee and came to the mountain Jesus had appointed. They had been sent there with a promise that they would see him. When they saw him, they worshipped him. Matthew also records that some doubted. This last scene includes the different responses of people who had come to the same place.~Jesus came to them and spoke first about his authority. All authority in heaven and on earth had been given to him. That was the basis on which he sent them out. They were to go and make disciples of all nations. The work was to reach people beyond the circle that had stood with him in Galilee.~He told them to baptise in the name of the Father, the Son and the Holy Spirit. He also told them to teach people to observe all he had commanded. Earlier in the Gospel these disciples had heard his teaching, seen his acts and at times been corrected by him. Now they were being given responsibility to teach others to practise what he had taught.~Jesus ended with a promise that he would be with them always, to the end of the age. Matthew began by naming Jesus Emmanuel, God with us. The Gospel closes with Jesus assuring the people he sends out that his presence will go with them as they do the work he gave them.|Jesus joins his authority to a command and a promise. Teaching people to practise what he commanded takes sustained attention, and the work rests on his continuing presence.|Which parts of Jesus' commission require a relationship that continues after a person first hears the message?
