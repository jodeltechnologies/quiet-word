1:1-11|A Charge to Stay in Ephesus|Teaching that produces love|Paul reminds Timothy why he asked him to remain in Ephesus. Some people were teaching differently and becoming absorbed in stories and genealogies that generated disputes. Paul says the aim of the charge is love from a pure heart, a good conscience and sincere faith. He explains that the law has a proper use in addressing conduct contrary to sound teaching.|A teacher can ask what a discussion is producing. Paul gives Timothy a measure that reaches the heart and the conduct of the people who listen.|What three things does Paul connect with the love his charge is meant to produce?
1:12-20|Mercy Given to a Former Persecutor|Remembering grace while defending the faith|Paul thanks Christ for strengthening and appointing him to serve. He recalls speaking against Christ and persecuting believers before receiving mercy. His life becomes an example of Christ's patience. After giving glory to God, Paul charges Timothy to hold faith and a good conscience and names two men whose course shows what can happen when these are put aside.|Paul speaks of receiving mercy before instructing Timothy about faithfulness. The truth he guards is the same truth that gave him a future after his former conduct.|What does Paul say his own experience shows to people who will later believe?
2:1-7|Prayer for People in Authority|Looking beyond familiar circles in prayer|Paul asks that petitions, prayers and thanksgivings be made for all people, including kings and others in authority. He connects that prayer with the possibility of a quiet and godly life. He says God desires people to be saved and to know the truth, and points to Christ Jesus as the one mediator who gave himself as a ransom.|Paul gives the congregation a wide field for prayer. A person in authority can be included even when the congregation's influence over that person's decisions is small.|How does Paul's description of Christ's work help explain the breadth of the prayer he requests?
2:8-15|Prayer, Modesty and Learning|Conduct fitted to worship|Paul directs men to pray without anger or quarrelling and urges women to give attention to modesty and good works. He calls for a woman to learn quietly and says he does not permit a woman to teach or exercise authority over a man. In making his case he refers to Adam and Eve. The passage closes with a reference to childbearing and continuance in faith, love, holiness and self-control.|Paul treats the manner of prayer, dress and learning as matters that shape gathered worship. The closing virtues deserve attention alongside the disputed details of the passage.|Which instructions in this passage address what worshippers bring into the gathering through their conduct?
3:1-16|The Character of Those Who Serve|Trustworthy conduct in God's household|Paul describes the qualifications for overseers and deacons. He gives weight to reputation, self-control, hospitality, the management of one's household and faithfulness in service. He explains that he is writing so Timothy will know how people should conduct themselves in God's household. The chapter closes with a confession concerning Christ and the mystery of godliness.|Paul asks the church to look across a person's life when entrusting responsibility. A visible gift needs the support of habits that other people can trust.|Which qualifications would be recognisable to people who know a prospective leader outside a church meeting?
4:1-16|Give Attention to Reading and Teaching|Training for godliness in public and private|Paul warns that some will turn towards teachings that forbid marriage and certain foods. He says God's gifts are to be received with thanksgiving. He tells Timothy to train himself for godliness, set an example in speech and life, devote himself to Scripture reading and teaching, and attend to the gift he has received. Timothy is to watch his life and his teaching together.|Paul gives a young leader several ways to become credible through conduct. Steady attention to Scripture and to his own life belongs to his care of others.|What two things does Paul tell Timothy to pay close attention to at the end of the chapter?
5:1-16|Care for Widows and Respect Within the Church|Making help dependable and responsible|Paul asks Timothy to approach older and younger people as family and to honour widows in real need. He distinguishes those who have relatives able to care for them from those left alone. He gives instructions for enrolling widows and asks families to shoulder responsibilities they can carry, so the church can assist those without support.|Care becomes dependable when people identify the needs and duties that are actually present. Paul gives a reason for asking households to take responsibility where they can.|How does Paul's instruction to households affect what the church is able to do for widows who are alone?
5:17-25|Honour, Evidence and Impartial Judgement|Careful decisions about leaders|Paul calls for honour for elders who lead well, especially those who labour in teaching. He requires witnesses before receiving a charge against an elder and asks Timothy to act without partiality. He warns against laying hands on someone hastily. After personal advice about Timothy's health, he observes that conduct sometimes becomes clear only with time.|Receiving an accusation carefully and treating people impartially are both necessary. The warning against haste gives Timothy time to learn what a first impression cannot show.|What safeguards does Paul place around receiving accusations and entrusting responsibility?
6:1-10|When Godliness Is Treated as Gain|Contentment that guards the heart|Paul addresses servants in relation to their masters and then warns about teachers whose arguments produce quarrels and whose interest in godliness is financial. He speaks of the gain of godliness with contentment and reminds readers what people bring into life and take from it. The desire to be rich can lead people into many troubles.|The passage asks what someone hopes to gain through religion. Contentment gives a person a basis for refusing a profitable course that damages faith and relationships.|What desires and outcomes does Paul connect with the pursuit of riches?
6:11-21|The Good Confession and the Open Hand|Keeping what has been entrusted|Paul tells Timothy to pursue righteousness, godliness, faith, love, endurance and gentleness. He recalls Timothy's confession and charges him before God and Christ to keep the command. The wealthy are to place hope in God, be rich in good works and share. Paul closes by telling Timothy to guard what has been entrusted to him.|Paul gives Timothy qualities to pursue and the wealthy a way to put their resources to work. Both forms of faithfulness are visible in choices made over time.|What does Paul tell people with wealth to do with it, and where are they to place their hope?
