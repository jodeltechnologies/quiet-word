32:1-34:35|The Calf, the Broken Tablets and the Name of the Lord|Moses intercedes after Israel breaks the covenant and asks God to go with the people|When Moses delays on the mountain, the people ask Aaron to make gods to go before them. Aaron collects gold earrings, fashions a calf and builds an altar. The people identify the image with the power that brought them from Egypt. Aaron announces a feast to the Lord. They offer sacrifices and sit down to eat and drink before rising to revel. The Lord tells Moses what has happened and speaks of consuming the stubborn people and making a nation from Moses. Moses pleads for them, recalling what Egypt would say and what the Lord swore to Abraham, Isaac and Israel. The Lord relents from the announced destruction. Moses goes down carrying the tablets written by God. Joshua hears noise and thinks of battle. Moses recognises singing.~Seeing the calf and dancing, Moses breaks the tablets at the foot of the mountain. He burns the calf, grinds it, scatters the powder on water and makes Israel drink. Aaron blames the people's conduct and tells the story as though the calf came out of the fire by itself. Moses calls those who are for the Lord, and the Levites gather. They carry out judgment in the camp and about three thousand die. The next day Moses tells the people he will go up and seek atonement. He asks the Lord to forgive them or blot him out of his book. The Lord says those who sinned will be blotted out, tells Moses to lead the people and sends a plague because they made the calf.~The Lord tells Israel to go on towards the promised land with an angel, but says he will not go in their midst lest he consume them. The people mourn and give up their ornaments. Moses pitches the tent of meeting outside the camp, where the cloud descends when he enters. The Lord speaks with him as a man speaks with a friend. Joshua remains at the tent. Moses asks to know God's ways and pleads that the Lord's presence must go with them, for that presence distinguishes Israel. God agrees. When Moses asks to see his glory, God promises to make his goodness pass before him and proclaim his name. Moses will be sheltered in a cleft, for no one can see his face and live.~Moses cuts two new tablets and ascends alone. The Lord descends and proclaims his name as merciful and gracious, slow to anger and rich in steadfast love and faithfulness, forgiving iniquity and also holding guilt to account. Moses bows and asks for pardon and God's presence. The Lord renews the covenant, warns Israel against alliances and idolatry and repeats duties concerning firstborn, Sabbath, feasts and offerings. Moses stays forty days and nights, and the covenant words are written on the tablets. When he comes down his face shines and the people are afraid. He tells them what God commanded and afterwards wears a veil, removing it when he goes before the Lord. The man who broke the first tablets returns with new ones and the visible mark of having stood in God's presence.|God's mercy gives grounds for confession and intercession when his people fail. His presence is worth seeking even when the outward benefits of the journey seem assured.|What would you ask God to restore if your chief concern were his presence rather than the outward success of your plans?
35:1-40:38|When the Glory Filled the Tent|Israel gives and builds, Moses examines the work, and the Lord makes his dwelling among the people|Moses gathers Israel and gives the Sabbath command before calling for materials for the dwelling. Men and women bring jewellery, cloth, skins, metals, wood, oil, spices and stones. Women spin yarn and goats' hair. Leaders bring stones and other materials for worship. The gifts come from people whose hearts move them to give, and the Lord has given Bezalel and Oholiab skill and the ability to teach. The craftsmen receive the gifts and eventually tell Moses the people have brought more than is needed. Moses has word sent through the camp to stop bringing offerings. The work goes on with sufficient materials and some left over. Israel has recently handed gold to Aaron for the calf. In this gathering people give their valuables to an undertaking whose pattern the Lord has supplied.~The craftsmen make the curtains, coverings, frames and bars, the inner veil and entrance screen. Bezalel makes the ark, its gold cover and cherubim, then the table and its vessels, the lampstand and lamps, and the altar for incense. He makes the anointing oil and incense. The bronze altar is made with its utensils, grating and poles. The bronze basin is made from mirrors offered by women serving at the entrance of the tent. The court is set out with its posts, hangings and screen. An accounting records the metals used, including silver from those counted in the congregation. Bezalel and Oholiab are named in connection with work whose result many people will use but whose pieces each had to be made and fitted by someone.~The garments of service and Aaron's garments are made. The ephod, engraved stones, breastpiece, robe with bells and pomegranates, tunics, turban, sashes and gold plate are completed. The workers bring the dwelling, its furnishings, the court equipment and the garments to Moses. He examines the work and sees that they made it as the Lord commanded. He blesses them. The Lord tells Moses to set up the tabernacle on the first day of the first month, place the ark behind the veil, arrange the table, lamps, altars and basin, and set up the court. He is to anoint the dwelling and furnishings, wash and clothe Aaron and his sons, and anoint them for priestly service.~In the second year Moses sets up the tent. He puts the testimony in the ark, hangs the veil, lays out the bread, lights the lamps, burns incense, makes offerings, sets water in the basin and raises the court. Moses and the priests wash before approaching the tent or altar. When Moses finishes the work, the cloud covers the tent and the glory of the Lord fills the tabernacle so that Moses cannot enter. When the cloud lifts, Israel sets out, and when it stays the people remain camped. Cloud by day and fire by night are visible to the household of Israel through its journeys. The book that opened with people forced to build cities for Pharaoh ends with a dwelling made by willing hands, where the Lord's presence directs when the whole camp moves.|God's presence is the purpose of the dwelling, and obedience gives place to many kinds of contribution. Faithful people can also learn to wait when God's direction is to remain where they are.|Which ability or possession could you offer gladly for work that serves others and honours God?
