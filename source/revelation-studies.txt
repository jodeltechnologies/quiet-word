1:1-20|A Voice Behind John on Patmos|The risen Christ stands among the churches and tells John to write|Revelation opens by tracing the message from God to Jesus Christ, through his angel to John, and from John to the servants who are to hear it. John bears witness to the word of God, to the testimony of Jesus Christ and to what he saw. The person who reads aloud and those who hear and keep the words are called blessed because the time is near. John writes to seven churches in Asia and greets them with grace and peace from him who is, who was and who is to come, from the seven Spirits before the throne, and from Jesus Christ. Jesus is the faithful witness, the firstborn from the dead and the ruler of the kings of the earth. He loves his people, frees them from their sins by his blood and makes them a kingdom and priests to God.~John announces that Jesus comes with clouds and every eye will see him, including those who pierced him. The tribes of the earth will mourn because of him. The Lord God declares himself the Alpha and the Omega, the one who is and was and is to come, the Almighty. John then tells his readers where the visions began. He calls himself their brother and companion in tribulation, in the kingdom and in patient endurance in Jesus. He was on Patmos because of the word of God and the testimony of Jesus. On the Lord's day he was in the Spirit and heard a loud voice behind him like a trumpet. He was told to write what he saw and send it to the seven named churches, from Ephesus to Laodicea.~When John turned, he saw seven golden lampstands. Among them stood one like a son of man, clothed in a long robe with a golden band. His hair was white, his eyes were like flame and his feet like glowing metal. His voice was like many waters. He held seven stars in his right hand, a sharp two-edged sword came from his mouth, and his face shone like the sun in full strength. John fell at his feet as though dead. The one whom he saw laid his right hand on him and told him to stop being afraid. He is the first and the last and the living one. He died and is alive for evermore and holds the keys of death and the grave.~Jesus tells John to write what he has seen, what is and what is to take place after this. He explains that the stars are the angels of the seven churches and the lampstands are the churches themselves. The churches that will soon hear his words already have a place in John's first vision. Jesus has appeared among them before he speaks to each one in turn. He has also placed on the frightened witness the same hand that holds the stars. John can write to people enduring pressure because the living Christ has met him on Patmos and has told him who holds the keys.|The risen Christ sees his churches and holds authority over death. His presence gives believers reason to listen and endure even when their circumstances make them afraid.|What changes about a present fear when you remember who stands among the churches and holds the keys of death?
2:1-3:22|What the Spirit Says to the Churches|Seven assemblies hear Christ speak to their labour, suffering, compromise and hope|Ephesus has worked hard, endured and tested people who falsely claimed to be apostles. Christ remembers all this, and he also says they have left their first love. They must remember where they fell, repent and do their first works. He commends their hatred of the deeds of the Nicolaitans and promises the one who overcomes access to the tree of life. To Smyrna he speaks as the one who died and came to life. He knows their affliction and poverty, though he calls them rich. He knows the slander against them and warns that some will be imprisoned and tested for ten days. Faithfulness even to death will receive the crown of life, and the second death will have no power to harm the one who overcomes.~Pergamum has held Christ's name even where Satan's throne is, and held it when Antipas was killed. Some, however, hold teaching like Balaam's, which enticed Israel into idolatry and immorality. Others hold the teaching of the Nicolaitans. Christ calls them to repent and promises hidden manna, a white stone and a new name to the one who overcomes. Thyatira has love, faith, service and endurance, with later works exceeding its first. Yet it tolerates the woman Christ calls Jezebel, who teaches his servants to commit immorality and eat what is sacrificed to idols. She had time to repent. Christ warns that he searches hearts and will repay according to works. Those who have refused her teaching are to hold fast until he comes. He promises the overcomer authority over the nations and the morning star.~Sardis has a reputation for being alive but is dead. Christ tells it to wake up, strengthen what remains and keep what it received. He will come like a thief if it fails to watch. A few have kept their garments clean. The one who overcomes will wear white, remain in the book of life and be acknowledged before the Father and his angels. In Philadelphia, Christ speaks as the holy and true one who holds David's key. He has set an open door before a church of little strength because it kept his word and did not deny his name. He promises to keep them in the coming hour of trial, tells them to hold what they have and promises the overcomer a lasting place in God's temple together with God's name, the name of the new Jerusalem and Christ's new name.~Laodicea says it is rich and needs nothing. Christ sees a poor, blind and naked church whose lukewarm condition makes him ready to spit it out. He counsels it to obtain gold refined in fire, white garments and salve for its eyes. He reproves and disciplines those he loves, so the church is to be earnest and repent. He stands at the door and knocks, promising to share a meal with anyone who hears and opens. The one who overcomes will sit with him on his throne, as he overcame and sat with his Father. Each church hears the call to listen to what the Spirit says to the churches. The words sent to one place were also for the rest to hear.|Christ knows the actual condition of each church. Lightning, voices and thunder come out from the throne. Seven burning lamps are identified as the seven Spirits of God, and a sea like glass lies before it. Four living creatures full of eyes are around the throne, with appearances like a lion, an ox, a human face and a flying eagle. Each has six wings.~The creatures never cease to declare the holiness of the Lord God Almighty, who was, is and is to come. Whenever they give glory and thanks, the elders fall down and cast their crowns before the throne. God is worthy to receive glory, honour and power because he created all things, and by his will they exist. John then sees a scroll in the right hand of the one on the throne, written on both sides and closed with seven seals. A mighty angel calls for someone worthy to open it. No one in heaven, on earth or under the earth is found able to open it or look into it, and John weeps. The worship around the throne has made plain whose world this is. The sealed scroll leaves John wondering who can bring its purpose forward.~One of the elders tells him to stop weeping. The Lion of Judah, the Root of David, has conquered and can open the scroll. John looks and sees a Lamb standing as though slain, amid the throne, creatures and elders. He has seven horns and seven eyes, which are the seven Spirits of God sent into all the earth. He takes the scroll from the hand of the one seated on the throne. The creatures and elders fall before him, holding harps and golden bowls of incense, which are the prayers of the saints. They sing that the Lamb is worthy because he was slain and purchased people for God from every tribe, language, people and nation. He made them a kingdom and priests, and they will reign on earth.~John hears angels in numbers beyond counting join the song. They ascribe power, riches, wisdom, strength, honour, glory and blessing to the Lamb who was slain. Then he hears every creature in heaven, on earth, under the earth and on the sea give blessing and honour to the one on the throne and to the Lamb for ever. The living creatures say Amen and the elders fall down in worship. John had wept because nobody could be found to open the scroll. The scene ends with heaven and earth addressing the one who has taken it. They call on the mountains to cover them from the face of the one on the throne and from the wrath of the Lamb. Hunger, thirst and scorching heat will trouble them no longer. The Lamb will shepherd them and lead them to springs of living water, and God will wipe every tear from their eyes. 8:1-9:21|When the Trumpets Sound|The prayers of the saints rise before God and successive warnings expose hardened hearts|When the Lamb opens the seventh seal, heaven falls silent for about half an hour. Seven angels before God receive seven trumpets. Another angel stands at the altar with a golden censer. He is given much incense to offer with the prayers of all the saints on the golden altar before the throne. The smoke rises before God from the angel's hand. Then the angel fills the censer with fire from the altar and throws it to the earth. Thunder, voices, lightning and an earthquake follow. The silence gives way to sound on earth. The prayers John already saw in the golden bowls remain in view as the angels prepare to sound their trumpets.~At the first trumpet, hail and fire mixed with blood fall on the earth. A third of the trees and all green grass are burned. At the second, something like a great burning mountain goes into the sea. A third of the sea becomes blood, a third of its living creatures die and a third of the ships are destroyed. At the third, a burning star called Wormwood falls on a third of the rivers and springs. Many die from the bitter waters. At the fourth, a third of the sun, moon and stars is struck so that part of both day and night loses its light. A flying messenger calls out three woes to those who live on earth because of the remaining trumpet blasts.~At the fifth trumpet John sees a star fallen to earth, given the key to the bottomless pit. Smoke rises from it and darkens the air. Locusts come out with power like scorpions, forbidden to harm vegetation and permitted to torment only people without God's seal. Their torment lasts five months and people seek death without finding it. John describes the locusts by the appearance of horses prepared for battle, crowns like gold, human faces, women's hair, lions' teeth, iron breastplates, noisy wings and scorpion tails. Their king is the angel of the pit, named Abaddon in Hebrew and Apollyon in Greek. One woe has passed and two remain.~At the sixth trumpet a voice from the golden altar tells the angel to release four angels bound at the Euphrates. They have been kept for the hour, day, month and year when a third of humanity will be killed. John hears the number of horsemen and sees horses with heads like lions and tails like serpents. Fire, smoke and sulphur from their mouths kill a third of humanity. The account ends with the conduct of those who survive. They continue worshipping demons and idols made of materials that can neither see nor hear nor walk, and they refuse to repent of murders, sorceries, sexual immorality and thefts. The vision also names the practices and refusals whose end is the second death, including unbelief, murder, idolatry and falsehood.~An angel who carried one of the bowls takes John in the Spirit to a great mountain and shows him the bride, the Lamb's wife, as the city descending with God's glory. Its wall has twelve gates with the names of Israel's tribes and twelve foundations with the names of the Lamb's apostles. The angel measures the city with a golden reed. Its length, width and height are equal. The wall is jasper, the city pure gold like glass, the foundations adorned with precious stones and the gates each made of a pearl. John sees no temple there because the Lord God Almighty and the Lamb are its temple. It needs neither sun nor moon because God's glory lights it and the Lamb is its lamp. The nations walk in its light and bring their glory into it. Its gates stay open and night is absent, yet nothing unclean enters. Those who enter are written in the Lamb's book of life.~The angel shows John a clear river of the water of life flowing from the throne of God and the Lamb. The tree of life bears twelve kinds of fruit, yielding each month, and its leaves are for the healing of the nations. The curse is gone. God's servants serve him, see his face and bear his name on their foreheads. They need no lamp or sunlight, for the Lord gives them light, and they reign for ever. The angel declares the words faithful and true. Jesus announces that he is coming soon and calls the person who keeps the words of the prophecy blessed. John again falls before the angel and is told to worship God. He is told to leave the prophecy unsealed because the time is near.~Jesus says he is coming with his reward, to repay each according to their work. He calls himself the beginning and the end, the Root and Offspring of David and the bright morning star. Those with a right to the tree of life enter by the gates, while those who love and practise falsehood and the other evils named remain outside. The Spirit and the bride say to come, the hearer is to repeat the invitation, and the thirsty may take the water of life freely. John warns against adding to or taking away from the prophecy. Jesus repeats his promise to come quickly. John answers with his own prayer for the Lord Jesus to come, and the book closes with grace. The home John was shown gives his readers something to ask for and something to keep faith with while they wait.|God's final promise is to dwell with his people. The invitation to receive life and the call to keep Christ's words belong together as believers wait for his coming.|How does the promise of seeing God's face shape what you long for and what you choose to do while you wait?
