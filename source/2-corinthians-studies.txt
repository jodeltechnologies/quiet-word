1:1-11|Comfort Received and Passed On|Learning dependence in trouble|Paul greets the church and blesses the Father of mercies. He speaks of receiving comfort in affliction so that he can comfort others. He remembers a distress in Asia that felt beyond his strength. It taught him to rely on God who raises the dead. He asks the church to share in the work through prayer.|Paul gives the people a glimpse of trouble he could not manage alone. The comfort he received becomes something he can pass on to others.|What does Paul say his distress taught him about where to place his confidence?
1:12-2:11|A Changed Visit and a Call to Forgive|Correction that seeks restoration|Paul explains that he changed his plans to spare the Corinthians another painful visit. He recalls writing in tears so they might know his love. Speaking of someone who had caused sorrow, Paul says the punishment by the majority is sufficient. He urges the church to forgive, comfort and confirm its love for the person.|Paul is concerned with the damage that can follow once correction has done its work. Restoration includes words and actions that assure a person of the community's love.|Why does Paul say the congregation should comfort the person it had corrected?
2:12-3:6|An Open Door and a Letter on Hearts|God makes people sufficient for service|Paul finds an open door at Troas but has no rest because he has not found Titus. He goes on to Macedonia. He describes God spreading the knowledge of Christ through his servants. The Corinthians themselves are a letter written by the Spirit. Paul locates his capacity for the ministry of the new covenant in God.|Paul's concern for Titus is part of the life in which he serves. His confidence about his work rests on what God does in people.|Why does Paul call the Corinthians a letter, and who has written it?
3:7-18|The Veil Lifted|Transformation in the Lord's presence|Paul recalls the glory on Moses' face and compares it with the glory of the Spirit's ministry. He speaks of a veil when the old covenant is read and of its removal when a person turns to the Lord. With unveiled faces believers behold the Lord's glory and are changed into his likeness by the Spirit.|The removal of the veil makes possible a way of seeing that leads to change. Paul connects transformation with turning to the Lord and the work of the Spirit.|What does Paul say happens when a person turns to the Lord?
4:1-18|Treasure in Earthen Vessels|Light and hope carried in weakness|Paul speaks of proclaiming Jesus Christ as Lord with openness and describes God shining in hearts. He compares the treasure to something carried in fragile jars. He lists pressures that have not destroyed the workers and connects their suffering with the life of Jesus becoming visible. He fixes his attention on what endures beyond what can be seen.|Paul lets his hearers see both the treasure and the fragility of those carrying it. Weakness gives room for the source of the power to become evident.|Why is the image of an earthen vessel fitting for the hardships Paul describes?
5:1-21|A Tent, a Home and a Message of Reconciliation|Living for the One who died and rose|Paul compares the present body to a tent and speaks of a dwelling from God. He longs for life to swallow up mortality and aims to please the Lord as all will appear before Christ's judgement seat. Christ's love presses him towards a new way of regarding people. God reconciles through Christ and entrusts his servants with the message of reconciliation.|Paul's future hope gives direction to present conduct. A person who has received reconciliation can bring its invitation to others.|How does Paul connect Christ's death and resurrection with the purpose for which people now live?
6:1-7:1|An Open Heart and a Call to Holiness|Integrity under hardship and pressure|Paul urges the church to receive grace with a present response. He describes ministry through hardship, patience, truth and apparently contradictory experiences such as sorrow with joy. He asks the Corinthians to open their hearts. Then he calls for care about binding alliances and recalls God's promise to dwell among his people. On the strength of those promises he calls them to pursue holiness.|The apostle asks for relational openness while taking loyalty to God seriously. Both concerns are responses to the God who has drawn near.|What qualities does Paul put beside the hardships he lists as marks of his ministry?
7:2-16|Titus Brings News From Corinth|Sorrow that leads to a changed response|Paul asks the Corinthians to receive him and recalls his concern in Macedonia. Titus arrives and brings comfort with news of their response. Paul had grieved over causing pain by his letter. He now rejoices that their sorrow led to repentance and describes the earnestness it produced. Titus himself is encouraged by their reception.|The sorrow is judged by what it produces. Paul also notices the comfort brought by an honest report of a relationship being repaired.|What outcomes does Paul name when he describes the Corinthians' sorrow?
8:1-24|The Macedonians Ask to Take Part|Giving willingly and handling gifts honestly|Paul tells how churches in Macedonia gave generously amid hardship and begged to share in the relief work. He urges Corinth to complete what it had begun and recalls Christ's grace. He explains giving according to what one has and sends approved companions with Titus so the gift will be handled honourably before God and people.|Paul pairs willingness to give with careful handling of the money. Generosity is strengthened when others can see that it is being administered honestly.|Why does Paul arrange for more than one trusted person to accompany the collection?
9:1-15|A Gift Ready Before the Visitors Arrive|Cheerful generosity and thanksgiving|Paul sends people ahead so the promised gift will be ready. He compares giving to sowing and asks each person to give as decided in the heart. God supplies what is needed for good work. Beyond meeting needs, the gift will lead people to give thanks and pray for those who gave.|Advance preparation can make room for a gift given freely. Paul sees the result in both material provision and gratitude to God.|What effects does Paul expect this gift to have after the recipients receive it?
10:1-18|Letters, Presence and the Limits of Boasting|Letting the Lord decide what is commendable|Paul answers people who call him bold in letters and weak in person. He describes the character of his conflict and says his conduct will match what he writes. He challenges people who measure themselves by one another and describes the sphere of work God has given him. Commendation comes from the Lord.|Comparing ourselves with a circle of admirers can make almost any claim seem justified. Paul directs attention to the work actually entrusted to him and to the Lord's judgement.|What different standards of commendation does Paul put before the Corinthians?
11:1-15|A Different Message and Impressive Messengers|Discernment when a speaker looks convincing|Paul fears the Corinthians will be drawn away from devotion to Christ. He names the danger of receiving another Jesus, spirit or gospel. He explains why he preached without charge in Corinth and warns that deceptive workers can present themselves attractively, recalling that even Satan can appear as an angel of light.|An impressive appearance is one thing a hearer can see. Paul asks the church to attend to the message and to the direction in which it moves their loyalty.|What three things does Paul ask the Corinthians to notice if the message they receive has changed?
11:16-33|The Experiences Paul Would Rather Mention|Strength measured by care in hardship|Paul reluctantly answers boasting by listing hardships: labour, imprisonment, beatings, journeys, dangers and hunger. He adds the daily concern he carries for the churches. If he must boast, he will speak of things that show weakness. He recalls escaping Damascus by being lowered in a basket through an opening in the wall.|Paul ends with an escape that depended on help and offers little occasion for display. His concern for the churches remains a daily pressure within the list.|Why is the escape in a basket a fitting end to this kind of boasting?
12:1-21|The Thorn and the Answer Paul Receives|Receiving grace in weakness|Paul describes a man in Christ receiving extraordinary revelations, then speaks of his own thorn in the flesh. Three times he asks for it to depart. The Lord answers that his grace is sufficient and his power is made perfect in weakness. Paul later returns to his concern for the Corinthians and his fear of finding unrepented sins when he visits.|The Lord answers Paul's repeated prayer with a promise about the grace available to him. Paul's concern for the church continues while he lives with the weakness.|What does Paul ask to be removed, and what assurance does he receive?
13:1-14|Before the Third Visit|Examining yourself and seeking restoration|Paul says he is coming again and that matters will be established by witnesses. The Corinthians seek proof that Christ speaks through him, and he asks them to examine themselves. He prays that they may do what is right, even if he appears weak. He closes with calls to restoration and peace and a blessing.|Paul's authority has an end: building people up. His prayer gives their good conduct priority over winning the contest about his reputation.|What does Paul ask the Corinthians to examine while they are asking him for proof?
