1:1-14|News Brought by Epaphras|A growing life that gives thanks|Paul and Timothy greet the Colossians. Paul gives thanks for their faith and love and recalls how they learned the gospel from Epaphras. He prays that they will know God's will, bear fruit, endure patiently and thank the Father who rescued them from darkness and brought them into his Son's kingdom.|The prayer joins knowledge to a way of walking. Paul expects understanding to show up in fruitful work, endurance and gratitude.|What does Paul pray their knowledge of God's will will produce?
1:15-29|Christ Holds All Things Together|Remaining grounded in reconciliation|Paul describes Christ as the image of the invisible God, the one through whom all things were created and the head of the church. Through his death God reconciles those once alienated. Paul calls them to remain grounded in faith. He describes his own work of making known Christ among them and presenting people mature in him.|The greatness of Christ gives weight to the peace he makes. Paul's labour seeks a settled maturity in the people who have received that peace.|How does Paul's description of Christ's place in creation and in the church shape what he says about reconciliation?
2:1-15|Rooted in the One They Received|Living from Christ's sufficiency|Paul wants believers to be knit together in love and attain a settled knowledge of Christ. He urges them to walk in the one they received and warns against deceptive teaching. Fullness dwells in Christ. Paul speaks of believers being buried and raised with him, forgiven and released from the record that stood against them.|Paul measures new teaching against what believers already have in Christ. Their roots are to grow deeper in him while their gratitude grows.|What reasons does Paul give for remaining rooted in Christ?
2:16-3:4|Shadows and the Life Hidden With Christ|Choosing the direction of your attention|Paul tells believers not to let others judge them over food, drink or religious days. He warns against practices that look wise while drawing people away from the head. Having died and risen with Christ, they are to seek what is above. Their life is hidden with Christ and will be revealed when he appears.|Paul answers pressure from religious regulations with a clearer account of where believers' lives are held. That belonging gives shape to what they seek.|What does Paul say is hidden now, and what will happen when Christ appears?
3:5-17|Getting Dressed for a Changed Life|Love binds the new habits together|Paul names destructive desires and practices to put away, including greed, anger and lying. He then describes what to put on as God's beloved people: compassion, humility, patience, forgiveness and love. He calls them to let Christ's peace rule, let his word dwell among them and do everything with thanksgiving.|Paul's clothing image helps people name practical habits to take up each day. Forgiveness and truthful speech give the change a form other people can recognise.|Which instruction in this passage would change the way you respond to an irritation at home or at work?
3:18-4:6|The Lord of the Home and the Open Door|Doing your work with accountability and grace|Paul addresses relationships between spouses, parents and children, and servants and masters. Those in power have duties, and everyone ultimately answers to the Lord. He asks the church to continue in prayer and to pray for a door for the word. He urges wisdom towards outsiders and speech seasoned with grace.|The Lord's authority reaches the way people carry out everyday duties and the way they speak outside the fellowship. Prayer draws both concerns into their shared life.|What does Paul request for his own witness, and what way of speaking does he ask of them?
4:7-18|Letters and Greetings Travelling Together|Remembering people whose work is easily missed|Tychicus and Onesimus will bring news. Paul names fellow prisoners, workers, hosts and messengers, and says Epaphras wrestles in prayer for the believers' maturity. He asks that letters be exchanged between churches and tells Archippus to attend to his ministry. Paul closes by asking them to remember his chains.|The list shows how letters, hospitality, prayer and personal news sustain churches at a distance. Paul's brief instruction to Archippus places a particular responsibility in his hands.|What does Epaphras pray for, and what does Paul ask Archippus to attend to?
