1:1-7|Paul Begins With the Good He Has Heard|Recognising love before making an appeal|Paul greets Philemon, Apphia, Archippus and the church meeting in the house. He gives thanks for Philemon's love and faith and prays that their fellowship will become effective as they recognise the good they have in Christ. He says the hearts of the saints have been refreshed through Philemon.|Paul names evidence of grace before he raises a difficult matter. His thanksgiving is specific enough to show that his appeal takes Philemon's earlier conduct seriously.|What does Paul say Philemon has already done for fellow believers?
1:8-25|Receive Onesimus as You Would Receive Me|Love changes how a person is welcomed|From imprisonment Paul appeals for Onesimus, who has become dear to him. He sends him back and says he wanted Philemon's good deed to be willing. He asks Philemon to receive Onesimus as more than a slave, as a beloved brother, and to welcome him as he would Paul. If there is wrong or debt, Paul offers to take it on himself. He closes with greetings and plans for a possible visit.|Paul puts his relationship with both men to work in the appeal. Calling Onesimus a brother gives Philemon a concrete way to reconsider how he will receive him.|What does Paul ask Philemon to do if Onesimus owes him anything, and what kind of welcome does he request?
