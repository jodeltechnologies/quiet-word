1:1-2:23|The Road Home and the Field of Boaz|Ruth binds herself to Naomi and finds room to work among the harvesters|When the judges ruled, famine leads Elimelech and Naomi to leave Bethlehem with their sons Mahlon and Chilion and live in Moab. Elimelech dies there. The sons marry Moabite women, Orpah and Ruth, and after about ten years both sons die. Naomi hears that the Lord has visited his people and given them food. She sets out for Judah with her daughters-in-law, then urges them to return to their mothers' homes. She asks the Lord to show them the kindness they have shown her and the dead and to give them rest in new households. They weep. Naomi asks what future she can offer them. She has no sons for them to marry and tells them that the Lord's hand has gone out against her.~Orpah kisses her goodbye, but Ruth clings to her. She asks Naomi to stop pressing her to leave. Where Naomi goes and lodges, Ruth will go and lodge. Naomi's people and God will be hers, and she binds herself by an oath that reaches to death and burial. Naomi ceases urging her. When they arrive in Bethlehem the town is stirred and the women ask whether this is Naomi. She tells them to call her Mara because the Almighty has dealt bitterly with her. She went out full and says the Lord has brought her home empty. Ruth is there beside her, and they arrive at the beginning of barley harvest. The account leaves Naomi free to speak of what she has lost even as it names the woman who came with her.~Ruth asks to glean where she finds favour. She comes to a field belonging to Boaz, a relative of Elimelech. Boaz greets the reapers in the Lord's name, asks whose young woman Ruth is and hears she came from Moab with Naomi and has worked since morning. He tells Ruth to stay with his women and drink from the vessels the young men fill. He has ordered the men not to mistreat her. Ruth bows and asks why he has noticed a foreigner. He says he has heard what she did for Naomi and prays that the Lord, under whose wings she has come for refuge, will reward her. At the meal he gives her roasted grain. She eats, is satisfied and has some left.~Boaz tells the reapers to let her glean among the sheaves and pull out grain for her without rebuke. Ruth works until evening, beats out about an ephah of barley and brings it to Naomi along with the food she saved. Naomi asks where she worked and blesses the man who noticed her. When Ruth names Boaz, Naomi blesses the Lord for kindness to the living and the dead and explains that he is a near relative with the right of redemption. She advises Ruth to stay with his women where she will be safe. Ruth gleans through the barley and wheat harvests while living with Naomi. The two women who returned with no assured livelihood now have food and a place to seek it again tomorrow.|Loyalty has practical costs, and kindness supplies practical protection. Boaz gives Ruth room to work safely and to carry food home to Naomi.|Who needs an opportunity to provide for themselves, and what could you do to make that opportunity safe and accessible?
3:1-4:22|At the Threshing Floor and the City Gate|Ruth asks for redemption, Boaz honours the nearer relative's claim and Naomi receives a child|Naomi seeks a secure home for Ruth and tells her that Boaz will winnow barley at the threshing floor that night. Ruth is to wash, anoint herself and go down after he has eaten, notice where he lies and uncover his feet. Ruth follows the instructions. At midnight Boaz is startled and finds a woman lying at his feet. Ruth names herself and asks him to spread his garment over her because he is a family redeemer. The man who prayed that she would find refuge under the Lord's wings now hears her ask him to take responsibility for giving her a place in his household.~Boaz blesses Ruth and praises the kindness in her request, since she has not gone after younger men, poor or rich. The townspeople know her as a woman of worth. He agrees to act, but tells her that another man is a nearer relative. That man must have the first opportunity. Boaz swears that if the other man declines he will redeem her. Ruth rises before people can recognise one another. Boaz gives her six measures of barley to take to Naomi so she will not return empty-handed. Naomi hears what happened and tells Ruth to wait, for Boaz will settle the matter that day. Ruth has spoken plainly, and now Boaz must make good on his word in public.~At the city gate Boaz calls the nearer relative aside and gathers ten elders as witnesses. He tells the man about the parcel of land belonging to Elimelech's family that Naomi is putting up for sale. The relative says he will redeem it. Boaz then explains that acquiring the field involves acquiring Ruth, Mahlon's widow, to preserve the dead man's name with his inheritance. The man declines because he fears impairing his own inheritance. He removes his sandal to confirm the transfer according to the custom of the time. Boaz calls the elders and people to witness that he has acquired the family property and taken Ruth as his wife so Mahlon's name will be remembered. They bless Ruth with the memory of Rachel and Leah and ask that the house be like that of Perez.~Ruth becomes Boaz's wife and the Lord gives her conception. She bears a son. The women of Bethlehem bless the Lord and tell Naomi that this child will restore her life and sustain her in old age. Ruth, who loves her, is worth more to her than seven sons. Naomi takes the child to her breast and cares for him. The neighbours call him Obed. He becomes the father of Jesse, the father of David, and the book closes by tracing the family from Perez to David. Bethlehem first heard Naomi say she had come home empty. The final scene lets her hold the child while the women name the love that came home with her all along.|Faithfulness can require acting openly and honouring another person's claim even when a desired outcome is at stake. God can carry kindness further than the people who first give it will live to see.|What private promise or intention needs a clear, public act of follow-through?
