1:1-9|The Work Left to Titus in Crete|Choosing people whose lives support their teaching|Paul greets Titus and reminds him why he left him in Crete: to put unfinished matters in order and appoint elders in each town. He describes their household conduct, self-control, hospitality and love of what is good. They must hold firmly to the trustworthy message so they can encourage with sound teaching and answer those who oppose it.|Paul links the authority to correct others with qualities visible in a person's daily life. The same person must be able to welcome, teach and exercise restraint.|Which qualities concern an elder's conduct, and which concern the handling of teaching?
1:10-16|When Teaching Upsets Households|Correction with the hope of sound faith|Paul says some people in Crete are misleading households for gain. He quotes a severe local saying and calls on Titus to rebuke the people concerned so they may become sound in faith. He warns about fables and human commands and observes that people can profess to know God while denying him by their deeds.|The stated aim of correction is soundness in faith. Paul judges a profession by what a person actually does and notices who is affected when teaching goes wrong.|What harm was spreading through the households, and what result did Paul seek through correction?
2:1-15|Grace Teaches People How to Live|Making sound teaching visible|Paul tells Titus to teach what fits sound doctrine and addresses older and younger people in different responsibilities. Titus must model good work and sound speech. Paul asks servants to show trustworthiness. He then explains that God's grace has appeared, training people to live with self-control and godliness as they await Christ and become eager for good works.|Paul describes grace as a teacher of daily conduct. The ordinary duties listed earlier are places where people can see what that teaching produces.|What does Paul say grace has appeared to teach people to leave behind and to practise?
3:1-8|What God Did When We Were Foolish|Mercy becomes a reason for gentleness|Paul asks believers to be ready for good work, considerate in public life and gentle towards others. He reminds them of their own former foolishness and hostility. God saved them through his mercy, renewal by the Holy Spirit and grace through Jesus Christ. Paul asks those who believe God to be careful to devote themselves to good works.|Remembering what mercy found in us can change how we speak to others. Paul places gentleness beside a clear account of the grace believers received.|How does remembering what believers once were support Paul's instruction about treating others?
3:9-15|Leaving Unfruitful Disputes and Meeting Real Needs|Giving useful work your attention|Paul tells Titus to avoid quarrels and arguments that accomplish little. He gives instructions for dealing with a divisive person after repeated warnings. Then he turns to travel plans and asks Titus to help Zenas and Apollos on their way. Believers should learn to maintain good works that meet pressing needs.|The closing turns quickly from unproductive arguments to named people with practical needs. Paul gives Titus a choice about where to spend time and effort.|What help does Paul want Titus to give Zenas and Apollos for their journey?
