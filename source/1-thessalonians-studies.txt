1:1-10|From Idols to the Living God|A changed life becomes a public witness|Paul, Silvanus and Timothy remember the Thessalonians' work of faith, labour of love and endurance of hope. The church received the word amid affliction with joy from the Spirit. Its faith became known in other places. People report how the believers turned from idols to serve God and wait for Jesus.|Paul describes conversion through a new service and a new hope. Both became visible beyond the congregation itself.|What changes did people report about the Thessalonians after they received the message?
2:1-12|The Manner in Which They Came|Gentle care and a life worth imitating|Paul recalls coming after mistreatment at Philippi and speaking with boldness. He denies using flattery or seeking advantage. He compares his care to a nursing mother and to a father encouraging his children. He reminds them that he worked to avoid burdening them and shared his life as well as the gospel.|The images of family give Paul's bold preaching a personal setting. His conduct lets the hearers examine how he cared for them while he spoke.|What details show how Paul sought to avoid taking advantage of those he taught?
2:13-3:13|Timothy Returns With Good News|Strengthening faith during separation|Paul gives thanks that the church received the message as God's word despite suffering. Unable to return, he sends Timothy to strengthen the believers and learn how they are faring. Timothy brings news of their faith and love. Paul is comforted and prays that they will increase in love and be established in holiness.|The separation leads to a practical act of care. Timothy's report then gives Paul fresh grounds for gratitude and prayer.|Why did Paul send Timothy, and what about his report encouraged him?
4:1-12|Holiness and a Quiet, Responsible Life|Respecting others in conduct and work|Paul urges the believers to grow in the way they live before God. He speaks about sexual holiness and warns against wronging another person. He commends their love for fellow believers and calls them to grow in it. He urges them to live quietly, attend to their responsibilities and work with their hands.|Paul joins holiness to the treatment of other people. Daily work and respect for others help give their witness credibility outside the church.|Which ordinary habits does Paul name when he speaks of their conduct towards outsiders?
4:13-18|Grieving With Hope|Comfort grounded in resurrection|Paul addresses concern for believers who have died. Because Jesus died and rose, those who have died in him are included in the hope of his coming. He says the dead in Christ will rise, and those alive will be gathered with them to meet the Lord. He tells the church to comfort one another with these words.|Paul takes grief seriously and gives it a source of hope. The teaching is given to a community that can speak comfort to people who mourn.|What assurance does Paul give about the place of believers who have already died?
5:1-11|Children of the Day|Readiness through faith, love and hope|Paul says the day of the Lord comes unexpectedly, like a thief at night. He calls the believers children of light and urges alertness and sobriety. Faith and love are pictured as a breastplate and hope of salvation as a helmet. He directs them to continue encouraging and building one another up.|Readiness has a communal shape. Paul gives faith, love and hope work to do while people wait for a day whose timing they cannot control.|How does Paul's description of readiness affect the way believers should treat one another?
5:12-28|Helping People According to Their Need|Patient care, prayer and discernment|Paul asks the church to respect those who work among them and be at peace. He distinguishes between warning the unruly, encouraging the fainthearted and supporting the weak. He calls for patience with everyone. He ends with instructions about prayer, thanksgiving and weighing what is heard, and prays for God to sanctify them wholly.|Patient care pays attention to what a person actually needs. Paul's final prayer puts the church's growth in the hands of the God who calls them.|How do the different instructions in verse 14 help you respond wisely to people in different situations?
