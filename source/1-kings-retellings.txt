21:1-29|Naboth's Vineyard and the Letters with the King's Seal|A ruler's desire becomes another man's death|Naboth owned a vineyard beside Ahab's palace at Jezreel. The king wanted it for a vegetable garden and offered a better vineyard or payment. Naboth refused because it was his ancestral inheritance before the Lord. Ahab returned home resentful, turned his face away and would not eat. Jezebel asked why he was distressed. When he explained, she challenged his exercise of kingship and promised to obtain the vineyard. The account begins with a boundary that a neighbour's conviction has placed before a ruler's desire. Ahab has heard the refusal, but his response makes that neighbour's property into a grievance within the palace.~Jezebel wrote letters in Ahab's name, sealed them with his seal and sent them to Naboth's elders and nobles. They were to proclaim a fast, place Naboth prominently and arrange two witnesses to accuse him of cursing God and the king. Then he was to be stoned. The local leaders obeyed. False witnesses gave the accusation publicly, and Naboth was taken outside and killed. The officials reported his death to Jezebel. She told Ahab to take possession, and he went down to the vineyard. Sacred language, civic office, legal appearance and royal authority had all been used to remove an innocent owner. The account lets the reader see the arrangement hidden behind the resulting possession.~The Lord sent Elijah to meet Ahab there. The message asked whether he had killed and also taken possession. It announced that dogs would lick his blood as they had Naboth's. Ahab called Elijah his enemy. The prophet answered that he had found him because he had sold himself to do evil. Judgment would remove his house like those of Jeroboam and Baasha, and Jezebel herself would meet a shameful death. The narrative underscores the evil of Ahab's idolatry and Jezebel's influence while keeping him responsible for what had been done through his name and for his benefit. Standing in the acquired vineyard did not separate him from the murder that had made it available.~When Ahab heard, he tore his clothes, put on sackcloth, fasted and moved in a humbled manner. God directed Elijah's attention to that response and said the full disaster upon the dynasty would come in his son's days rather than his own. The chapter ends with a merciful delay, while Naboth remains dead and the announced judgment remains real. Ahab's humbling is acknowledged without calling the earlier transaction innocent. The prophet had met him at the very place where desire had become possession. That meeting exposed a ruler's responsibility beneath the actions of queen, elders and witnesses, and it brought the Lord's word into a scene the king had expected to enjoy as a private gain.|Coveting can draw institutions and other people into grave injustice. He answered that he would speak what the Lord told him. At first he repeated the favourable formula, but when pressed for truth he described Israel scattered like sheep without a shepherd. Ahab immediately treated the warning as proof of the prophet's hostility.~Micaiah then told of a heavenly council in which a lying spirit would lead Ahab through his prophets toward defeat. He openly identified the assurances being heard as part of that judgment. Zedekiah struck him and mocked his claim to speak by the Spirit. Ahab ordered him imprisoned upon meagre food until the king returned safely. Micaiah answered that a safe return would prove the Lord had not spoken through him and called the people to witness. The warning was therefore delivered plainly before the campaign. The king chose confinement for its bearer while continuing toward the battle whose danger had just been named.~Ahab disguised himself, leaving Jehoshaphat in royal clothing.
