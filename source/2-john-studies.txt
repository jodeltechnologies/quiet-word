1:1-13|Truth, Love and the Open Door|The elder's letter on walking faithfully and receiving teachers wisely|The elder writes to the elect lady and her children, whom he loves in truth. He adds that this love is shared by all who know the truth because it abides in them and will remain with them forever. Grace, mercy and peace come from God the Father and from Jesus Christ, the Father's Son, in truth and love. The opening tells the readers what holds their relationship together before it asks them to make a difficult decision. Their affection rests on a truth that both writer and readers have received, and the greeting invokes mercy and peace for people who will need sound judgment.~The elder had found some of the lady's children walking in truth, as the Father had commanded, and it had given him great joy. He asks the lady to love others in keeping with the command they had from the beginning. He explains love by pointing to a way of walking, living according to God's commands. The language puts the emphasis on repeated conduct. A reader could begin by asking what faithfulness looked like in a decision that had to be made that day. For this household or community, one such decision concerned the reception given to people who might arrive with teaching about Christ.~Many deceivers had gone out into the world. The elder identifies them by their refusal to confess Jesus Christ as coming in the flesh. He tells the readers to watch themselves so that what had been worked for would be preserved and they might receive a full reward. A person who goes ahead and fails to remain in Christ's teaching does not have God, while the person who remains in the teaching has both the Father and the Son. The warning follows naturally from the greeting. The truth that brought the readers into fellowship concerned the Son himself, and their faithfulness to that truth would shape whom they recognised as a teacher.~If someone came without this teaching, the readers were told to withhold the welcome or greeting that would make them participants in the visitor's work. An open home could lend a teacher credibility and help the teaching reach others. The elder takes the consequences of that decision seriously enough to place it in a letter about love. He has much more to say and hopes to visit and speak face to face so that their joy may be full. The children of the elect sister send their greetings. The close preserves the warmth of the opening and leaves the readers with a picture of fellowship carried by people who both love one another and care what is taught in their midst.|Love for people includes care for what they are taught about Jesus and discernment about whose teaching we help to spread.|Before sharing a message about Jesus with others, what would help you judge whether it is faithful to him?
