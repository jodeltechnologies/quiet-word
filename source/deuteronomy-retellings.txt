1:1-4:49|What Moses Remembered Across the Jordan|Israel hears how rebellion delayed its journey and why it must remember the Lord at Horeb|In the fortieth year Moses speaks to Israel east of the Jordan. From Horeb to Kadesh-barnea was a journey of eleven days by the road through Seir, yet the generation that stood at Kadesh had spent years in the wilderness. Moses recalls appointing wise, experienced men to share the burden of judging. They were to hear small and great alike, including the foreigner, because judgment belonged to God. At Kadesh the people asked to send men ahead. The spies brought back fruit and said the land was good. The people nevertheless refused to go up and claimed the Lord had brought them out because he hated them. Moses reminded them of the God who fought for them in Egypt and carried them in the wilderness like a father carrying his son.~The Lord barred that generation from the land except Caleb, and named Joshua as the man who would bring Israel in. Moses recalls that he too would remain outside. The children they feared would be taken as prey would enter. Some tried to advance after God told them to turn back, and the Amorites chased them down. Years later Israel passed near the descendants of Esau and the lands of Moab and Ammon. God had given those peoples their possessions, and Israel was forbidden to seize them. Moses contrasts those boundaries with the command concerning Sihon, who refused Israel passage. Israel defeated Sihon and then Og of Bashan. Moses gave land east of the river to Reuben, Gad and half of Manasseh on condition that their fighting men help the other tribes receive theirs.~Moses encouraged Joshua with what he had seen the Lord do and asked God to let him cross. The Lord refused and told him to look out from Pisgah and strengthen Joshua. Moses then urges Israel to obey without adding to the commands or taking from them. They saw at Peor what happened to those who followed Baal. Their statutes should display wisdom to the nations, and they must teach their children what happened at Horeb. They heard a voice from the fire and saw no form. Moses warns against making an image of any creature or bowing to the heavenly lights. The Lord who brought them out of Egypt has claimed them as his own.~If they corrupt themselves in the land, Moses says they will be scattered. From exile they will find the Lord if they seek him with the whole heart and soul. He is merciful and remembers the covenant with their fathers. Moses asks whether any people has heard God speak from fire and lived, or seen God take a nation out of another nation with such signs. Israel is to know that the Lord is God in heaven and earth and keep his commands. Moses sets apart Bezer, Ramoth and Golan as refuge for those who kill unintentionally. The speech ends by placing the hearers again east of Jordan, on ground they had received and at the edge of land they had yet to enter.|Remember how God carried you before deciding what a present danger means. His faithfulness gives reason for obedience and for returning to him after failure.|What past mercy contradicts a conclusion you have drawn about God in a present difficulty?
5:1-8:20|Words to Teach at Home|Moses repeats the covenant commands and prepares Israel for the dangers of plenty|Moses calls Israel to hear, learn and do the statutes. He recalls the covenant made at Horeb and his place between the Lord and the people, who feared the fire. The Lord who brought them out of slavery forbids other gods, carved images and misuse of his name. They are to keep Sabbath so that children, servants, livestock and strangers may rest. They are to remember that they were slaves in Egypt and the Lord brought them out. They must honour their parents and refrain from killing, adultery, stealing, false testimony and coveting. The Lord wrote these words on two stone tablets. When the people heard his voice they asked Moses to receive the rest of his words. God heard their promise to obey and expressed his desire that their hearts would remain inclined to fear him.~Moses calls Israel to hear that the Lord is one and to love him with the whole heart, soul and strength. The words are to be on their hearts and taught diligently to their children. They are to speak of them when sitting at home, walking, lying down and rising, and set reminders on their hands, foreheads, doors and gates. Israel will inherit cities it did not build, houses it did not fill, wells it did not dig and vineyards it did not plant. When the people eat and are satisfied they must remember the Lord who brought them from Egypt. They must refrain from testing him as at Massah. When a child asks the meaning of the laws, parents are to begin with the family's former bondage and the Lord's strong hand, then explain why he gave the commands.~Moses warns Israel against covenants, intermarriage and worship that would turn the people towards the gods of the nations in Canaan. He tells them to tear down the places of idolatry. The Lord chose Israel because of his love and his oath to their fathers, while they were few in number. Moses promises blessing and reminds them of the signs in Egypt when they fear peoples stronger than themselves. The Lord will drive those nations out little by little, so wild animals do not multiply against Israel. The metal on idols must not be taken for gain. Something worth money in the market can become a snare when brought into an Israelite house.~The people are to remember the whole wilderness journey. The Lord humbled them, let them hunger and fed them manna so they might know that a person lives by every word from his mouth. Their clothes did not wear out and their feet did not swell. Like a father instructing a son, the Lord disciplined them. They are entering a good land with streams, crops and mineral wealth. Moses tells them to bless the Lord after eating and warns that houses, herds and silver can make the heart proud. They must remember who gives power to gain wealth in fulfilment of his covenant. If they follow other gods they will perish like the nations being displaced.|God's words belong in everyday conversation and teaching. Remembering who gave us food, strength and opportunity guards against pride once we have enough.|What ordinary moment in your household could become an occasion to remember and speak of God's provision?
9:1-12:32|The Land Was Never Their Wages|Moses recalls the golden calf and calls Israel to wholehearted loyalty in the land|Israel is about to cross against nations stronger than itself, with fortified cities and people feared for their stature. Moses says the Lord will go before them like a consuming fire. When the land is taken, Israel must not claim it as a reward for righteousness. The nations are being judged for their wickedness and the Lord is keeping his oath to the fathers. Israel itself has been stubborn. At Horeb Moses stayed forty days and nights on the mountain without eating or drinking and received the two tablets. God told him to go down because the people had made a calf. Moses saw it and broke the tablets before their eyes. He fell before the Lord for the people and prayed for Aaron too. He burned and crushed the calf and threw its dust into the stream from the mountain.~Moses recalls other places of provocation, including Taberah, Massah and Kibroth-hattaavah, and the refusal at Kadesh. He recalls pleading that the Lord remember Abraham, Isaac and Jacob and spare the people whom he brought from Egypt. The Lord told him to cut new tablets and make an ark. Moses received the words again and placed the tablets in the ark. The account also remembers stages of travel, Aaron's death and Eleazar's succession, and the setting apart of Levi to carry the ark, stand before the Lord and bless in his name. Levi has the Lord as its inheritance. Moses was then told to go ahead of the people so they might enter the land.~Moses asks what the Lord requires of Israel. They are to fear him, walk in his ways, love and serve him, and keep his commands for their good. Heaven and earth belong to him. He chose their fathers and their descendants, judges without favour or bribes and gives justice to the orphan and widow. He loves the foreigner and gives food and clothing. Israel is to love the foreigner because it lived as foreigners in Egypt. Moses addresses people who have seen the Lord's dealings with Egypt and the wilderness congregation, including the judgment on Dathan and Abiram. The land ahead drinks the rain of heaven. If they serve the Lord he will give rain in season, but idolatry will close the sky. Moses sets blessing and curse before them, to be pronounced on Gerizim and Ebal.~Israel must destroy the nations' worship sites and bring offerings to the place the Lord chooses for his name. Households are to rejoice there and include Levites. They may slaughter and eat ordinary meat in their towns, whether ceremonially clean or unclean, but must pour out its blood. Vows, tithes and sacred gifts belong at the chosen place even if the journey is long. Moses warns them not to ask how the displaced nations worshipped and copy their practices, including the burning of children. They are to do what the Lord commands without adding or subtracting. The people who once made a calf at the foot of Horeb are again told who has the right to direct their worship.|God's mercy gives people a reason for humility and gratitude. A people sustained by his faithfulness can give the foreigner and the vulnerable the care he asks of them.|Where have you treated a gift from God as evidence of your own superiority?
13:1-16:22|The Open Hand and the Shared Feast|Israel is taught to guard worship, care for neighbours in debt and remember deliverance together|Moses warns that even a prophet whose sign comes true must be rejected if the message directs Israel to other gods. The test concerns love for the Lord. An enticement from a close relative or friend is to be brought to judgment, and a report that a town has turned to other gods must be investigated thoroughly before the severe sentence laid down in the law. The proceeds of a condemned town are not to enrich the people carrying out the judgment. Israel is called the Lord's children and treasured people, and is forbidden certain mourning practices. The distinctions between clean and unclean animals extend to land animals, fish and birds. An animal found dead is treated differently from meat prepared for Israel's consumption, and a young goat must not be boiled in its mother's milk.~A yearly tithe of produce is to be eaten before the Lord at his chosen place. If the road is too long, the tithe can be converted to money and the household can buy what it needs there and rejoice before the Lord. The Levite must not be forgotten. At the end of each third year the tithe is laid up locally so Levites, foreigners, orphans and widows may eat and be satisfied. Every seventh year brings a release of claims against fellow Israelites. Moses warns against refusing a poor neighbour a loan because the release year is near. Israel is to open its hand and give what the person needs. The people hear both a promise of provision under obedience and the practical command to help the poor who will still be among them.~A Hebrew man or woman who serves six years is to be released in the seventh and supplied generously from flock, threshing floor and winepress. The owner must remember slavery in Egypt and redemption by the Lord. A servant who wishes to stay may make that choice under the stated procedure. The firstborn male of herd and flock is consecrated to the Lord and is neither worked nor shorn. The household eats it before the Lord, subject to the rules concerning blemish and blood. Moses moves from the release of a servant to a meal where the household acknowledges what belongs to God. Both instructions place a limit on what a prosperous person may keep for personal use.~The people are to keep Passover in the month of Abib because the Lord brought them out by night. They eat unleavened bread in remembrance of their hurried departure. They count seven weeks from the beginning of the harvest and bring a freewill offering as the Lord has blessed them. At the Feast of Booths they rejoice after gathering from threshing floor and winepress. The household's joy extends to servants, Levites, strangers, orphans and widows. Three times a year the men appear before the Lord with gifts according to their blessing. Moses ends with instructions to appoint judges, refuse bribes, pursue justice and keep trees of pagan worship and sacred pillars away from the Lord's altar. The celebrations of deliverance are to belong to a people practising justice in its own towns.|Remembering deliverance loosens our grip on money, labour and food. The Lord measures a shared feast partly by whether those without resources have room at it.|Who would have reason to rejoice with you if you shared this week's provision more deliberately?
17:1-21:23|Judgment, Leadership and Life in the Towns|The covenant sets limits for rulers and gives procedures for cases that cannot be decided by impulse|Moses forbids bringing a blemished offering and gives instructions for investigating reports of idolatry. A capital sentence requires two or three witnesses, and the witnesses act first in carrying it out. Difficult cases go to the priests and judge at the place the Lord chooses. If Israel asks for a king, the Lord is to choose him from among their brothers. The king must refrain from multiplying horses, wives and wealth or sending people back to Egypt for horses. He is to write a copy of the law and read it all his days so he fears the Lord and does not raise his heart above his brothers. Authority places the king under a word he must keep reading rather than above the people who hear it from him.~The priests and Levites live from what is assigned to them because the Lord is their inheritance. A Levite who comes from a town to serve at the chosen place may receive a share with the others. Israel must refuse divination, sorcery and consultation with the dead. The Lord will raise a prophet like Moses from among their brothers and put his words in that prophet's mouth. This answers the people's request at Horeb for someone to speak to them on God's behalf. A prophet who presumes to speak falsely is accountable. An unfulfilled prediction shows that the Lord did not speak it. Israel is not required to fear such a claim.~Refuge towns and roads to them are provided for people who cause death without prior hatred, as when an axe head flies off its handle. A murderer who flees there is handed over for judgment. Boundaries must not be moved. One witness cannot establish a charge, and malicious witnesses must answer for what they intended to do. When Israel goes to war, the priest tells the people to remember the Lord who goes with them. Officers release men with unfinished household duties or fearful hearts. Moses gives different directions for distant towns and for the peoples of the land. He also says that fruit trees should not be cut down during a siege, preserving food even while the land is in conflict.~When a slain person is found and no killer is known, the elders of the nearest town answer publicly with a rite and a prayer for pardon from innocent blood. Instructions address a captive woman taken as a wife, protecting her from resale if dismissed, and the right of a firstborn son whose mother is less loved. A persistently rebellious son is brought by his parents to the elders for judgment under the law. The body of an executed person hung on a tree must be buried that day. Moses has placed occasions of fear, grief and family conflict under public rules, witnesses and limits rather than leaving each household or ruler to decide what its anger permits.|God's law puts rulers under instruction and gives judges reasons to investigate before acting. A person's power over someone else brings duties as well as authority.|In what decision involving another person's future do you need to slow down and hear the facts more fairly?
22:1-26:19|The Neighbour in the Details|Moses brings covenant obedience into fields, homes, workplaces and worship|Moses tells Israel to return a neighbour's lost animal or garment and help raise a fallen beast. Roofs need parapets so a fall does not bring bloodguilt. Other rules concern distinctions in dress, protection of a nesting bird, mixtures of seed, animals and fabrics, and tassels on a garment. Cases involving marriage accusations, sexual misconduct and assault are judged according to circumstances that the passage distinguishes, including whether a woman was forced. The next chapter gives rules for admission to Israel's assembly involving certain family lines and peoples, remembering how Moab and Ammon treated Israel and how the Lord turned Balaam's curse into blessing. The military camp is to be kept clean because the Lord walks among the people.~A runaway slave is not to be handed back to the master and may live where he chooses without oppression. The Lord forbids cult prostitution and refuses its wages as payment of a vow. Israelites are instructed concerning interest, keeping vows and taking a handful of grain or eating grapes when passing through a neighbour's field without harvesting it for themselves. Moses addresses divorce and remarriage, grants a newly married man a year at home, and prohibits taking millstones as a pledge because these supply a person's living. He gives judgment against kidnapping and recalls what happened to Miriam when speaking about skin disease. A creditor must wait outside for the debtor to bring a pledge, and a poor person's cloak must be returned by sunset.~Workers are to receive wages the same day, whether fellow Israelites or resident foreigners. Parents and children are accountable for their own offences. Israel must give justice to strangers and orphans and refrain from taking a widow's garment in pledge. A forgotten sheaf, the olives left after the first beating and grapes left after gathering belong to the stranger, orphan and widow. The reason again is Israel's former slavery. Judges must limit corporal punishment so a brother is not degraded before them. An ox must be allowed to eat while treading grain. The law addresses the duty to preserve a deceased brother's family name and the public procedure when that duty is refused. It condemns dishonest weights and tells Israel to remember Amalek's attack on the weak and weary at the rear of the march.~In the land a worshipper brings the firstfruits in a basket and recalls before the Lord how an ancestor went down to Egypt, how his people suffered, and how God heard and brought them out into a fruitful land. The household rejoices with the Levite and foreigner. After giving the third year's tithe to those entitled to receive it, the worshipper declares that the sacred portion has been distributed as directed and asks the Lord to bless Israel. Moses closes with the people's acknowledgement of the Lord as their God and the Lord's acknowledgement of Israel as his treasured people. The confession at the basket names the history that makes forgotten grain, prompt wages and fair measures matters of faithfulness.|God's commands give special attention to people who cannot force us to treat them fairly. The memory of bondage makes room for generosity in harvest, wages and credit.|What is one obligation you can meet promptly for a person who cannot afford to wait?
27:1-30:20|Life and Death Set Before the People|Moses gives the covenant signs, announces its consequences and calls Israel to choose life|After crossing the Jordan Israel is to set up large stones on Mount Ebal, plaster them and write the law clearly upon them. An altar of unhewn stones is to be built there. The people offer sacrifices, eat and rejoice before the Lord. The tribes are assigned to Gerizim and Ebal for blessing and curse. The Levites pronounce curses on hidden idolatry, contempt for parents, shifting boundaries, misleading the blind, denying justice and various abuses of kinship or life. The people answer Amen, including to the curse on anyone who fails to uphold the words of the law. Moses has given them a ceremony in which they hear in public how the Lord regards wrong that might have been done without a witness.~Obedience brings blessing in city and field, in children and livestock, in basket and kneading bowl, with rain and victory over enemies. The Lord establishes his people as holy if they keep his commands. If they refuse, the curses reach the same settings. Work and planting fail, disease and enemies come, and people lose homes, fields and children. A nation from far away besieges their towns. Hunger becomes so grave that family affection gives way to horrors. The Lord warns of scattering and fear among the nations and of a return towards Egypt. The length of the warning makes Israel reckon with consequences beyond the immediate gain found in disregarding a command.~Moses addresses everyone standing in Moab, including leaders, families, foreigners and people who cut wood and draw water. The covenant reaches future generations too. He reminds them that their clothes and sandals did not wear out and that the Lord gave them victory over Sihon and Og. He warns against a hidden root of idolatry in a person who hears the oath and assures himself of safety while following his own stubborn heart. A later generation and visiting foreigners will see the desolation and ask why the Lord did it. They will hear that Israel left the covenant. What is hidden belongs to God, while what he has revealed belongs to the people and their children so they may do it.~If scattered Israel returns to the Lord and listens, he will gather the people even from the farthest places, bring them back and circumcise their hearts to love him. The covenant word is neither beyond heaven nor across the sea. It is near, in their mouth and heart, so they may do it. Moses calls heaven and earth to witness that he has set life and death, blessing and curse before them. They are to choose life by loving the Lord, listening to him and holding fast, because he is their life and the length of their days. The address leaves the people with a decision they can make before anyone sets foot across the river.|God's word calls for an answer from the whole community and from each hearer. Even the warning of exile leaves room for return and renewed love for him.|Where do you need to give up a private excuse and answer God's instruction plainly?
31:1-34:12|The Song, the Blessing and the View From Nebo|Moses commissions Joshua, leaves Israel a witness and sees the land before his death|Moses says he is 120 years old and will not cross the Jordan. The Lord will go before Israel, and Joshua will lead them. Moses tells the people and Joshua to be strong because the Lord will not forsake them. He writes the law and gives it to the priests and elders, ordering that it be read every seventh year at the Feast of Booths. Men, women, children and strangers must hear and learn, including children who have never heard it. At the tent the Lord tells Moses that the people will turn to other gods after his death. He tells Moses to write a song and teach it to Israel as a witness. Joshua receives his commission. The completed law is put beside the ark, and Moses calls elders and officers to hear the words he will speak.~Moses calls heaven and earth to listen. He praises the Lord as the Rock whose work is perfect, then asks why a corrupt and foolish people repays him as it does. He tells Israel to ask its elders and remember how the Lord found Jacob in a wilderness, guarded him and carried him like an eagle. God gave rich food, and Israel, grown fat, became unfaithful and sacrificed to strange gods. The Lord speaks of hiding his face and provoking his people by a people they despise. Calamity will follow. Yet he also judges their enemies, who might otherwise take credit for what happened. He will vindicate his servants and make atonement for his land and people. Moses and Joshua teach the song, and Moses tells the people to take its words to heart and teach them to their children.~The Lord directs Moses to Mount Nebo to see Canaan and die there. He recalls the failure to uphold his holiness at Meribah. Before going up, Moses blesses Israel. He recalls the Lord's coming from Sinai and speaks over the tribes. He asks that Reuben live, that Judah receive help, and that Levi teach God's judgments and serve at his altar. He speaks of Benjamin's safety, Joseph's fruitful inheritance, Zebulun and Issachar's rejoicing, and the portions or character of Gad, Dan, Naphtali and Asher. He closes by recalling the God who rides the heavens to help Israel and whose everlasting arms are beneath his people.~From Nebo the Lord shows Moses the land from Gilead and Dan across the territories towards the western sea and south to the plain of Jericho and Zoar. He tells him this is the land sworn to Abraham, Isaac and Jacob. Moses sees it without crossing. He dies in Moab and is buried in a valley, his grave unknown. His sight and strength had remained, and Israel mourns thirty days. Joshua is full of the spirit of wisdom because Moses laid hands on him, and Israel listens to him.
