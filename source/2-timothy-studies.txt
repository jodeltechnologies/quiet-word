1:1-18|Paul Remembers Timothy's Tears|Courage drawn from grace and fellowship|Paul remembers Timothy's tears and the sincere faith he saw in Timothy's grandmother Lois and mother Eunice. He asks Timothy to stir up his gift and to share in suffering for the gospel according to God's power. Paul tells him to guard what was entrusted through the Holy Spirit. He remembers Onesiphorus, who sought him out in Rome and refreshed him.|Paul encourages Timothy by naming people who passed on faith and people who assisted a prisoner. Their acts give courage a human shape alongside God's promise of power.|What did Onesiphorus do that showed he was willing to be associated with Paul?
2:1-13|Passing the Work to Faithful People|Endurance for a trust that goes beyond you|Paul tells Timothy to be strong in grace and entrust what he has heard to faithful people able to teach others. He uses images of a soldier, an athlete and a farmer to speak of discipline and patience. He directs Timothy to remember the risen Jesus, and says that although he himself is bound, God's word is free.|Timothy receives something he is expected to pass on. The images remind him that work spanning generations takes concentration, honest practice and patience.|What qualities should Timothy look for in the people to whom he passes on the teaching?
2:14-26|A Workman Who Handles the Word Carefully|Gentleness when correction is needed|Paul warns about quarrels over words and tells Timothy to handle the word of truth accurately. He names teaching about the resurrection that has upset some believers. Using vessels in a house as an image, he calls Timothy to a clean life. The Lord's servant must teach patiently and correct opponents gently in hope that they will repent.|Accuracy and gentleness belong together in Paul's instructions. The teacher's purpose is that a person may come to recognise truth and change.|What does Paul say the Lord's servant should do instead of entering a quarrel?
3:1-17|What Timothy Has Known Since Childhood|Scripture equips a faithful life|Paul describes difficult times when people may keep an outward form of godliness while denying its power. He reminds Timothy of the conduct and suffering he has witnessed in Paul. Timothy is to continue in what he learned and remember the sacred writings he has known since childhood. Scripture teaches, reproves, corrects and trains so that God's servant is equipped for good work.|Paul points Timothy to trusted teaching, lived example and Scripture. He describes what Scripture does to equip a person for the conduct God requires.|Which of the four uses of Scripture named here speaks most directly to a need in your life?
4:1-8|Preach the Word and Finish the Course|Faithfulness when hearers prefer something else|Paul solemnly charges Timothy to preach, correct and encourage with patience. People will gather teachers who say what they want to hear. Timothy is to remain watchful, endure hardship and fulfil his ministry. Paul speaks of his own departure as near and looks back on a fight fought, a course finished and faith kept.|Paul puts the immediate duty of teaching alongside a whole life of service. Timothy can measure success by fulfilling the work entrusted to him through changes in the audience.|What does Paul ask Timothy to keep doing when people turn towards messages they prefer?
4:9-22|Come Before Winter|Remembering the needs of a faithful worker|Paul asks Timothy to come soon. He tells him who has left and who remains, asks him to bring Mark and requests a cloak, books and parchments. He recalls facing his first defence without human support and says the Lord stood with him. He sends greetings, asks Timothy to come before winter and closes with grace.|The letter leaves Paul with ordinary needs and difficult memories alongside confidence in the Lord. The request for Mark also makes room for a worker to be useful after earlier trouble.|What concrete acts of help does Paul ask Timothy to bring with him?
