1:1-12|A Living Hope for Scattered Believers|God's mercy gives an inheritance beyond the reach of present trials|Peter addressed believers scattered across Pontus, Galatia, Cappadocia, Asia and Bithynia. He described their life in relation to God's foreknowledge, the sanctifying work of the Spirit and obedience associated with the sprinkling of Jesus Christ's blood. Their geographical separation and vulnerable social position did not exhaust the truth about who they were. They belonged within God's saving purpose. The greeting of grace and peace therefore reached people whose daily circumstances could make them feel displaced. Before discussing their suffering, Peter named the divine work that gave their faith its beginning and their community its identity. They could hear the rest of the letter as people whom God had called and set apart.~He blessed the God and Father of the Lord Jesus Christ, who in abundant mercy had given them new birth into a living hope through Christ's resurrection. That hope looked towards an inheritance untouched by corruption, defilement or fading, kept in heaven for them. They too were guarded by God's power through faith for salvation ready to be revealed. The promise addressed both the safety of the inheritance and the keeping of those awaiting it. Their future did not depend upon the durability of possessions they might lose or the approval of neighbours who might reject them. The risen Christ gave hope a foundation stronger than changing circumstances.~They rejoiced in this salvation even though, for a little while and as necessary, various trials brought grief. Peter allowed grief and rejoicing to stand together. The testing of faith was more precious than gold refined through fire and would result in praise, glory and honour at Jesus Christ's revelation. The readers had not seen him, yet loved him. They did not presently see him, yet believed and rejoiced with a joy words could not fully contain, receiving the outcome of faith in the salvation of their souls. The passage recognises a real relationship with Christ sustained without physical sight. Faithfulness was not made unreal by the ache of waiting or the pain of a difficult season.~The prophets had searched carefully concerning this salvation and the grace intended for those who would later receive the gospel. The Spirit of Christ testified beforehand to Christ's sufferings and the glories following them. The prophets learned that their service reached beyond their own time towards the people now hearing the message through the Holy Spirit sent from heaven. Even angels desired to look into these things. Peter placed the scattered believers within a long and honoured history of revelation. Their present hardship had not reduced them to forgotten lives. They were receiving a salvation towards which prophets had looked, and their living hope invited patient trust, grateful worship and love for the Saviour they had not yet seen.|Christ's resurrection gives believers a living hope and an enduring inheritance. Present grief can be brought honestly within that larger confidence.|What present uncertainty needs to be viewed in the light of the living hope God has given through Christ?
1:13-25|Set Your Hope and Walk in Holiness|Redeemed people learn to live as children of the holy Father|Peter moved from the greatness of salvation towards the response it required. The readers were to prepare their minds for action, remain sober and set their hope fully upon the grace to be brought at Jesus Christ's revelation. Hope was to give direction to present thinking. As obedient children, they should cease shaping themselves by the desires that governed their former ignorance. The God who called them was holy, so their conduct in every area should reflect his holiness. This was a call reaching beyond a meeting, a profession of belief or a particular duty. Their received identity as God's children was to become visible in the daily choices through which a life took its shape.~They called upon a Father who judged each person's work without partiality, and should therefore spend their time as sojourners in reverent fear. Nearness to God did not make carelessness appropriate. They had been redeemed from an empty inherited manner of life through a price far greater than corruptible silver or gold. The precious blood of Christ, compared with a lamb without blemish or spot, had secured their release. The contrast concerned the worth and effectiveness of the ransom. Familiar customs could feel dependable because they came through ancestors, yet inherited familiarity alone could not make an empty life sound. Redemption called them to receive a new pattern under the authority of the one who had purchased them.~Christ was foreknown before the foundation of the world and manifested in these last times for their sake. Through him they believed in God, who raised him from the dead and gave him glory. Their faith and hope were therefore directed towards God. The saving work had a purpose and depth greater than their immediate experience of it. Peter then connected obedience to the truth with sincere love of the brothers and sisters. They were to love one another earnestly from a pure heart. Holiness did not mature into cold distance from fellow believers. A purified life should become more capable of genuine love, freed from the deceit and selfish desire that made religious relationships shallow or competitive.~They had been born again through the living and enduring word of God, rather than through perishable seed. Scripture compared human life and its glory with grass and a flower that withered and fell, while the Lord's word remained forever. This was the word announced to them in the gospel. Peter thus returned their new conduct to its lasting source. A people born through an enduring word should not allow fading desires and passing admiration to set their purpose. The chapter invites steady attention to what redemption is changing. Reverence, holiness and sincere love belong together when the price of salvation and the permanence of God's promise are allowed to govern everyday life.|Those redeemed through Christ are called to holiness, reverence and sincere love. Let the enduring gospel shape conduct that passing desires once controlled.|What inherited habit or former desire needs to give way to the holy life God has called you to practise?
2:1-12|Living Stones and a People Who Declare God's Praise|New identity becomes a visible life among neighbours|Peter called his readers to put away malice, deceit, hypocrisy, envy and slander. These habits damaged the love to which the previous passage had summoned them. Like newborn infants longing for pure milk, they were to desire the nourishment through which they would grow, having tasted that the Lord was gracious. The image did not praise permanent immaturity. It commended an eager and undivided appetite for what supported the life God had given. A person cannot expect spiritual growth while steadily feeding the resentments and falsehoods that work against it. The goodness already received from the Lord should draw believers towards more attentive dependence upon his instruction.~Coming to Christ, they approached a living stone rejected by human beings but chosen and precious before God. They too were being built as living stones into a spiritual house and a holy priesthood, offering spiritual sacrifices acceptable through Jesus Christ. Their place in God's household did not depend upon sharing the judgments of those who had rejected the cornerstone. Scripture spoke of a chosen foundation stone in Zion and of the stone rejected by builders becoming the head of the corner. Christ's worth was settled by God. Trusting him required believers to receive that judgment even when influential people regarded him, and those who followed him, as unworthy of honour.~The stone also became a cause of stumbling for those who disobeyed the word. Peter then named the believing community as a chosen generation, royal priesthood, holy nation and people belonging to God. Their purpose was to declare the excellencies of the one who had called them out of darkness into his marvellous light. Once they had not been a people, but now they were God's people. Once they had not received mercy, but now mercy had reached them. These descriptions gave dignity without grounds for self-congratulation. Their identity rested upon God's call and mercy, and its outward purpose was praise that made his goodness known. Belonging brought a responsibility to represent the giver faithfully.~Peter appealed to them as strangers and sojourners to abstain from fleshly desires that warred against the soul. Their conduct among the Gentiles should be honourable, so that neighbours who accused them might see good works and glorify God in the day of visitation. Public misunderstanding could not be fully controlled, but their own conduct remained an area of responsibility. The community's witness would involve more than answering charges with words. Honest work, restraint, generosity and dependable care could make the goodness of God visible. The passage invites the reader to live from received mercy, seeking growth and honourable conduct because Christ has already given his people a place in the house he is building.|God's mercy gives his people a shared identity and a public calling. Grow in his word and let good conduct declare his goodness.|What would a neighbour learn about God's goodness from the way you presently speak, work and treat people?
2:13-25|Entrusting Judgment to the Righteous Judge|Honourable conduct and Christ's example under unjust suffering|Peter urged believers to submit for the Lord's sake to human institutions, including the king and governors appointed to punish wrongdoing and commend good conduct. God's will was that doing good should answer ignorant accusations. The readers were to live as free people whose freedom served God, rather than becoming a covering for evil. They should honour everyone, love the brotherhood, fear God and honour the king. These instructions gave a community under suspicion a way to act faithfully in public. Human authority was recognised within a life whose deepest allegiance belonged to God. Courtesy and useful conduct mattered even when neighbours misunderstood the gospel or treated its followers with contempt.~The letter then addressed household servants within the coercive arrangements of the ancient world. They were urged towards respectful conduct even under harsh masters. Peter distinguished suffering that followed personal wrongdoing from unjust suffering borne while conscious of God. The latter could be an expression of faithfulness. He was speaking to people with limited social power and directing their hope towards God's judgment. The passage should never become a weapon by which an employer, church or household requires someone to accept abuse or prevents that person from seeking protection. Its instruction concerns how a believer can remain faithful when wronged. It gives no moral approval to the person inflicting the wrong.~Christ's suffering provided the central example. He committed no sin, and no deceit was found in his mouth. When insulted, he did not answer with insult. When suffering, he did not threaten, but entrusted himself to the one who judged righteously. His conduct showed strength governed by trust in the Father. The example did not require pretending injustice was harmless or calling the guilty innocent. It placed final judgment in God's hands and refused to let an enemy dictate the character of the response. A believer following Christ may pursue truth and protection while refusing the bitterness, falsehood and personal vengeance that suffering can tempt a person to adopt.~Peter then spoke of Christ bearing sins in his body upon the tree so that his people might die to sins and live to righteousness. The language of healing belonged to this saving work and its renewal of a wandering people. They had been like straying sheep, but had now returned to the Shepherd and Overseer of their souls. The passage therefore moved beyond a moral example towards the grace that made a new life possible. Christ did not simply show sufferers an admirable response. Through his sacrifice he brought sinners back into the Shepherd's care. Faithful endurance grows from that received salvation, finding courage to do good and entrust judgment to God while remaining attentive to the safety and dignity of those who suffer.|Christ bore sins and brought wanderers back to the Shepherd. Follow his truthfulness and restraint when wronged, entrusting final judgment to God.|How can you seek a truthful and responsible response to wrong without allowing retaliation to govern your words or actions?
3:1-12|Honour Within the Home and Mercy Within the Church|The hidden life must agree with public faith|Peter addressed wives, including those whose husbands did not obey the word. Their faithful conduct could bear witness without a continuing contest of arguments. The emphasis was upon reverent purity and the hidden person of the heart. Elaborate hair, gold and outward clothing could not supply the enduring beauty of a gentle and quiet spirit precious before God. The passage directed attention towards the character clothing could neither create nor conceal. A life seeking God's approval would cultivate modesty, humility and inward steadiness. These qualities were not an admission that a woman's voice or judgment had no value. They described a heart whose confidence rested in God and whose witness could be seen in the manner of daily living.~The holy women of earlier times provided examples of hope in God, with Sarah named in relation to Abraham. Peter encouraged doing good without surrendering to terror. Husbands were then called to live with their wives according to understanding, giving honour as to the weaker vessel and as fellow heirs of the grace of life. The instruction required attentiveness and respect, recognising a vulnerability that must never be exploited. The shared inheritance placed wife and husband together before God. A husband's treatment of his wife could hinder his prayers. Spiritual standing and public responsibility therefore gave no exemption from the duty of honour within the closest relationship of the household.~Peter widened the appeal to the whole community. They were to share a unity of purpose, compassion, brotherly love, tenderness and humility. Evil should not receive evil in return, nor insult another insult. They were called to bless, remembering the blessing they themselves were to inherit. The command tested how the community would respond when patience was most difficult. Courtesy at a gathering could coexist with harshness at home or bitterness after an argument. Peter required a more connected life. Those who hoped in God were to allow received grace to shape their response to another person's weakness, disagreement or hostility, including the person least able to reward their restraint.~A quotation from the Psalms then joined a well-ordered life to disciplined speech and active goodness. Those desiring life and good days should keep the tongue from evil and the lips from deceit, turn from evil, do good, and seek peace with determination. The Lord's eyes were upon the righteous and his ears open to their prayers, while his face opposed wrongdoing. The passage ended by placing home and community beneath God's attentive care. Peace was something to pursue through conduct, rather than a pleasant mood expected to arrive by itself. A practical response begins with listening more carefully, speaking truthfully, giving honour and refusing the small acts of contempt through which relationships are gradually damaged.|Faith should become visible in modesty, respect, truthful speech and active pursuit of peace. God sees how we treat people within the home as well as in public.|What change in your speech or attention would give greater honour to someone who lives or works closely with you?
3:13-22|Ready to Explain the Hope Within You|A good conscience rests upon the suffering and victory of Christ|Peter asked who would harm those eager to do good, yet immediately recognised that suffering for righteousness could still occur. Such sufferers were blessed and should not be ruled by their opponents' threats. They were to honour the Lord within their hearts and remain ready to explain the hope they held. The manner of the answer mattered. Gentleness and reverence should accompany the defence, together with a good conscience. A truthful explanation could be undermined by harshness or conduct that justified the accusation. The reader was therefore called to prepare both understanding and character. Hope in Christ should become a reasoned answer carried by a life that did not contradict it.~If suffering came, it was better to suffer for doing good than for doing evil. Peter directed attention again to Christ, who suffered once for sins, the righteous for the unrighteous, in order to bring people to God. He was put to death in the flesh and made alive in the Spirit. His suffering was neither proof that evil had escaped God's rule nor an incomplete effort requiring another saving sacrifice. It accomplished reconciliation through the one who was himself righteous. Believers facing accusation could remember that their Saviour had already passed through unjust suffering and brought them into a relationship stronger than their opponents' judgment of them.~Peter then referred to Christ's proclamation to spirits in prison, connected with those disobedient when God's patience waited during the preparation of Noah's ark. This brief passage has received several Christian interpretations. Some understand Christ to have preached through Noah by the Spirit to people now in confinement, while others understand a proclamation of his victory to imprisoned spirits. Peter does not give a full account of the timing or manner here. The study should therefore leave that uncertainty visible rather than build an elaborate unseen history upon a few verses. What the text does clearly connect is ancient disobedience, God's patience, Noah's deliverance and the saving work and triumph of Christ.~Eight people were brought safely through the water in the ark. Peter related that deliverance to baptism, carefully distinguishing the removal of dirt from the body's surface from the appeal or answer of a good conscience towards God through Jesus Christ's resurrection. The saving reality could not be reduced to an outward washing detached from the risen Lord. The passage ended with Christ in heaven at God's right hand, with angels, authorities and powers subject to him. The closing confidence therefore rested upon his victory. Believers could answer honestly, live with a clear conscience and face suffering without surrendering to fear because the Saviour who brought them to God had been raised and exalted above every opposing power.|Explain your hope with gentleness and a good conscience. Christ's saving suffering, resurrection and exaltation give faith its firm foundation.|How could you prepare to explain your hope clearly while showing greater gentleness towards the person asking?
4:1-11|The Time Remaining Is for God's Will|A changed life becomes prayer, hospitality and faithful service|Peter urged believers to arm themselves with the understanding shown by Christ in suffering. The person resolved to endure for him had made a decisive break with the rule of sin and should live the remaining time according to God's will. This did not claim that physical suffering automatically removed every sinful inclination. It described the change of allegiance expressed when obedience became more important than avoiding every cost. The readers had already spent enough time following the former pattern of unchecked desires, drunkenness, revelry and unlawful idolatry. The gospel gave them a reason to ask how the time still available could now serve the God who had called them.~Former companions were surprised that the believers no longer joined the same flood of excess and spoke abusively about them. Changed conduct could therefore bring social pressure from people who regarded refusal as an insult to their shared habits. Peter reminded the readers that these accusers would give account to the one ready to judge the living and the dead. The following reference to the gospel preached to the dead has been understood in different ways, often as a reference to people who heard the gospel while alive and had since died. Its place in the argument points towards life before God beyond human judgment. It should not be turned into a confident promise of a new opportunity for repentance after death.~The end of all things was near, so the community should be sober and alert for prayer. Peter did not direct them towards excitement that displaced ordinary responsibility. Their awareness of the approaching fulfilment should make them more attentive to God and more faithful towards one another. Above all they were to maintain earnest love, because love covered a multitude of sins. This meant patient, forgiving care that refused to magnify every offence, rather than concealment of wrongdoing that needed protection and correction. Hospitality was to be offered without grumbling. An open door could lose much of its kindness when the person welcomed was made to feel like an unwelcome interruption.~Each person had received a gift and should use it in serving others as a faithful steward of God's varied grace. Speaking should be governed by the seriousness of communicating God's words, while service should depend upon the strength God supplied. The purpose was that God might be glorified through Jesus Christ, to whom belonged glory and dominion. The passage placed ability beneath stewardship. A gift was entrusted for another person's good and the giver's honour, rather than as a possession to display for personal standing. The reader is invited to examine the remaining time, existing opportunities and ordinary relationships through that purpose. Prayer, patient love, a willing welcome and dependable service become ways the changed life honours its Lord.|Use the time remaining for God's will. Prayer, earnest love, willing hospitality and service make received grace useful to others.|What gift or opportunity could you use more faithfully for another person's good this week?
4:12-19|Do Not Be Surprised by the Fiery Trial|Suffering for Christ calls for trust and continued goodness|Peter told the beloved community not to be surprised by the fiery trial among them as though something strange had happened. Their suffering needed to be understood in relation to Christ rather than treated as proof that faith had failed. Insofar as they shared his sufferings, they could rejoice in anticipation of joy when his glory was revealed. The exhortation did not demand delight in cruelty or the suppression of grief. It gave suffering believers a larger account of what their faithfulness meant. Those enduring reproach for Christ's name were blessed because the Spirit of glory and of God rested upon them. Their opponents' contempt did not determine God's regard for them.~The passage then made a necessary distinction concerning the reason for suffering. No believer should suffer as a murderer, thief, evildoer or meddler. Religious identity could not convert the consequences of wrongdoing into persecution for righteousness. Peter required truthful self-examination before taking comfort from the promises addressed to those suffering for Christ. If someone suffered as a Christian, however, that person should not be ashamed but glorify God in that name. A hostile label could become an occasion for faithful confession. The practical question was whether one's conduct actually belonged to the Lord whose name was being claimed, especially when pressure made self-defence and personal resentment tempting.~The time for judgment to begin at God's household had come. If it began with his own people, Peter asked what the outcome would be for those disobeying the gospel. He reinforced the seriousness through the scriptural question about the righteous being saved through difficulty and the ungodly facing judgment. These words placed the community's trials within God's searching governance without making the church immune from examination. They also prevented the readers from assuming that people presently enjoying safety had therefore escaped accountability. God was attentive to both his household and those opposing the gospel. Present comfort and present hardship could not be used as simple measures of final approval or rejection.~Peter concluded that those suffering according to God's will should entrust their souls to a faithful Creator while continuing to do good. The last instruction is important because trust remained active. They were not to withdraw into despair, answer cruelty with evil or stop caring for other people because their own circumstances were painful. Their Creator could be trusted with the life they could not fully protect, and they still had opportunities to act faithfully within it. The passage invites a response of courage joined to moral honesty. We examine our conduct, refuse shame for belonging to Christ and continue whatever good remains possible while bringing grief, weakness and the future into God's faithful keeping.|When suffering comes for faithfulness to Christ, entrust yourself to God and continue doing good. Examine your conduct honestly rather than treating every conflict as persecution.|What good can you continue faithfully while a painful situation remains unresolved?
5:1-14|Shepherds, Humble Hearts and a Watchful People|Faithful leadership and shared endurance beneath God's care|Peter appealed to the elders as a fellow elder, a witness of Christ's sufferings and one who would share the glory to be revealed. They were to shepherd God's flock among them, exercising oversight willingly and eagerly. Greed should have no place in the task, and authority should not become domination over those entrusted to their care. The elders were to be examples to the flock. Their responsibility belonged beneath the Chief Shepherd, whose appearing would bring an unfading crown of glory. The image placed both leaders and people within a relationship owned by Christ. A congregation was never a leader's private possession, and spiritual responsibility required a life whose pattern others could safely learn to follow.~Younger members were told to respond respectfully to elders, and all were to clothe themselves with humility towards one another. God resisted the proud and gave grace to the humble. They should humble themselves beneath his mighty hand, trusting him to lift them up in due time, and cast their anxieties upon him because he cared for them. Humility did not mean that distress must be hidden or carried alone. The same God whose authority called for submission invited his people to entrust him with their burden. A community receiving that care should become more capable of listening, sharing responsibility and giving help without turning another person's need into an occasion for superiority.~The readers also needed sober watchfulness. Their adversary the devil prowled like a roaring lion seeking someone to devour. They were to resist him, firm in the faith, knowing that their brothers and sisters throughout the world experienced similar sufferings. The warning did not invite an elaborate search for hidden explanations behind every inconvenience. It called for spiritual alertness and steadfast trust under real pressure. Isolation could make a trial seem unique and defeat appear inevitable. Remembering the wider fellowship of believers strengthened the resolve to stand. Peter joined the seriousness of opposition to the assurance that suffering people did not face it as a forgotten or solitary company.~The God of all grace, who had called them to eternal glory in Christ, would restore, establish, strengthen and settle them after they had suffered for a little while. Dominion belonged to him. Peter then named Silvanus in connection with the letter, urged them to stand in the true grace of God, and sent greetings from the community associated with Babylon and from Mark. A loving greeting and a blessing of peace closed the epistle. Its ending gathered leadership, humility, prayerful dependence and watchfulness beneath grace. The reader's response can be practical and steady. Care for entrusted people, receive correction without pride, bring anxieties to God and stand alongside other believers while waiting for the Chief Shepherd's promised glory.|Lead through example, live humbly and cast your cares upon God. His grace strengthens a watchful people who stand together in faith.|Which anxiety should you entrust to God, and whose burden could you help carry with greater humility?
