1:1-14|Blessed in Christ|Grace gives God's people a home and a future|Paul blesses God for choosing a people in Christ and adopting them as his children. He speaks of forgiveness through Christ's blood and of God's purpose to bring all things together in him. Those who hear and believe the gospel receive the promised Spirit as the pledge of their inheritance.|Paul starts with gifts God has already given. Remembering those gifts can give steadiness to a life otherwise governed by the need to prove its worth.|Which gifts does Paul associate with the Father, with Christ and with the promised Spirit?
1:15-23|A Prayer for Opened Eyes|Knowing the hope of God's calling|After hearing of their faith and love, Paul gives thanks and prays that the believers will know God better. He asks that they understand the hope of their calling and the greatness of God's power towards believers. He points to that power in raising Christ and setting him above every authority as head of the church.|Paul prays for people who already believe. He wants them to grasp more fully what God's action means for their future and for the church.|What three things does Paul want the believers to recognise more clearly?
2:1-10|From Death to a Life of Good Works|Grace gives rise to obedience|Paul recalls a former life governed by sin and disobedience. Then he describes God, rich in mercy, making people alive with Christ. Salvation is received by grace through faith and leaves no ground for boasting. Those saved are God's workmanship, created in Christ for good works God has prepared.|Good works have a clear place after the gift of salvation. Paul gives a reason to pursue them without turning them into a claim on God.|Where do good works belong in the sequence Paul sets out?
2:11-22|Those Who Were Far Off Come Near|Peace that makes room for others|Paul asks Gentile believers to remember when they were strangers to Israel's covenants and without hope. Christ brings them near through his blood and makes peace between divided peoples. Both have access to the Father through one Spirit. Paul pictures them as members of God's household and stones in a temple where God dwells.|Peace with God changes the standing of people towards one another. The household image makes that change a matter of belonging, rather than mere tolerance.|What changes for those Paul first describes as strangers?
3:1-13|Paul Names the Mystery|Sharing the promise across former boundaries|Writing as a prisoner, Paul explains the grace given to him to proclaim Christ among the Gentiles. He names the mystery plainly: through the gospel Gentiles are fellow heirs and members of the same body. God's wisdom is made known through the church. Paul asks his readers to take courage in the face of his sufferings for them.|The mystery becomes visible in people who now share a body and an inheritance. Paul sees that shared life as worth the trouble his ministry has brought him.|Which three expressions does Paul use to describe Gentile believers' place in God's people?
3:14-21|Rooted and Grounded in Love|Praying for strength in the inner person|Paul bows before the Father and prays that believers will be strengthened through the Spirit and that Christ will dwell in their hearts through faith. He asks that they grasp, together with all the saints, the dimensions of Christ's love. He ends by giving glory to God, whose power can do more than they ask or think.|Paul asks for strength to receive and comprehend love. The words 'with all the saints' give the fellowship a part in learning what that love is like.|What does Paul ask God to strengthen the believers to understand?
4:1-16|One Body Learning to Grow|Unity and maturity through shared service|Paul calls believers to walk with humility, patience and care for unity. He names what they share: body, Spirit, hope, Lord, faith, baptism and Father. Christ gives different people to equip the church for service. As each part does its work, the body grows in maturity, speaking the truth in love.|Unity gives different gifts room to serve a common purpose. Paul measures growth by the maturity of the whole body as its members help each other.|How do truth and love work together in Paul's picture of a growing church?
4:17-32|Putting Off the Old Way|Practising truth and kindness|Paul tells believers to put off their former way of life, be renewed in mind and put on the new person. He then names what this looks like: truthful speech, dealing promptly with anger, honest work that enables sharing and words that build people up. He calls them to forgive as God has forgiven them in Christ.|Paul gives each rejection of a harmful habit a positive direction. Honest work, for instance, gives a person something to share with someone in need.|Choose one old habit and the action Paul gives in its place. What would that change look like today?
5:1-21|Walking in Love, Light and Wisdom|Making good use of the time|Paul asks God's children to imitate him by walking in love as Christ did. He then speaks of speech and conduct fitting for people who belong to the light and urges them to discern what pleases the Lord. He tells them to use the time wisely and be filled with the Spirit, giving thanks and speaking to one another in song.|Paul's images of walking give direction to repeated choices. Spirit-filled life is shown in what the community says and sings and in its gratitude.|What habits does Paul place alongside his instruction to be filled with the Spirit?
5:22-6:9|Faithfulness Within the Household|Answering to Christ in close relationships|Paul addresses wives and husbands, using Christ and the church to explain the seriousness of marital love. He tells husbands to love with the care Christ gives the church. He speaks to children and parents, then to servants and masters. He tells masters to stop threatening and remember that in heaven they have the same Master, who shows no partiality.|The passage places duties on people with greater authority as well as on those under it. Christ's care sets a demanding measure for how power is used.|What responsibilities does Paul place on husbands, fathers and masters?
6:10-24|Standing Firm and Praying for Others|Strength supplied by the Lord|Paul gives an image of armour for a struggle involving spiritual powers. He names truth, righteousness, readiness with the gospel, faith, salvation and God's word. He calls for prayer for all believers and asks for boldness to speak as a prisoner. Tychicus is sent to give news and encourage their hearts.|The image leads into watchful prayer for other people. Paul himself asks the church to pray for his speech, showing how believers can support one another in the struggle.|What does Paul ask the church to pray for him as he closes the letter?
