1:1-13|The Road, the River and the Wilderness|The beginning of the good news|Mark begins in the wilderness, where John calls people to repentance and baptises them. John points forward to someone who will baptise with the Holy Spirit. Jesus comes from Nazareth, is baptised and hears the Father's voice. The Spirit immediately drives him into the wilderness, where he is tempted for forty days and angels minister to him.|The Father's declaration precedes the wilderness. A place of testing can come directly after a clear word of assurance.|What does Mark tell you about Jesus before he records his first public words?
1:14-34|From the Nets to the Doorway|Authority that reaches ordinary homes|After John is put in prison, Jesus announces the kingdom and calls four fishermen. In Capernaum he teaches in the synagogue and commands an unclean spirit to leave a man. Then he goes into Simon and Andrew's home, where Simon's mother-in-law lies ill. He lifts her up, and she begins serving them. By evening many from the town have come to the door.|Mark follows Jesus from public teaching to a sickbed in a home. His authority has a personal reach, and the healed woman answers with service.|What does Jesus do for Simon's mother-in-law, and what is her first recorded response?
1:35-45|The Place of Prayer and the Man Outside|Compassion guided by purpose|Before daylight Jesus went to a solitary place to pray. Simon and the others found him and said everyone was looking for him. Jesus led them towards other towns to preach. A man with leprosy asked to be made clean. Jesus touched him and sent him to the priest, instructing him to keep quiet. The man told many people, and Jesus could no longer enter a town openly.|The search of the crowd did not settle where Jesus would go. Prayer and the purpose for which he came help us read his decision to move on.|How did Jesus respond when told that everyone was looking for him?
2:1-17|Through the Roof and Around the Table|Forgiveness and fellowship|A crowd filled the house where Jesus was teaching. Four men carried a paralysed man to the roof, opened it and lowered him. Jesus spoke forgiveness, then healed him openly when scribes questioned his authority. Later Jesus called Levi from the tax booth and ate with people whom the scribes regarded as sinners. He spoke of a physician whose work was with the sick.|The four men make their faith visible through steady trouble on another person's behalf. Jesus brings the man's need of forgiveness into view before he sends him home walking.|What do the four men recognise about the need to bring their friend to Jesus?
2:18-28|A Wedding and a Walk Through Grain|Receiving God's gifts in their purpose|People asked why Jesus' disciples did not fast as others did. He compared his presence to a bridegroom's time with his guests and used images of cloth and wineskins. On a Sabbath the disciples plucked grain as they passed through a field. Jesus answered an objection by recalling David's need and said that the Sabbath was made for people. He declared the Son of Man Lord of it.|Jesus draws attention to the occasion for a practice and the purpose for which God gave it. Those questions can expose needless severity in our treatment of people.|How does Jesus use David's need to answer the objection over the grain?
3:1-19|A Hand Restored and Twelve Appointed|Being with Jesus before being sent|In the synagogue Jesus called a man with a withered hand to stand where everyone could see him. He asked whether the Sabbath permitted doing good or harm. Their silence grieved him, and he restored the hand. Opposition grew as crowds gathered. On a mountain Jesus appointed twelve, calling them to be with him and preparing to send them to preach.|Jesus puts a man's need in the centre of the room. When he appoints the Twelve, he makes being with him part of their calling before their public work.|What two purposes does Mark name when Jesus appoints the Twelve?
3:20-35|Voices Outside the House|The family that does God's will|People close to Jesus sought to take charge of him after hearing what was said about him. Scribes from Jerusalem attributed his authority over demons to their ruler. Jesus answered with a divided house and the image of binding a strong man. When his mother and brothers waited outside, he looked at those sitting around him and called those who do God's will his family.|Outsiders were busy explaining Jesus, while people around him were listening to him. The family he names is marked by a response to God's will.|How does Jesus' picture of a divided house answer the scribes' charge?
4:1-25|The Seed and the Lamp|Attending to what you hear|Jesus taught the crowd from a boat about seed landing in four kinds of soil. Alone with his followers, he explained how opposition, worries and the desire for riches can cut short the effect of the word. He spoke of a lamp brought to be set on a stand and urged his hearers to take heed to what they heard.|The parable gives us more to examine than our first response to the word. It asks whether we are making room for it to grow after that first hearing.|What do the thorns represent, and where could they be choking what you have heard?
4:26-41|A Growing Field and a Quiet Sea|Faith when you cannot control events|Jesus described seed growing while a farmer slept and a mustard seed becoming a large plant. That evening the disciples took him across the water. A storm filled the boat while Jesus slept on a cushion. They woke him with a question about whether he cared. He silenced the wind and sea, then asked about their fear and faith. The disciples wondered who he was.|The farmer cannot account for every stage of the growth, and the disciples cannot command the storm. The two scenes ask where trust belongs when events are beyond our control.|What did the disciples' question assume about Jesus while he was asleep?
5:1-20|The Man Who Wanted to Go With Jesus|Witness starts at home|On the far shore a man who lived among the tombs ran to Jesus. The spirits called themselves Legion. Jesus instead sent him home to tell what the Lord had done for him.|The restored man received a task close to home. Telling people who knew his former condition gave them a concrete account of mercy.|What did Jesus ask the man to tell his own people?
5:21-43|Two Daughters in Need|Jesus receives the hidden sufferer|Jairus begged Jesus to come to his dying daughter. On the way a woman who had suffered for twelve years touched Jesus' clothing. He stopped, heard her tell the truth and called her daughter. News then came that Jairus's child had died. Jesus went on, took the girl's hand and told her to rise. Afterwards he gave instructions that she should be fed.|Jesus addresses one sufferer in a crowd and remembers an ordinary need after raising another. Care can include attention to details others consider too small to notice.|What did Jesus ask people to do for the girl after she rose?
6:1-13|The Hometown and the Roads Beyond|Witness amid rejection|Jesus taught in his hometown, where people took offence at someone whose work and family they knew. He marvelled at their unbelief and continued through the villages. He sent the Twelve in pairs, instructing them to travel lightly, stay where welcomed and leave a testimony where people refused them. They preached repentance and ministered to the sick.|Rejection in a familiar place did not end the work. Jesus trained the Twelve to minister together and to handle refusals without getting stuck in them.|What did Jesus instruct the Twelve to do when a place would not receive them?
6:14-29|Herod's Banquet and John's Death|The cost of pleasing an audience|When reports of Jesus reached Herod, he remembered John, whom he had beheaded. John had spoken against his marriage. Herod feared John and listened to him, while Herodias wanted him dead. At a banquet her daughter asked for John's head after Herod offered her a reward. Herod was grieved and gave the order because of his oaths and the guests.|Listening to a truthful person can coexist with refusing to act on what he says. Herod's decision shows what can happen when approval in a room matters more than conscience.|How did Herod's opinion of John differ from what he finally chose to do?
6:30-56|Bread for the Crowd and Jesus on the Water|Remembering the help already given|The apostles returned tired, and Jesus took them aside. The crowd arrived ahead of them. Seeing people like sheep without a shepherd, Jesus taught them and fed them with five loaves and two fish. After praying on the mountain, he came across the water to the disciples. They were terrified, and the wind fell when he joined them. Mark connects their astonishment with their failure to understand the loaves.|A fresh fear can hide the significance of help we have just received. Mark invites readers to hold the feeding and the crossing together as they ask who Jesus is.|What does Mark say the disciples had failed to understand before the crossing?
7:1-23|The Gift That Excused Neglect|Looking beneath religious appearances|Scribes and Pharisees questioned the disciples' eating customs. Jesus answered by describing how a claim that something was devoted to God could be used to withhold help from parents. He then taught the crowd about what defiles a person. In the house he explained that evil actions and desires come out of the heart.|A religious claim can become a polished excuse for neglecting someone. Jesus makes both the neglected duty and the motives of the heart visible.|Which command did Jesus say could be set aside through the tradition about gifts?
7:24-37|A Mother's Reply and an Opened Ear|Mercy in unfamiliar places|In the district of Tyre a Gentile woman asked Jesus to deliver her daughter. He spoke of the children's bread, and she answered that dogs beneath the table ate the children's crumbs. Jesus sent her home to find the child delivered. Later people brought him a man who could scarcely hear or speak. Jesus took him aside, looked towards heaven and opened his ears.|Jesus hears the mother's reply and deals personally with the man brought by others. People beyond familiar religious boundaries receive his attention.|What do you learn from the way Jesus handled the man privately before the healing became widely known?
8:1-26|Another Meal and Sight in Stages|Learning to see what is before you|Jesus fed another hungry crowd, this time with seven loaves and a few fish. Pharisees asked him for a sign, and he sighed deeply. In the boat the disciples worried about bread and missed his warning about leaven. He asked them to recall the baskets left after both meals. At Bethsaida a blind man first saw people like walking trees, then saw clearly after Jesus touched him again.|Mark places questions about seeing beside a healing in stages. Honest attention to what we only partly understand can make room for further instruction.|What do the disciples remember about the baskets, and what do they still need to understand?
8:27-38|The Confession on the Road|The identity of Jesus and the cost of following|On the road near Caesarea Philippi, Jesus asked the disciples who people said he was. Peter answered the more direct question by saying he was the Christ. When Jesus began teaching about suffering, rejection, death and rising again, Peter rebuked him. Jesus called the crowd to hear what following him meant. He asked what it would profit someone to gain the world and lose his life.|Peter accepts a title and objects to what Jesus says it will involve. Jesus teaches disciples to examine what they are protecting when his way calls for surrender.|What words of Jesus provoked Peter's objection?
9:1-29|Glory on the Mountain and Need Below|Prayer when faith is weak|Jesus was transfigured before Peter, James and John, and a voice from the cloud told them to hear him. Below, a crowd gathered around the other disciples, who had been unable to help a suffering boy. The boy's father pleaded with Jesus and acknowledged that he needed help with his unbelief. Jesus delivered the boy. Later he spoke to the disciples about prayer.|The father's plea gives words to imperfect faith. He brings his uncertainty to Jesus along with his son's need.|How does the father's plea hold belief and need together?
9:30-50|The Argument They Kept Quiet|Welcome, humility and care|Jesus again spoke of his death and resurrection. 10:1-31|Children at the Door and a Man With Wealth|Receiving the kingdom with open hands|Jesus answered a question about divorce by returning to God's purpose for marriage. 10:32-52|Two Requests on the Road|Serving and seeing|Jesus walked ahead on the way to Jerusalem and spoke of what awaited him. James and John asked to sit in places of honour. 11:1-26|Branches, a Temple and a Withered Tree|The fruit of prayer and forgiveness|Jesus entered Jerusalem amid cries of Hosanna. The next day he found a fig tree with leaves and no fruit. 11:27-33|An Answer Kept Back|Being honest about authority|Chief priests, scribes and elders asked who authorised Jesus' actions. He asked whether John's baptism came from heaven or from people. 12:28-44|The Listening Scribe and the Widow's Coins|Wholehearted devotion|A scribe asked which command came first. Jesus answered with love for God and neighbour.
