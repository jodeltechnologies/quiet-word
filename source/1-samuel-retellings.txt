1:1-18|Hannah Pours Out Her Grief at Shiloh|Bringing sorrow honestly before God|Every year Elkanah travelled from his home in the hill country of Ephraim to worship and sacrifice at Shiloh. His household went with him. Peninnah had sons and daughters, while Hannah had no children. Elkanah loved Hannah and gave her a special portion when the family shared the sacrificial meal. Yet his affection could not take away the grief she carried. Peninnah repeatedly used Hannah's childlessness to hurt her. The journey to worship became another occasion for provocation. Year after year Hannah wept and lost her appetite. Elkanah asked why she was so sorrowful and whether his love meant more to her than ten sons. His questions show his concern, but Hannah still had something she needed to bring before God.~After the meal at Shiloh, Hannah rose and prayed. Eli the priest was sitting beside a doorway of the sanctuary. Hannah wept bitterly as she addressed the Lord of hosts. She asked Him to look on her distress, remember her and give her a son. With that request she made a vow. If God gave her the child, she would give him to the Lord for his whole life, and no razor would come upon his head. Her prayer joined a deeply personal longing to a promise of lifelong dedication. The account lets us hear her grief and her request without pretending that the years of waiting had been easy.~Hannah continued praying, and Eli watched her mouth. Her lips moved, but he heard no voice. He concluded that she was drunk and rebuked her, telling her to put away her wine. At the very place where she had come with her sorrow, Hannah now had to answer a mistaken accusation. She replied respectfully. She had drunk neither wine nor strong drink. She was a woman in deep distress, pouring out her soul before the Lord. Eli had seen the movement of her lips but had misunderstood the heart behind it. Hannah asked him to recognise the abundance of grief that had kept her praying there.~Eli changed his response. He told her to go in peace and asked that the God of Israel grant what she had requested. Hannah received his words and went away. She ate, and her face was no longer downcast. At this point in the story she still had no child, and the narrator has announced no pregnancy. What has changed is the woman who has brought her burden before the Lord. The household's painful circumstances have been told plainly, the priest's mistake has been corrected, and Hannah has left the sanctuary able to eat again. The next morning will bring another act of worship and the journey home.|Hannah brings her grief to God in honest prayer and answers a mistaken accusation with restraint. Her story invites us to pray sincerely and to listen carefully before judging another person's distress.|What do Hannah's prayer and her answer to Eli teach you about responding when sorrow is misunderstood?

Elkanah agreed that she should care for him until that time.~When Samuel was weaned, Hannah took him to Shiloh with offerings of animals, flour and wine. He was still young. This was the child for whom she had asked, and the Lord had granted her petition. She now gave him to the Lord for the whole of his life. The well fed seek work for bread, while the hungry cease to hunger. Her words about the barren woman and the mother of many belong to a larger picture of God overturning circumstances that people assume will last. Samuel remains the one child whose birth has been narrated here.~Hannah goes on to speak of the Lord bringing down and raising up, making poor and making rich.
