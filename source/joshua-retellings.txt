1:1-2:24|A New Leader and a Window in Jericho|Joshua receives his commission, and Rahab acts on what she has heard of the Lord|After Moses dies, the Lord speaks to Joshua son of Nun, who served Moses. He tells him to rise and cross the Jordan with the people into the land he is giving them. He describes its bounds and promises to be with Joshua as he was with Moses. Joshua must be strong because he will give the people their inheritance. He must also be strong and careful to obey the law Moses gave. It is to remain in his mouth, and he is to meditate on it day and night so that he does what is written. The Lord tells him to put away fear and discouragement because God will be with him wherever he goes. Before Joshua sends anyone across the river, he hears how he himself must listen and live.~Joshua orders the officers to have the people prepare provisions because within three days they will cross the Jordan. He reminds Reuben, Gad and half of Manasseh of their promise. Their wives, children and livestock may stay east of the river, but their capable fighting men must cross ahead of their brothers and help until the other tribes also receive rest and land. They answer that they will go where Joshua sends and obey as they obeyed Moses, asking that the Lord be with him. They call on him to be strong and courageous. The tribes already holding land begin Joshua's leadership by acknowledging a duty to people still waiting for theirs.~Joshua secretly sends two men from Shittim to look over the land and Jericho. They lodge with Rahab. The king of Jericho hears they have come and sends to demand that she bring them out. She hides the men under flax on her roof and directs the king's agents away from the house. The men pursuing them go towards the Jordan crossings and the city gate is shut. Rahab tells the spies that the inhabitants know how the Lord dried up the sea and what happened to Sihon and Og. Their courage has melted. She acknowledges that the Lord is God in heaven above and on earth beneath, and asks the men to spare her parents, brothers, sisters and their households because she has shown them kindness.~The spies agree under stated conditions. Rahab lowers them by a rope through her window because her house is on the city wall. She sends them into the hills to wait three days. They tell her to tie a scarlet cord in the window and gather her family inside, and say the oath will not bind them if she tells their business. She agrees and ties the cord. The spies stay in the hills until pursuit ends, cross back and report to Joshua that the Lord has given the land into their hands and its inhabitants fear them. The words that encourage Israel's leader come in part from a woman the king of Jericho expected would hand his men over.|Courage grows through attention to God's word and through acting faithfully on what we have heard. Rahab's concern for her household gives her confession a concrete cost and purpose.|What honest act of loyalty to God would put your professed trust into practice this week?

At harvest the river overflows its banks. When the priests' feet enter the water, the waters upstream stand in a heap near Adam, and the flow towards the Salt Sea is cut off. The priests stand on dry ground in the middle until all Israel crosses opposite Jericho.~The Lord tells Joshua to have one man from each tribe take a stone from where the priests stood and carry it to the night's camp. The stones will answer a future child's question. Israel will tell how the water was cut off before the ark. Joshua also sets twelve stones in the river at the priests' place. The people cross in haste, including about forty thousand armed men from the tribes with lands east of the river. When the priests finally come out at Joshua's command, the Jordan returns to its banks. The people come up on the tenth day of the first month and camp at Gilgal. Joshua sets up the stones there and says the nations are to know the Lord's mighty hand and Israel is to fear him always.~The kings west of the Jordan lose courage when they hear what has happened. The Lord tells Joshua to circumcise the Israelites born on the wilderness journey. The man identifies himself as commander of the Lord's army. Joshua falls on his face and asks what his lord says to his servant. Seven priests with trumpets go before the ark, with armed men ahead and a rear guard following. The people keep silent as directed. On the seventh day they go around seven times. At the trumpet blast Joshua tells them to shout and reminds them that the city is devoted to the Lord. Rahab and those with her are to live because she hid the messengers. Silver, gold, bronze and iron are reserved for the Lord's treasury. The wall falls, Israel takes the city and puts its inhabitants and animals to death. The spies bring out Rahab and her household. Joshua pronounces a curse on rebuilding Jericho, and Rahab lives among Israel.~Israel sends a small force against Ai and is driven back, losing about thirty-six men. Joshua and the elders fall before the ark. The Lord says Israel has broken the covenant by taking things set apart to destruction and hiding them among its goods. Joshua brings forward tribes, clans and households until Achan is identified. Achan confesses that he saw a fine garment from Shinar, silver and a bar of gold, coveted them and hid them beneath his tent. Messengers find them. Israel takes Achan, his household, livestock and goods to the Valley of Achor, where the judgment is carried out and a heap of stones raised. The object of one man's desire had been hidden in a tent, while its consequence was felt by people who had gone out to Ai.~The Lord tells Joshua to fear no longer and to take the fighting force to Ai. This time the spoil and livestock may be taken. Joshua sets an ambush. When the defenders pursue the retreating force, the men in ambush seize and burn the city. At the Lord's command Joshua stretches out his javelin, and the force that had fled turns back. Ai's inhabitants are killed, its king is executed and his body is taken down at sunset and covered with stones at the city gate. The two encounters with Ai are told on either side of Achan's confession and judgment. The permission to keep spoil at Ai also recalls the specific restriction that Israel broke at Jericho.~Joshua builds an altar of unhewn stones on Mount Ebal as Moses commanded, and Israel offers burnt offerings and peace offerings. Joshua writes a copy of the law on stones in the presence of the people. Israel stands on both sides of the ark, half facing Gerizim and half Ebal, with elders, officers, judges and the resident foreigner present. Joshua reads the blessings and curses and all that Moses commanded, in the hearing of women, children and foreigners as well as the men. Israel examines their provisions and does not ask counsel of the Lord. Joshua makes peace with them and the leaders swear an oath. Three days later Israel learns they are neighbours. The assembly murmurs, but the leaders refuse to kill people to whom they have sworn by the Lord. Joshua confronts them, and they say they acted because they feared what God commanded concerning the land. They are made cutters of wood and drawers of water for the assembly and altar. The oath stands and changes the work the Gibeonites will do among Israel.~The king of Jerusalem hears what Gibeon has done and enlists four other Amorite kings to attack it. The Gibeonites appeal to Joshua. The Lord tells him not to fear and Joshua goes up from Gilgal. The Lord throws the attackers into confusion and strikes many with hailstones during their flight. Joshua calls for the sun to stand over Gibeon and the moon over the valley of Aijalon. The book refers to the record in the Book of Jasher and says the Lord fought for Israel. The five kings hide in the cave at Makkedah, where they are shut in until the pursuit is over. Joshua has them brought out, and they are executed and placed back in the cave. The people Israel might have wished to avoid are the ones whose appeal brought it into this conflict.~Joshua goes from Makkedah through a series of southern cities, including Libnah, Lachish, Eglon, Hebron and Debir. The account records the taking of these places and the killing of their inhabitants and says that the Lord fought for Israel. Then Jabin of Hazor gathers northern kings, with many troops and horses at the waters of Merom. The Lord again tells Joshua not to fear. Israel defeats them and Joshua burns Hazor. The account distinguishes Hazor from other cities that were left standing and states that the Lord hardened the kings' hearts so they came to war. It recalls Joshua's removal of the Anakim from much of the hill country and says the land had rest from war.~The record then names the kingdoms already defeated east of the Jordan under Moses, including Sihon and Og. It gives their territory and recalls the gift of that land to Reuben, Gad and half of Manasseh. It lists the kings defeated west of the river under Joshua in the hill country, lowlands, plain and south. Jericho and Ai begin the list, followed by kings from the southern and northern campaigns and other places. Thirty-one kings are counted. The names close the account of conquest before the book turns to the lands still to be allotted. It remembers that Balaam was killed and that certain peoples remained among Israel. The Levites receive no tribal territory like the others. The Lord and the offerings made to him are named in connection with their inheritance.~Eleazar, Joshua and the tribal leaders oversee the western allotment by lot. At Gilgal Caleb recalls how Moses sent him to spy out the land at forty years of age. He brought back what was in his heart and followed the Lord wholly while others made Israel lose courage. Forty-five years later he is eighty-five and says he remains strong. He asks for the hill country with its fortified cities and Anakim because the Lord may be with him to drive them out. Joshua blesses him and gives him Hebron. The record names the former association of Hebron with Arba and says the land had rest. Caleb has waited most of a lifetime for ground that still calls for work when it is assigned to him.~Judah's boundaries lead around the south, east, north and west, with lists of cities by region. Receiving an allotment and taking possession of it are repeatedly shown as distinct stages in the life of the tribes.~The western half of Manasseh receives its portion.
