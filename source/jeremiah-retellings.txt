1:1-19|The Young Prophet and the Almond Branch|God's word equips the messenger he calls|Jeremiah belongs to a priestly family in Anathoth. The book places his ministry from Josiah's reign through the troubled years that end with Jerusalem's exile. Before recounting those events, it tells of his calling. The Lord declares that he knew and set him apart before birth and appointed him a prophet to the nations. Jeremiah answers with his inability to speak and his youth. The reply does not ask him to manufacture impressive confidence. He must go where God sends and speak what God commands. The promise beneath the task is God's presence, especially when the faces of powerful hearers make obedience frightening.~The Lord touches Jeremiah's mouth and places his words there. His commission involves uprooting and pulling down, destroying and overthrowing, building and planting. These actions describe what the announced word will accomplish within nations and kingdoms. They do not give the young prophet permission to seize political power or treat his own opinions as unquestionable divine commands. His authority depends upon the message entrusted to him. The order also leaves room for hope. Judgement will expose and remove what is corrupt, yet building and planting remain part of God's purpose beyond the destruction the book will describe.~Jeremiah sees an almond branch. The Lord connects the sight with his watching over his word to perform it, a wordplay expressed more clearly in Hebrew than in the older English translation. Another vision shows a boiling pot tilted from the north. Disaster will pour towards Judah, and northern rulers will establish themselves at Jerusalem's gates and against its towns. The explanation names the people's abandonment of God and their worship of what their own hands have made. The coming danger is therefore announced with moral meaning. A covenant people cannot use its inherited identity to make stubborn idolatry safe.~Jeremiah must prepare himself, stand and speak without surrendering to fear. He is compared to a fortified city, an iron pillar and bronze walls against kings, officials, priests and people. Their resistance is expected, not evidence that the commission has been withdrawn. They will fight him, but God's presence gives the final assurance of deliverance. The chapter does not promise a ministry free of grief, imprisonment or exhaustion. Those experiences will soon make the promise costly to trust. It gives Jeremiah a foundation before them, his calling begins with God's knowledge, his speech depends upon God's word and his endurance rests in the Lord who remains with him through the opposition he has already foreseen.|A calling to speak begins with dependence upon God's word and presence.
