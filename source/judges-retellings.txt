1:1-2:23|The Generation After Joshua|Israel begins to settle the land, and the Lord explains the trouble that will follow|After Joshua dies, Israel asks the Lord who should go up first against the Canaanites. Judah is named and asks Simeon to fight alongside him, promising the same help in return. They defeat the forces at Bezek. Adoni-bezek, captured and mutilated, remembers doing the same to seventy kings and acknowledges the judgment that has come upon him. Judah takes Jerusalem and fights in hill country, lowland and south. At Hebron and Debir the narrative recalls Caleb's family, Othniel's capture of Debir and Achsah's request for water with her land. Caleb gives her upper and lower springs. The Kenites settle among Judah's people, and Judah and Simeon take Zephath, calling it Hormah.~Judah takes Philistine cities, but the inhabitants of the plain have iron chariots. Benjamin does not expel the Jebusites from Jerusalem. The house of Joseph takes Bethel after a man shows them the entrance and is spared with his household. He builds another city named Luz elsewhere. What follows is a list of places where Israel did not displace the inhabitants. Manasseh and Ephraim leave Canaanites in several towns, and Zebulun, Asher and Naphtali do likewise. Some communities later exact forced labour. Dan is pressed back into the hills by the Amorites. The opening victories give way to a picture of neighbours whose worship and power will continue to shape Israel's life.~The angel of the Lord comes from Gilgal to Bochim and reminds Israel that God brought them from Egypt and promised never to break his covenant. They were to make no covenant with the inhabitants and to pull down their altars. They have failed to listen. The surviving peoples and their gods will become snares. Israel weeps and sacrifices there. The narrative then looks back to Joshua's dismissal of the people to their inheritances. Israel served the Lord in Joshua's days and in the days of elders who had seen his works. After that generation died, another arose that did not know the Lord or what he had done for Israel.~The people turn to the Baals and Ashtaroth and abandon the God who brought them from Egypt. They fall into the hands of plunderers and enemies. The Lord raises judges who deliver them, moved by their groaning under oppression. Yet they refuse to listen, and after each judge dies they return to ways more corrupt than before. The Lord declares that nations Joshua left will remain and test whether Israel will walk in his ways. The sound of weeping at Bochim was real. The rest of the book will show what happens when the people who wept leave the next generation without a settled knowledge of the Lord.|A generation can receive the benefit of God's works and still fail to remember or teach them. Faithfulness asks for decisions that outlast a moment of grief.|What part of God's faithfulness have you assumed the next generation already knows?
3:1-5:31|Deliverance From Moab to the Kishon|Othniel, Ehud, Shamgar, Deborah and Barak serve in years of oppression and rescue|The nations left in the land include Philistines, Canaanites, Sidonians and Hivites. Israel lives among them, intermarries and serves their gods. The Lord gives Israel into the hand of Cushan-rishathaim for eight years. When they cry out, he raises Othniel, and the Spirit of the Lord comes upon him. He judges Israel and defeats its oppressor. The land rests forty years. After Othniel dies Israel again does evil, and Eglon of Moab joins Ammon and Amalek and takes the city of palms. Israel serves Moab eighteen years. When the people cry again, the Lord raises Ehud, a left-handed Benjaminite who carries Israel's tribute to Eglon.~Ehud returns after delivering the tribute, obtains a private audience and kills Eglon. The king's attendants delay opening the doors, allowing Ehud to escape. He sounds a trumpet in the hills of Ephraim, and Israel takes the Jordan fords and defeats Moab. The land rests eighty years. Shamgar also delivers Israel, killing six hundred Philistines with an oxgoad. Then Israel again does evil and comes under Jabin, king of Canaan. His commander Sisera has nine hundred iron chariots and oppresses Israel for twenty years. The people cry to the Lord. Deborah, a prophetess, judges under a palm between Ramah and Bethel, where Israelites come to her for decisions.~Deborah calls Barak and gives him the Lord's command to gather men of Naphtali and Zebulun at Tabor. God will draw Sisera to the Kishon and give him into Barak's hand. Barak asks Deborah to go with him. She agrees and says the honour of the victory over Sisera will go to a woman. The Lord routs Sisera's force, and Sisera leaves his chariot and flees to Jael's tent. Jael receives him, and he asks for water and concealment. She gives him milk and later kills him as he sleeps. When Barak arrives, she shows him the man he was seeking. Israel presses until Jabin's rule ends.~Deborah and Barak sing of the Lord's coming and the people who offered themselves willingly. They recall empty highways and villages left exposed, and name tribes that came and others that stayed behind. The stars fight in the song and the Kishon sweeps the enemy away. Meroz is cursed for failing to help, and Jael is blessed. The song imagines Sisera's mother waiting at a window while her attendants suppose the army is dividing spoil. It ends by asking that God's enemies perish and those who love him rise like the sun. The land rests forty years.|The Lord hears people in distress and can give courage and responsibility to people others overlook. Deborah's song also remembers who shared the burden and who withheld help.|Where could you offer help willingly while others are still deciding whether the burden concerns them?
6:1-8:35|Gideon and the Victory Israel Could Not Claim|The Lord calls a fearful man, reduces his army and exposes the danger that follows success|Midian, Amalek and people from the east ruin Israel's crops and leave the people hiding in caves. When Israel cries out, a prophet reminds them that the Lord rescued them from Egypt and told them to fear no Amorite gods. At Ophrah the angel of the Lord finds Gideon threshing wheat in a winepress to hide it. Gideon asks why Israel suffers if the Lord is with them and how someone from a weak family can save Israel. The Lord promises to be with him. Fire consumes the food Gideon brings, and the angel disappears. Gideon fears he will die, but the Lord speaks peace and Gideon builds an altar.~God tells him to tear down his father's altar to Baal and cut down the sacred pole beside it. Gideon acts at night because he fears his household and town. When people demand his death, his father Joash asks whether Baal cannot plead for himself, and Gideon is called Jerubbaal. When the invaders gather, the Spirit of the Lord comes upon Gideon and he summons Israelites. He asks for the signs of the fleece, first wet with dew while the ground is dry, then dry while the ground is wet. God grants both. At the spring of Harod the Lord reduces Gideon's company from thousands to three hundred, lest Israel claim it saved itself.~Gideon goes near the enemy camp with his servant and hears a man tell a dream about a loaf overturning a tent. His companion understands it as the defeat of Midian by Gideon. Gideon worships and returns encouraged. His company surrounds the camp with trumpets and concealed lamps. At their signal the enemy panics and turns on itself. Israel pursues, and Ephraim's men capture Oreb and Zeeb. When they complain they were called late, Gideon answers in a way that quiets them. He crosses the Jordan pursuing Zebah and Zalmunna, and Succoth and Penuel refuse food to his exhausted men.~Gideon captures the kings and on his return punishes Succoth and Penuel. He kills Zebah and Zalmunna after learning they killed his brothers. Israel asks him and his descendants to rule. He says the Lord will rule, yet asks for gold earrings from the spoil and makes an ephod at Ophrah. Israel goes after it and it becomes a snare to his family. The land rests forty years, and Gideon has many sons, including Abimelech, whose mother lives at Shechem. After Gideon dies, Israel returns to the Baals and forgets both the Lord's deliverance and what Gideon's household did for them.|God can give strength to people who are afraid. Success calls for the same humility as the hour when we first asked for help.|What good result are you tempted to use to secure a standing or influence God never asked you to seek?
9:1-12:15|Abimelech's Crown and Jephthah's Vow|Israel sees the cost of power sought through bloodshed and of words spoken without care|Abimelech gains support from his mother's relatives at Shechem and hires men with silver from Baal-berith's temple. He kills his brothers at Ophrah, and only Jotham escapes. Shechem makes Abimelech king. From Mount Gerizim Jotham tells how the olive, fig and vine refuse to reign over the trees, but the bramble accepts and threatens fire. He asks whether Shechem dealt rightly with Gideon's house. After three years distrust grows between Abimelech and Shechem. Gaal challenges him and Zebul warns him. Abimelech defeats Gaal, destroys Shechem and burns the people who take refuge in its stronghold. At Thebez a woman drops a millstone on him from a tower. He dies, and the narrator says God brought his evil and Shechem's back on them.~Tola and then Jair judge Israel. Jair's thirty sons have thirty towns in Gilead. After Jair's death Israel again serves the gods of surrounding peoples and falls under Philistine and Ammonite pressure. When the people cry out, the Lord recalls earlier deliverances and tells them to cry to the gods they chose. They confess, put away foreign gods and serve the Lord, and he is grieved by Israel's misery. As Ammon assembles, Gilead looks for someone to lead it. Jephthah, a capable warrior driven out by his half-brothers, has been living in Tob. The elders ask him to return. He recalls their rejection and obtains their pledge that he will lead them if the Lord gives victory.~Jephthah asks Ammon's king why he has come to fight. The king claims Israel seized his land on the way from Egypt. Jephthah answers by recalling Israel's requests for passage, Sihon's attack and the Lord's gift of Amorite territory. He asks why the land was unclaimed for three hundred years and appeals to the Lord as judge. Ammon refuses to listen. The Spirit of the Lord comes upon Jephthah. Before battle he vows to offer whatever first comes out of his house to meet him if he returns in peace. His daughter, his only child, comes out with music and dancing. He tears his clothes, and she asks for time to mourn her virginity with her companions.~She returns after two months and Jephthah carries out his vow. Israel's daughters remember her yearly. Ephraim then threatens Jephthah for going to war without them. The Gileadites defeat them and use the pronunciation of Shibboleth at the Jordan crossings to identify fugitives. Forty-two thousand Ephraimites fall. Jephthah judges six years. Ibzan, Elon and Abdon follow, and the account records years of service and the households of two of them. The reading began with one man's bid for rule costing him his brothers, and ends with tribal bloodshed and the memory of a daughter who bore the cost of her father's vow.|A position won by kinship or force can consume the people who sustain it. Words spoken before God require care, especially when others will bear their consequences.|Whose interests do you need to consider before making a pledge that would also bind them?
13:1-16:31|Samson and the Calling He Treated Lightly|A child set apart before birth begins Israel's deliverance and finally prays in his weakness|Israel falls under the Philistines for forty years. The angel of the Lord tells Manoah's barren wife she will bear a son. She is to avoid wine and unclean food, and no razor shall touch the boy's head because he will be a Nazirite and begin to deliver Israel. Manoah prays for further instruction, and the angel repeats it to his wife. When Manoah offers a sacrifice, the angel ascends in the flame. Manoah fears death, but his wife reasons that God would not have accepted their offering or made such a promise if he intended to kill them. Samson is born and the Lord blesses him. The Spirit begins to stir him in the territory of Dan.~Samson wants a Philistine woman at Timnath. His parents object, unaware that the Lord is seeking occasion against the Philistines. On the road Samson kills a lion. Later he finds honey in its carcass, eats and shares it without explaining its source. At the wedding feast he proposes a riddle about it. His companions threaten his wife until she obtains the answer. Samson pays the forfeit after killing men at Ashkelon, and his wife is given to his companion. When he returns and learns of this, he burns Philistine crops. The Philistines burn the woman and her father. Samson strikes them and withdraws to Etam. Men of Judah bind him to hand him over, but the Spirit comes upon him and he defeats a thousand men with a donkey's jawbone.~Thirsty afterwards, he calls on God, who supplies water. He judges Israel twenty years in the days of the Philistines. At Gaza he escapes an ambush, carrying away the city gates. Later he loves Delilah, whom the Philistine rulers pay to discover the source of his strength. He misleads her three times before yielding to her pressure and telling her that his uncut hair marks the consecration established from his birth. She has his hair shaved while he sleeps. He wakes expecting to free himself and does not know the Lord has departed from him. The Philistines blind him and put him to work in prison. His hair begins to grow again.~The Philistine rulers gather to sacrifice to Dagon, saying their god has delivered Samson into their hand. They bring Samson out to entertain them. He asks the boy guiding him to let him feel the pillars supporting the house. With the rulers and crowd present, he asks the Lord to remember and strengthen him once more. He presses against the pillars and the house falls. Those he kills at his death are more than those killed during his life. His family takes him away and buries him between Zorah and Eshtaol in his father's burial place. The child whose birth God announced has begun the deliverance foretold, though his years have carried grave costs for himself and others.|Gifts and a calling do not remove the need to guard one's desires and honour God. Samson's final prayer shows his need of the strength he had once expected to find at will.|What gift or responsibility are you treating as permanently yours even while neglecting the devotion it requires?
17:1-21:25|What Seemed Right in Their Own Eyes|Private worship and tribal violence expose the disorder that closes Judges|Micah of Ephraim returns silver he took from his mother. She dedicates silver to the Lord and has an image made. Micah sets up a shrine, makes an ephod and household gods and appoints a son, then a wandering Levite, as priest. He expects blessing because he now has a Levite. Danite spies seeking a place to settle ask the priest about their journey. They see Laish living securely and urge their people to take it. Armed Danites return, take Micah's sacred objects and persuade his priest to go with them. Micah protests and has to turn back when threatened. The Danites destroy Laish, rebuild it as Dan and establish the image and priesthood there.~A Levite living in Ephraim goes to Bethlehem to bring back his concubine. After delays at her father's house they lodge in Gibeah of Benjamin with an old man from Ephraim. Men surround the house demanding the Levite. His concubine is put outside and assaulted through the night. At morning she lies at the doorway. The Levite takes her body home and cuts it into twelve pieces to send through Israel. The tribes gather at Mizpah. He tells them what happened, and they demand that Benjamin surrender the men of Gibeah. Benjamin refuses and gathers for battle. Outrage at one town's crime becomes a war between brothers.~Israel suffers heavy losses in two battles. The people weep, fast and sacrifice before the Lord. Phinehas ministers before the ark. On the third day Israel defeats Benjamin after drawing its fighters out of Gibeah, and six hundred men escape to the rock of Rimmon. Israel turns on Benjamin's towns. The congregation then mourns the prospect that one tribe could disappear, while remembering its oath not to give daughters to Benjamin. They discover that Jabesh-gilead sent no one to Mizpah. They attack that town, spare four hundred young women and give them to the survivors. Two hundred men are still without wives.~The elders tell the remaining men to seize wives from the daughters of Shiloh while they dance at the Lord's feast. They plan how to answer the women's relatives without admitting that Israel gave its daughters away. Benjamin's survivors return, rebuild and live in their towns, and the tribes disperse. The book closes by saying that there was no king in Israel and everyone did what was right in their own eyes. The congregation has found a way around its vow, and the women who are taken bear the price. The last scenes ask the reader to remember the original victim while watching Israel make new victims in the name of preserving a tribe.|Outrage over a genuine wrong does not make every proposed remedy just. Listen to the people whose lives will be changed before deciding what counts as a solution.|When have you been so intent on defending a principle that you stopped noticing who was paying the price?
