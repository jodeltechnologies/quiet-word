1:1-11|A Prisoner Gives Thanks for His Partners|Praying for love with discernment|Paul and Timothy greet the church at Philippi, and Paul gives thanks for its partnership in the gospel from the first day. He trusts that God will complete the work he began. He prays that their love will grow with knowledge and discernment, so that they can recognise what matters and bear righteous fruit through Christ.|Paul prays for a love that learns how to make good judgements. His gratitude for past fellowship gives substance to his prayer for their growth.|What does Paul ask to grow alongside love?
1:12-30|Chains Open a Door for the Gospel|Courage and purpose under restrictions|Paul reports that his imprisonment has helped make Christ known and encouraged others to speak. Some preach from goodwill and some from rivalry. Paul rejoices that Christ is proclaimed. He considers whether he will live or die and sees continuing life as an opportunity for fruitful service. He asks the church to stand together in the face of opposition.|Paul explains what remains possible in a situation he cannot control. His concern for the church gives him a reason to welcome further work if it is given to him.|What effects of his imprisonment does Paul want the church to understand?
2:1-11|Christ Takes the Place of a Servant|Humility that gives others attention|Paul asks the church to share one purpose and look to the interests of others. He points to Christ, who took a servant's form and humbled himself to death on a cross. God exalted him, and every knee will bow and every tongue confess that Jesus Christ is Lord.|Paul places Christ's conduct inside a request about relationships. The dignity of the one who stoops to serve makes rivalry over rank harder to defend.|What connection does Paul make between Christ's humility and how believers treat each other?
2:12-30|Two Fellow Workers Worth Receiving|Obedience and honour in ordinary service|Paul calls the church to work out its salvation with reverence because God works in it. He asks the believers to act without grumbling and to hold to the word of life. He then explains his plans for Timothy, who genuinely cares for them, and Epaphroditus, who came close to death in service. He asks them to honour such people.|The two workers make the preceding appeal practical. Care for others and costly service deserve recognition even when they receive little public attention.|What qualities make Paul commend Timothy and Epaphroditus?
3:1-21|What Paul Once Counted as Gain|Knowing Christ and pressing forward|Paul lists reasons he once had for confidence in his religious standing and performance. He counts those gains as loss beside knowing Christ and receiving righteousness through faith. He longs to know Christ's resurrection and share his sufferings. Describing himself as still pressing forward, he calls the church to live as people whose citizenship is in heaven.|Paul's past achievements could have supplied a continuing object of pride. He gives them a new value in light of the one he is seeking to know.|What does Paul now want to gain or know after setting aside his former grounds for confidence?
4:1-9|A Plea for Two Fellow Workers|Reconciliation, prayer and disciplined thought|Paul urges Euodias and Syntyche to agree in the Lord and asks another person to help them. He remembers that both worked beside him. He calls the church to rejoice, make requests known to God with thanks, think about what is true and good, and practise what they have learned.|Paul remembers both women's service while addressing a current disagreement. Peace can involve the help of others, the discipline of prayer and attention to what is worth dwelling on.|How does Paul ask the church to help the two women while remembering their previous service?
4:10-23|The Gift That Reached Paul Again|Contentment and generosity|Paul rejoices that the Philippians have renewed their practical care for him. He has learned to live with abundance and with need through Christ's strength. He remembers their repeated support, welcomes the gift brought by Epaphroditus and describes it as pleasing to God. He trusts God to supply what they need.|Paul's contentment allows him to receive the gift gratefully without making his well-being depend on it. The church's support is a practical expression of fellowship.|Which changing circumstances does Paul say he has learned to face?
