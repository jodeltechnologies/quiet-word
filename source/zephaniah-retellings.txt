1:1-2:15|The Day Judah Had Put Out of Mind|Zephaniah calls the people to seek the Lord while there is still time|The word of the Lord came to Zephaniah during the reign of Josiah. The prophet gives his family line back to Hezekiah before he announces a sweeping judgment. People and animals, birds and fish are all named in the opening warning. Then the Lord turns his attention to Judah and Jerusalem. He will cut off what remains of Baal worship and deal with people who bow to the stars on their rooftops. Some swear allegiance to the Lord while also swearing by another god. Others have turned away altogether and have ceased to seek him. The warning reaches people whose worship is visible in Jerusalem and people whose neglect has become so ordinary that there is little to see.~Zephaniah calls for silence because the day of the Lord is near. The Lord has prepared a sacrifice and consecrated his guests. Officials and the king's sons will answer for their conduct, as will people in foreign dress and those who fill their masters' houses with violence and fraud. Cries will rise from the Fish Gate, the newer part of the city and its hills. Merchants will lose their place, and the Lord will search Jerusalem with lamps for people settled in complacency. They say in their hearts that the Lord will do neither good nor harm. The houses they build and the vineyards they plant will pass out of their hands. Their silver and gold will be of no use when the day they dismissed arrives.~Before the decree takes effect, Zephaniah calls the nation to gather. He speaks particularly to the humble who have followed the Lord's judgments. They are to seek the Lord, seek righteousness and seek humility. Perhaps they will find shelter in the day of his anger. The prophet then looks west towards the Philistine cities. Gaza will be deserted and Ashdod driven out at noon. The coast where cities stood will become pasture. The remnant of Judah will lie down in the houses of Ashkelon when the Lord attends to his people and restores their fortunes. The land that seemed permanently divided among local powers lies open before him.~Moab and Ammon have insulted the Lord's people and boasted against their territory. The prophet compares their coming desolation to Sodom and Gomorrah and says the nations will bow to the Lord from their own lands. Cush also comes under the sword, and to the north the Lord will stretch out his hand against Assyria. Nineveh, a city that lived at ease and imagined it had no equal, will become a dry ruin where animals lie down and cries are heard at the windows. The prophecy returns us to the attitude of those complacent people in Jerusalem. A city could imagine itself secure while ignoring the God who governed its future. The summons to seek him was given while it could still be heard and answered.|The Lord sees divided worship, dishonest gain and the quiet conviction that he will never intervene. Seek him in humility while the opportunity to respond is open.|What has become ordinary in your life that you need to bring back under God's direction?
3:1-20|The City That Heard God Sing|Jerusalem's rebellion gives way to a promise of purification and homecoming|Zephaniah turns to a rebellious, polluted city. Jerusalem has refused to listen, receive correction, trust the Lord or draw near to him. Its officials are roaring lions and its judges are evening wolves that leave nothing for the morning. Its prophets are reckless and treacherous, and its priests profane holy things and do violence to the law. Yet the Lord in the midst of the city remains righteous. Morning by morning he brings his justice to light. The people have an example before them every day in the God whose ways they have refused. Their leaders have used the places entrusted to them in ways that injure the very people they ought to serve.~The Lord has cut off nations and left streets empty. He expected Jerusalem to see what happened, fear him and accept correction. Instead its people rose early to corrupt their deeds. He tells his hearers to wait for him until the day he rises to gather nations and pour out his indignation. Then the language changes. He will give the peoples purified speech so that they may call upon his name and serve him together. From beyond the rivers of Cush his worshippers, including his scattered people, will bring him an offering. The reach of divine judgment in the preceding chapters is matched by the reach of worship here. People from distant lands will call on one Lord.~On that day Jerusalem need no longer live under the shame of her rebellion. The Lord will remove the proud who exult in her midst and leave a humble, lowly people who take refuge in his name. The remnant will do no wrong, speak no lies and carry no deceit on its tongue. They will feed and lie down with nobody to frighten them. Their security has a moral shape. The speech that once carried deceit will be made clean and the pride that once made the holy mountain a place for boasting will give way to reliance upon the Lord. Zephaniah calls the daughter of Zion to sing because the Lord has taken away her judgments and turned back her enemy.~The King of Israel is in her midst. Jerusalem is told to let her hands grow weak no longer, for the mighty God who saves is with her. He rejoices over her with gladness, quiets her with his love and rejoices over her with singing. He will gather those who mourn for the appointed feasts, deal with their oppressors, save the lame and bring home the outcast. Those whose shame was known will receive praise where they once suffered. The poem closes with the Lord promising to gather his people and restore their fortunes before their own eyes. The city that opened the chapter by keeping its distance from God ends by hearing the promise of his presence and his joy over the people he restores.|God's restoration reaches our speech, our dealings with others and the place we give the vulnerable. His presence gives the humbled people of Zion courage to lift their hands again.|Whose loneliness or shame might you ease by making room for them in the life of your fellowship?
